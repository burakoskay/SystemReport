Public disagreement over political violence now hinges on how we count it, not just who says it’s happening. The same definitional tug‑of‑war shows up in health research, media consolidation, and AI governance.

Russell Vought is reportedly slated to receive veto power over every NIH grant[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5], the NIH director has publicly objected[^1][^3][^4][^5], California just settled a lawsuit that challenged the Paramount‑Warner merger[^6][^7][^8], and President Donald Trump unveiled an "AI Force" after dismissing calls to slow AI development. Each move rewrites the rulebook in its arena.

## Measuring Political Violence

A new analysis in WIRED argues that the public’s split on whether political violence is rising stems from competing definitions of what counts as violence. Researchers point to divergent thresholds for protest‑related injuries, property damage, and lethal incidents.

When scholars standardize metrics, the headline numbers shift dramatically. The piece warns that policymakers who ignore definitional nuance risk crafting legislation that either over‑penalizes peaceful dissent or under‑reacts to genuine threats.

## NIH Grant Veto Power

Russell Vought, the former White House chief of staff, will reportedly be given veto authority over all NIH grant applications[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]. The pending move faces direct opposition from the NIH director, who argues the power threatens scientific independence[^1][^3][^4][^5].

If Vought’s office can block any proposal, researchers may see a surge in politically safe projects and a retreat from controversial topics. Funding pipelines that once flowed based on peer review could instead be filtered through a political lens.

Historically, federal research agencies have resisted overt political control. The last comparable episode involved a mid‑2000s attempt to tie grant eligibility to ideological criteria, which was ultimately rolled back after congressional pushback. Vought’s potential veto revives that debate and could reshape the biomedical research landscape for years.

## California Settlement on Paramount‑Warner Merger

California’s attorney general office settled a lawsuit challenging the Paramount‑Warner merger, a decision that angered consumer advocates[^6][^7][^8]. State AGs had built what Lina Khan described as a "very strong" case that the merger violated antitrust law.

The settlement stops further litigation but leaves the merged entity intact, effectively conceding that the market can absorb the combined media powerhouse[^6][^7][^8]. Critics argue the outcome weakens the enforcement momentum generated by recent high‑profile antitrust actions.

The case also highlights a growing clash between state‑level enforcement and federal oversight. While the FTC under Khan has pursued aggressive actions against tech conglomerates, state attorneys general are now forced to negotiate settlements that may dilute those efforts.

## Trump's AI Force Initiative

President Donald Trump rejected recent industry pleas to pause advanced AI development and instead announced the creation of an "AI Force." The administration offered few details on the unit’s mandate, staffing, or budget.

The vague rollout stands in stark contrast to bipartisan calls for a coordinated slowdown to address safety and bias concerns. By framing the effort as a force rather than a regulatory body, the White House signals a more militarized approach to AI governance.

Analysts note that without a clear charter, the AI Force could become a political tool rather than a technical watchdog. Its existence may also spur other nations to adopt similar structures, accelerating a global race for AI dominance.

## What to Watch

Track whether the NIH director’s objections translate into legislative hearings or a formal rescindment of Vought’s authority. Monitor the FTC’s next move on media consolidation, especially any follow‑up to the Paramount‑Warner settlement. Finally, watch for the AI Force’s first public briefing, which will reveal whether it will shape policy, fund research, or simply serve as a political statement.
