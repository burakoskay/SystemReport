Google is testing AI‑guided flight reroutes to eliminate contrail formation under Operation Blue Skies. If the trial reduces cirrus clouds, airlines could shave fuel costs and climate regulators could gain a new mitigation tool.

Operation Blue Skies, a joint effort with the UK government, will subtly reroute commercial flights to test contrail avoidance.[^1][^2][^5][^7][^8] The plan does not overhaul flight paths; it nudges them just enough to keep exhaust plumes from freezing into high‑altitude ice crystals.[^5] The experiment will run on a limited corridor, allowing researchers to compare satellite‑derived cloud data before and after the AI adjustments.[^5]

The AI model behind the reroutes ingests real‑time weather, aircraft performance, and traffic data.[^4] It then proposes micro‑adjustments that keep planes on schedule while staying clear of moisture‑rich layers.[^4] By avoiding those layers, the model hopes to prevent the formation of persistent contrails that act like thin cirrus clouds, trapping heat and contributing to warming.[^1][^2][^4][^5][^6][^7][^8] Critics note that any deviation adds fuel burn, but the trial will measure net climate impact versus additional emissions.[^4]

Nvidia finally let Firefox host its GeForce Now streaming service without a separate client. The company said it set a “high bar” for any browser integrating GeForce Now without added programs, and Firefox has seemingly met those standards as in‑browser support goes live.

The move matters because most cloud‑gaming users have been locked into Chrome or a dedicated app. Firefox’s lower memory footprint and strong privacy track record could broaden the service’s appeal to developers who fear bloat. Nvidia’s statement implies the browser met performance and security thresholds that were previously only achievable on Chrome.

Early adopters report latency comparable to the Chrome experience, but they also note occasional hiccups when switching tabs mid‑session. Nvidia has not disclosed whether the Firefox integration will support RTX‑accelerated features, leaving power users to test those limits on their own rigs.

Google rolled out Android 17’s new 3D emoji design on the Pixel 11 series this week. The emojis replace flat icons with animated, depth‑aware models that react to device orientation and lighting.

For everyday chat, the change feels like a subtle visual upgrade rather than a gimmick. Users can see a smiley tilt when they rotate the phone, and the shading adjusts as the screen’s brightness shifts. The rollout does not affect older Pixel devices, which continue to show the legacy 2D set.

Developers can already access the new assets through the Android SDK, meaning third‑party messaging apps could adopt the 3D look without waiting for a system update. Google’s engineering blog frames the move as a step toward more immersive UI elements, but the immediate impact is limited to the Pixel 11’s launch cohort.

Best Buy opened pre‑order deals for the Pixel 11 Pro, Pro XL, and Pro Fold, with shipments starting tomorrow. Amazon cut the price of the new Olive Google Pixel Bud s Pro 2, and a $1,350 discount targets 2026 Copilot+ PCs and gaming laptops. LG’s most affordable 2026 OLED TV dropped $300, while Best Buy’s Anniversary Chromebook sale began at $179.

The bundle of offers reflects a broader push to clear inventory ahead of the next hardware refresh cycle. Retailers are bundling older‑generation devices with accessories to keep shelves moving, a strategy that often signals an upcoming price drop on newer models. Consumers should watch for price adjustments on the Pixel 11 line as the holiday season approaches.

A YouTuber demonstrated how to flash Android onto a reMarkable Paper Pro, turning the e‑paper tablet into a conventional Android device. The tutorial shows the hardware hack step‑by‑step, but the result defeats the Paper Pro’s low‑power, distraction‑free purpose. The modified device runs standard Android apps, yet the e‑ink screen struggles with video playback and rapid UI changes.

The hack underscores a niche community that repurposes single‑purpose hardware for broader use cases. While the conversion is technically impressive, it highlights the trade‑off between flexibility and the original design intent. Most reMarkable users will likely stick with the native note‑taking experience rather than adopt a slower, less readable Android environment.

What to watch: The next report from Operation Blue Skies is due in six months and will reveal whether AI‑driven reroutes achieved measurable contrail reduction without inflating fuel use.[^6] Nvidia plans to expand GeForce Now support to additional browsers, so keep an eye on Edge and Safari updates. Finally, monitor Pixel 11 pricing as retailers adjust deals ahead of the 2026 device rollout, and watch for community feedback on the reMarkable Android hack for clues about the viability of e‑ink as a general‑purpose platform.
