## DOJ Moves Against Former Trump Aide
The Justice Department is weighing perjury and obstruction-of-justice charges against Cassidy Hutchinson, the former White House aide who testified before the Jan 6 committee. The move signals that the investigation into the Capitol attack may still generate criminal fallout.

Hutchinson, who sat on the committee’s record‑room panel, provided direct accounts of internal White House discussions. According to the DOJ brief, prosecutors are assessing whether her testimony contains false statements that could obstruct the committee’s fact‑finding mission. The potential case arrives as the department continues to pursue accountability for the Jan 6 breach, a strategy that has already produced multiple indictments.

Legal scholars note that perjury prosecutions in politically charged inquiries are rare but not unprecedented. The Department of Justice has previously filed perjury charges in high‑profile investigations, using them to reinforce the credibility of congressional records. If a grand jury decides to indict Hutchinson, the case could test the limits of executive‑branch testimony protection.

## Federal Funding and Media Battles
The CDC has allocated $1.6 million to a study on alleged harms of the hepatitis B vaccine, a decision that has ignited ethical concerns among public‑health watchdogs. Critics argue the funding backs a line of inquiry that lacks scientific consensus, labeling the move “unethical.”

At the same time, a federal judge is scrutinizing whether former President Donald Trump’s decision to delay early access to Truth Social posts by a single second harms the press. The judge’s questioning reflects broader tensions over the president’s control of his own platform and the potential impact on news gathering. Legal analysts suggest that even a one‑second delay could set a precedent for how political figures manage real‑time communications.

Both episodes illustrate how federal agencies are navigating the gray area between public interest and political fallout. The CDC’s funding choice and the judge’s inquiry into Truth Social access each raise questions about the balance of governmental authority and private enterprise in shaping public discourse.

## Defense Expansion Meets Community Risk
Castelion, a next‑generation defense‑tech firm, is erecting a facility in Rio Rancho Estates, New Mexico. Documents obtained by local reporters show the plant could create a major fire and chemical hazard for the surrounding neighborhood.

Residents who moved to the area seeking a stable, family‑friendly environment now face the prospect of living next to a hypersonic missile factory. The project’s proximity to homes has sparked protests and demands for stricter safety assessments. While the federal government has approved the construction, community leaders argue that the risk assessment process failed to account for worst‑case scenarios.

The situation underscores a recurring pattern where defense‑industry growth collides with civilian safety concerns. Past installations have prompted similar backlash, prompting lawmakers to revisit zoning and environmental‑impact standards for high‑risk facilities.

## What to Watch
Watch for an indictment filing on Cassidy Hutchinson as the DOJ finalizes its perjury assessment. Monitor the court’s ruling on Trump’s Truth Social delay, which could reshape how political figures handle real‑time information.
