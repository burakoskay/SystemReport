## Microsoft's Linux Gambit

Microsoft has released Azure Linux 4.0, its first general-purpose Linux distribution, marking a significant shift in the company's approach to open-source software. This move is a far cry from the company's past stance on Linux, which was once seen as a threat to its Windows dominance. Azure Linux 4.0's design is centered around a modular architecture, allowing users to customize their installations according to their specific needs.

## A History of Microsoft and Linux

In the past, Microsoft's relationship with Linux was complicated, to say the least. The company was a vocal critic of open-source software, and its patent portfolio was often used to assert control over Linux-based projects. However, with the appointment of Satya Nadella as CEO in 2014, Microsoft began to change its tune. Under Nadella's leadership, the company has become increasingly open to open-source software, and has even joined the Linux Foundation as a Platinum member. Wim Coekaerts, corporate VP of Microsoft Enterprise Open Source Group, has been a key figure in this shift, stating that "Azure, Microsoft's cloud program, has to be open-source and Linux friendly."

## The Azure Connection

Azure Linux 4.0 is a key part of Microsoft's cloud strategy, which is built around its Azure platform. The company has been investing heavily in Azure, and the release of Azure Linux 4.0 is a major milestone in its efforts to make the platform more attractive to customers who use Linux. 

## Patent Revenue and Linux

One of the most interesting aspects of Microsoft's relationship with Linux is its patent portfolio. The company is believed to make significant revenue from patent licensing agreements with companies that use Linux-based products, such as Android device manufacturers. According to a report in CRN, Microsoft makes between $5 and $15 in royalties on each Android device sold, generating a $2 billion annual revenue stream. This has led some to accuse Microsoft of profiting from open-source software without contributing to its development.

## Technical Mechanics

The modular design of Azure Linux 4.0 enables Microsoft to more easily update and patch the operating system, ensuring that users have access to the latest security fixes and features. This approach is a departure from traditional Linux distributions, which often come with a one-size-fits-all approach.

## Downstream Implications

As Microsoft continues to deepen its commitment to open-source software, it's likely that we'll see more Linux-based products and services from the company. One thing to watch is how Microsoft's patent portfolio will be used in the future. Will the company continue to assert its patents against Linux-based projects, or will it take a more collaborative approach? Only time will tell.

## History of Microsoft's Patent Assertions

Microsoft's patent assertions against Linux-based projects have been a point of contention for years. In 2007, the company launched a patent lawsuit against Linux-based companies, including TomTom and Linux distributors. This led to a significant backlash from the open-source community, with many accusing Microsoft of attempting to stifle innovation. Since then, the company has taken a more conciliatory approach, partnering with Linux distributors and agreeing to cross-license patents.
