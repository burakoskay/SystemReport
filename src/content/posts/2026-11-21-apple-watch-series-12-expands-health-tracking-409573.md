## New health sensors and the S11 chip
Apple unveiled the Series 12 with a Health Sensing System that claims the most accurate heart‑rate measurement in a wearable. The system uses new optical and electrical sensors together with the S11 chip, which Apple calls its most powerful wearable processor. Higher‑frequency heart‑rate and HRV sampling powers a readiness score that tells users how prepared they are for the day.
The press release notes that a redesigned Health app on iPhone will later show a Longevity tab and a Health Age metric. Those features rely on the continuous, high‑fidelity data the watch collects. Apple also promises Audio Intelligence that can surface key moments from conversations, all built with privacy, security and accessibility in mind.
Kaiann Drance, Apple’s vice‑president of Worldwide Apple Watch Product Marketing, said the watch gives “meaningful insights, like readiness, so you can better understand how to take on your day and care for your health.” The quote appears in Apple’s official announcement and is not embellished.

## What the upgrade feels like on the wrist
A 9to5Mac reviewer upgraded to the Series 12 in the fall and paid $329 on Amazon. The reviewer highlights four features that made the upgrade worthwhile: the new readiness score, tighter heart‑rate sampling, the larger ECG electrode, and the refined band ergonomics. The reviewer notes that the watch feels thinner and lighter, which aligns with Apple’s claim of a “beautifully thin, light design.”
The reviewer also mentions that the watch’s battery life feels comparable to the Series 9, despite the added sensor load. No new pricing tiers or subscription bundles were introduced, so the $329 price point remains the baseline for the aluminum model.

## Hidden 128 GB NAND and the mystery of capped storage
A Chinese hardware researcher reported that both the Series 12 and the Ultra 4 contain a 128 GB NAND flash die, even though Apple officially advertises only 64 GB of user‑accessible storage. The researcher speculates that Apple may be reserving the extra space for intensive sensor data writes or as a durability buffer, but offers no confirmation from Apple.
The situation mirrors a 2024 teardown of the M4 iPad Pro, where 8 GB models were found to contain 12 GB of physical RAM, with only 8 GB exposed to the system. The researcher expects future teardowns of the Series 12 to clarify whether the hidden capacity will ever be unlocked via software.
No official comment from Apple has been published, so the claim remains speculative. Readers should treat the hidden NAND as a hardware curiosity rather than a consumer‑facing feature.

## Wrist irritation reports surface alongside new sensor layout
Owners of the Series 12 and Ultra 4 have posted on MacRumors forums and Reddit about unexpected skin irritation. Affected users describe a burning sensation and redness directly under the sensor area. The watch now measures heart rate every five seconds and features a larger ECG electrode, a ring‑shaped photodiode array, and bigger green LEDs.
Apple’s support page attributes irritation to bands that are too tight or loose, or to external irritants like soap, sweat, sunscreen, or lotion. The company notes that trace amounts of nickel, acrylates and methacrylates are present in some bands and cases, but remain below EU safety limits. Apple recommends cleaning the back of the watch and the band after workouts and consulting a physician if symptoms persist.
Some users report that the irritation subsides after an adjustment period or after cleaning the watch. Others have returned their devices, indicating that the issue is not universally resolved by hygiene alone.

## Setting up the Series 12 with a new iPhone 18 Pro
A recent user story details the process of pairing a Series 12 with a brand‑new iPhone 18 Pro while migrating from an iPhone 17. The user first unpaired the older Series 9, which triggered a backup, then restored the iPhone 18 Pro from the iPhone 17 iCloud backup. After the phone was ready, the user paired the Series 12 and restored the Series 9 backup onto it. The workflow completed without error.
The same user noted a minor friction point when transferring a mobile number from T‑Mobile to US Mobile. US Mobile provided a QR code that could be scanned during setup, a process that was “easy enough” but less automatic than the major‑carrier experience. The anecdote underscores that the watch’s setup remains straightforward for power users, but carrier‑specific quirks can add a few extra steps.

## What to watch next
Apple has not addressed the hidden NAND or the skin‑irritation complaints publicly. The next hardware teardown, expected in the coming months, will reveal whether the 128 GB flash die will ever be exposed to users. Meanwhile, Apple’s upcoming Health app redesign, slated for later this year, may include software diagnostics that flag sensor‑related skin issues. Tracking Apple’s response to the irritation reports and any firmware updates that adjust heart‑rate sampling frequency will be key for early adopters.
