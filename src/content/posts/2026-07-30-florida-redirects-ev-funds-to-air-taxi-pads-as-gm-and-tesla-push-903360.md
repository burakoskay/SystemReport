Florida will divert $200 million of federal electric‑vehicle‑charging money to build air‑taxi pads. The move reshapes how public funds support mobility, shifting from ground‑level chargers to vertical take‑off points for emerging eVTOL services.

The state plans to locate pads at golf courses, luxury apartment complexes and regional airports. Officials argue the network will shorten trips that currently require a car ride to the nearest charger. Critics warn that the reallocation reduces the charger count needed to meet the federal goal of nationwide EV coverage.

---

Tesla rolled off its 10 millionth electric vehicle this week, a milestone that puts the company halfway to one of four production targets tied to Elon Musk’s $1 trillion compensation package. The figure comes from TechCrunch, which notes the milestone as a key lever in Musk’s pay structure.

The same source reminds readers that Tesla’s Model S still relies on driver‑assist features that demand constant human vigilance. The HN post warns that the semi‑autonomous system can lull drivers into complacency, creating a safety risk. Tesla’s broader ambition includes a ride‑sharing fleet that will compete with Uber and Lyft, but the company has barred its cars from being used on rival platforms.

Beyond the headline, the milestone highlights Tesla’s dominance in the EV market. With a market cap near $144 billion, Tesla still dwarfs General Motors, whose valuation hovers around $45 billion. The gap underscores why Tesla’s production numbers matter to investors and to the regulatory conversation about autonomous vehicle safety.

---

General Motors unveiled a new modular electric‑vehicle platform dubbed Ultium, accompanied by a battery pack that uses large‑format pouch cells. The HN‑sourced briefing says the cells range from 50 kWh to 200 kWh and can be stacked vertically or horizontally, enabling ranges of 400 miles or more.

GM altered the cell chemistry from the industry‑standard NCM to NCMA, adding aluminum and cutting cobalt content by 70 percent. The company also trimmed wiring by roughly 80 percent compared with the Chevy Bolt architecture. These changes aim to push battery‑pack costs below $100 per kilowatt‑hour, a price point GM believes is critical for scaling.

The automaker pledged $20 billion in capital and engineering spend through 2025 to back the platform. It will underpin 20 new electric nameplates by 2023, including the Cadillac Lyriq crossover, two Hummer EV variants, a refreshed Chevy Bolt, and the Cruise Origin self‑driving pod slated for a near‑term launch. The platform’s flexibility mirrors Volkswagen’s MEB architecture, but GM emphasizes its ability to support front‑wheel, rear‑wheel, all‑wheel and performance‑grade drivetrains.

Infrastructure discussions have expanded beyond plug‑in stations. The HN post documents electric buses charging at depots in Inverness and at bus stations, illustrating that electrification reaches heavy‑duty fleets. In Italy, buses have used inductive charging at stops for five years, a technology comparable to Qi wireless chargers in smartphones.

Scotland’s remote Ardnamurchan peninsula now hosts a charger with multiple adapters, showing that even sparsely populated regions receive investment. Meanwhile, London’s Ultra‑Low‑Emission Zone, which the post cites as preventing roughly 10 000 premature deaths annually, exemplifies how policy can drive demand for zero‑emission transport. The post also urges ride‑hailing apps to let users request all‑electric rides, a feature that could amplify the impact of emerging fleets like Tesla’s.

## Industry context

The push to blend electric propulsion with vertical take‑off and landing (VTOL) services reflects a broader industry trend: using existing funding streams for new mobility concepts. Florida’s reallocation mirrors similar debates in California, where state grants for charging stations have been proposed for autonomous shuttle pilots. The tension between ground‑based charging infrastructure and aerial mobility assets raises questions about the most efficient use of limited public dollars.

At the same time, battery chemistry advances like GM’s NCMA cells could lower the cost barrier for both ground and air electric vehicles. Lower‑cost, higher‑energy packs make longer‑range eVTOLs more viable, while also supporting the higher power demands of fast‑charging stations. If GM meets its sub‑$100/kWh target, developers of air‑taxi networks may find the economics of operating fleets more attractive.

## Technical mechanics

Pouch‑style cells differ from the cylindrical formats used by many legacy EVs. Their flat shape allows designers to pack cells more densely, which is why GM can claim vertical stacking without sacrificing structural integrity. The addition of aluminum to the cathode reduces reliance on cobalt, a metal linked to supply chain volatility and ethical concerns.

Inductive charging, highlighted in the HN post, works by creating a magnetic field between a coil in the road and a receiver on the vehicle. While the technology adds convenience, it currently delivers lower power than plug‑in DC fast chargers. For buses that stop frequently, the trade‑off between dwell time and charging speed can be acceptable, but eVTOLs will likely need higher power density to achieve rapid turnaround.

## Policy friction

Federal EV‑charger funds were allocated under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, which earmarked money for building a nationwide charging network. Florida’s decision to repurpose $200 million for air‑taxi pads could set a precedent for other states to reinterpret the intent of the law. Environmental groups argue that diverting funds undermines the act’s climate goals, while industry advocates claim that air‑taxi services could reduce vehicle miles traveled on congested roads.

Local jurisdictions, such as London’s ULEZ, illustrate how policy can accelerate adoption of zero‑emission transport. The zone’s health‑impact data—10 000 early deaths per year—provides a stark metric for evaluating the effectiveness of any mobility investment, whether it funds chargers or aerial pads.
