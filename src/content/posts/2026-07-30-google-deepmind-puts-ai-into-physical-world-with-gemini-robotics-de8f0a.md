# Google DeepMind's Ambitious Leap into Physical AGI

Google DeepMind's latest AI model, Gemini Robotics 2, has made significant strides in physical artificial general intelligence (AGI). The new model can control entire humanoid robots, allowing them to walk, crouch, stretch, and manipulate objects.

## The Next Frontier in Robotics

The Gemini Robotics 2 model builds upon the previous version, which focused on controlling a humanoid robot's upper body. With the new model, robots can perform a wider range of actions, as demonstrated by Apptronik's Apollo 2 robot. Videos shared by Google show the robot bending over to pick up a watering can and finding specific items on a shelf.

## Risks and Uncertainty

While the advancements in Gemini Robotics 2 are impressive, they also come with risks. Plopping AI into the real world is a complex task that requires careful consideration of safety, ethics, and potential consequences. Google DeepMind has acknowledged the challenges, but the extent of their planning and preparedness remains unclear.

## The Broader Industry Context

The development of Gemini Robotics 2 is part of a broader trend in AI research, where companies are exploring the potential of AGI in various domains. The use of AI in robotics has been gaining traction, with applications in manufacturing, healthcare, and other areas. However, the integration of AGI into physical systems raises questions about accountability, liability, and the potential for unintended consequences.

## A History of Caution

The development of Gemini Robotics 2 is not without precedent. Prior launches of AGI-powered robots have raised concerns about safety and ethics. The 2019 launch of Boston Dynamics' Atlas robot, for example, sparked controversy over the potential for robots to be used for malicious purposes. Earlier incidents of robots malfunctioning or causing harm have also highlighted the need for more stringent regulations and guidelines.

## Technical Mechanics

Gemini Robotics 2 utilizes a combination of machine learning algorithms and computer vision to enable robots to perform complex tasks. The model is trained on a vast amount of data, allowing it to learn from experience and adapt to new situations. The integration of AGI into physical systems requires a deep understanding of robotics, AI, and computer science.
