Shimmer released a web‑based ADHD coaching platform that moves the core experience from phone screens to laptops.

The startup says its community now exceeds 60 000 members, it has recruited more than 20 coaches, and it has already served over 1 000 members through the new program. The move follows a year‑long effort to address the “mobile‑first” bias that many ADHD tools still carry.

## The mobile‑first trap for ADHD users

People with ADHD often struggle to keep information in view. A notification that disappears off a small phone screen can be lost forever, and the constant buzz of other apps competes for attention. Shimmer’s founders observed that members would set goals on the web, then forget them when they switched to their phones.

The platform’s designers note that laptops provide a stable visual field. A larger display lets users keep goals, actions, and coach notes in sight while they work. The web app also sidesteps the habit of opening a phone during a deep‑focus session and getting pulled into social feeds.

## What the web interface actually does

The new system adds several entry points that let members start a coaching task from any device, then continue it on the web without losing context. Expanded typing areas let users draft reflections, journal entries, and action plans without the cramped mobile keyboard. A persistent visual cue for coaching goals stays on the screen, nudging users toward completion.

Beyond typing, the web app supports bookmarking and quick retrieval of past insights. Users can tag a breakthrough moment, then pull it up later with a few clicks. The platform also integrates pre‑ and post‑session workflows, so coaches can capture notes directly in the same environment where members type their reflections.

## Why the shift matters for the market

ADHD coaching has largely lived in the app ecosystem, where push notifications and short‑form interactions dominate. That model works for habit‑forming products but clashes with the deep‑thinking work many ADHD users need. By offering a distraction‑reduced web space, Shimmer challenges the assumption that mobile is always the best channel.

Competitors such as Simple Habit and BetterHelp still rely on native apps for most of their therapeutic content. Those services have the advantage of native push, but they also inherit the same attention‑splitting problem. Shimmer’s approach could force the niche to reconsider how much of the coaching workflow truly belongs on a phone.

## Technical trade‑offs and community feedback

Building a responsive web app for a user base that spends most of its time on laptops required a redesign of the existing mobile‑first codebase. The team added keyboard shortcuts, larger text areas, and a layout that scales from desktop monitors to small tablets. Early beta testers praised the ability to type longer reflections without the autocorrect mishaps common on phones.

However, the shift is not without friction. Some members who rely on on‑the‑go sessions missed the convenience of a native app that can be launched with a single tap. Shimmer’s engineers responded by keeping the mobile client alive for quick check‑ins while pushing deeper work to the web.

## What to watch next

The next milestone will be the platform’s adoption rate after the initial launch window. Shimmer plans to publish monthly active user numbers and coach‑to‑member ratios. Watch for any moves toward a hybrid model that blends the web’s depth with mobile’s immediacy, and for how rival coaching services respond to the same user‑experience pain points. The data will reveal whether the web can become a durable complement to mobile in the ADHD coaching space.
