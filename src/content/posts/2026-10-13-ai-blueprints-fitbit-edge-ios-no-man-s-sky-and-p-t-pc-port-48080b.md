Blueprints AI can spin a code‑compliant construction plan from a single photo.

The Santa Monica startup announced software that turns a sketch, PDF, or photo into a permit‑ready blueprint without a human drafter, according to TechCrunch.

## AI‑Driven Blueprints Disrupt Traditional Drafting

Blueprints AI markets its tool as a way to shave weeks off the permitting cycle. Contractors upload a hand‑drawn floor plan, the engine parses walls, doors, and load‑bearing elements, then outputs a drawing that meets local code references. The claim is that the output can be filed directly with municipal review boards, eliminating the back‑and‑forth that typically stalls projects.[^5][^6][^7][^8]

The service runs on a cloud model, meaning firms need only a browser and a picture of their design. TechCrunch notes the company is bootstrapped, so there is no venture‑backed pressure to overpromise. If the accuracy holds, the product could force drafting firms to reposition themselves as consultants rather than sole producers of construction documents.[^1][^2][^3]

## Fitbit Edge Positions Google Between Air and Pixel Watch

Google priced the Fitbit Edge at $179.99, a sweet spot between the stripped‑down Fitbit Air and the premium Pixel Watch. The device carries an OLED display and a chassis that feels as slim as the Air, according to TechCrunch.

By bundling Google’s fitness stack with a modest price tag, the company signals a middle‑ground strategy. The Edge offers the same health sensors found on higher‑priced wearables but drops the LTE option, keeping the bill of materials low. Competitors will need to decide whether to chase the low‑end market with stripped features or double down on the high‑end smartwatch experience.

## No Man's Sky Expands to iPhone 11 and Later

After a decade of patches and community redemption, No Man’s Sky is now available on iPhone 11 and newer models, Ars Technica reports. The port brings the procedurally generated universe to a handheld that can render billions of planets on a mobile GPU.

The move matters because it tests how far a sprawling, PC‑first title can survive on constrained hardware. Apple’s A13‑plus chips in the iPhone 11 series provide enough horsepower for the game’s core loop, but visual fidelity will be trimmed. The launch could open the door for other sandbox experiences to consider mobile as a viable first‑class platform.

## P.T. Gets Official PC Release After Years on PS4

Kojima’s notorious “playable trailer” P.T. finally leaves the aging PS4 hard drives and arrives on PC, Ars Technica confirms. The horror cult classic was pulled from digital stores years ago, leaving only physical copies and community‑hosted backups.

The PC port restores the original 720‑p video loop, the unsettling hallway, and the infamous glitch that resets the player’s progress. By making the experience legally available again, the release revives interest in a title that shaped modern horror design and forces platform holders to reckon with how they handle abandoned games.

## What to Watch

The next quarter will reveal whether Blueprints AI’s claim of permit‑ready output survives municipal audits. Google’s Edge sales data will indicate if the mid‑tier price point can sustain a profitable wearables line. iOS users will test No Man’s Sky performance on the iPhone 13 series, providing a benchmark for future console‑to‑mobile ports. Finally, the reception of P.T. on PC may prompt other developers to resurrect shelved experiences for broader platforms. Tracking these signals will show which of today’s announcements reshape their markets.
