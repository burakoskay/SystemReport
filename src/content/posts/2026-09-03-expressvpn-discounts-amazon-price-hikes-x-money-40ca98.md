## ExpressVPN's coupon blitz

ExpressVPN is slashing prices with coupons that claim up to 73 % off, and multi‑year plans now promise as much as 61 % savings, according to WIRED. The company is betting that a flood of low‑cost subscriptions will outweigh the margin loss from each deal.

The discount strategy forces rivals to either match the price or risk losing price‑sensitive users. Yet cutting the price tag that hard can erode perceived value; customers may churn once the promotional period ends, leaving the brand with a revolving door of short‑term subscribers. The flexibility of a one‑month plan versus a locked‑in three‑year commitment adds a layer of choice, but the headline‑grabbing percentages suggest a race to the bottom rather than a sustainable growth model.

## Amazon's pre‑holiday price hike

Amazon has raised the list price on its Kindle e‑readers, Echo smart speakers, Eero mesh routers and Fire TV streaming sticks ahead of the holiday shopping rush, WIRED reports.[^6][^7][^8] The adjustment hits core hardware that typically anchors holiday sales bundles.

While the hike may boost short‑term revenue, it also nudges bargain hunters toward competing retailers that still list the older pricing. Price elasticity for these devices is high; a few dollars can shift a purchase decision. The move signals that Amazon is willing to gamble on brand loyalty over pure price competition, but it risks alienating a segment of shoppers who expect the platform to be the cheapest option for holiday tech gifts.

## X replaces Stripe with X Money for creator payouts

X announced that U.S. creator payouts will now be routed through its own X Money payments service, effectively retiring the Stripe‑powered system that powered payouts until now, TechCrunch notes. The shift centralizes the flow of money under X’s financial infrastructure.

By owning the payout pipeline, X gains richer data on creator earnings and tighter control over transaction fees and compliance. Creators, however, may view the change with suspicion; Stripe’s reputation for reliability and transparent pricing set a benchmark. If X Money imposes higher fees or less predictable settlement times, it could push high‑earning creators to platforms that still rely on third‑party processors, fracturing X’s creator ecosystem.

## Google backs geothermal power for AI compute

Google has signed a deal to purchase 400 MW of enhanced geothermal electricity from Fervo, a transaction that could scale to a full gigawatt to power a massive AI data center in Utah, TechCrunch reports. The move aligns the tech giant’s AI compute ambitions with its public sustainability goals.

Geothermal energy offers baseload power with a low carbon footprint, a stark contrast to the intermittent nature of solar or wind. Scaling from 400 MW to 1 GW will test the limits of current drilling technology and the economics of heat extraction at that magnitude. If Google can lock in a stable, renewable supply, it may lower the operational cost of running AI workloads, giving it a competitive edge in a market where energy bills are a significant line item.

## Jio's $11 AI‑ready PCs from old hardware

Jio is piloting a program that converts aging desktop computers into AI‑ready machines for roughly $11 for a two‑month subscription, TechCrunch says. The offer targets users in emerging markets who cannot afford brand‑new hardware but still want to run AI‑driven applications.

The low price point is enticing, but the performance ceiling of refurbished hardware will limit the complexity of models that can run locally. The initiative also raises questions about the lifespan of the repurposed devices and the environmental impact of extending their use. Still, providing a gateway to AI tools at a price that many can afford could accelerate digital adoption and spur a new wave of low‑cost innovation in regions that have been left behind by the high‑end PC market.

## What to watch

Track how ExpressVPN’s deep‑discount model affects churn rates and whether competitors launch similar coupon campaigns. Monitor Amazon’s price adjustments through the holiday season to see if sales volume compensates for higher per‑unit revenue. Keep an eye on creator sentiment toward X Money as the first payout cycles roll out. Watch whether Google’s geothermal purchase expands to the projected gigawatt and how that influences its data‑center operating costs. Finally, follow Jio’s rollout metrics—subscription uptake, device reliability, and any partnership announcements that could scale the $11 AI‑ready PC model.
