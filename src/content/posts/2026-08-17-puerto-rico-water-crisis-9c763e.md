## Introduction to the Crisis
Puerto Rico could capture billions of gallons of water a year by implementing systems to catch rainwater as it falls. The island's geography, with its frequent rainfall, makes it an ideal location for rainwater harvesting. This method of collecting and storing rainwater could significantly reduce the island's reliance on traditional water supply systems.
## The Potential of Rainwater Harvesting
The island could collect a substantial amount of rainwater. This water could then be used for various purposes such as drinking, irrigation, and other non-potable uses. By harvesting rainwater, Puerto Rico could reduce the strain on its existing water supply systems, which are currently struggling to meet the demand. This, in turn, could help to prevent water rationing and ensure a more stable water supply for the island's residents.

By examining the history of water management in Puerto Rico, it becomes clear that the island's water crisis is a complex issue with deep-rooted causes.
## Technical Mechanics
The technical mechanics of rainwater harvesting are relatively straightforward. The process involves collecting and storing rainwater in tanks or cisterns, which can then be used for various purposes. However, the implementation of rainwater harvesting systems on a large scale is more complex. It requires significant investment in infrastructure, including the installation of collection systems, storage tanks, and distribution networks. Additionally, the water collected must be treated and tested to ensure it is safe for consumption.
