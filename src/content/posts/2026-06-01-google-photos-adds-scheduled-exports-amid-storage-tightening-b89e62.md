Google Photos now offers a built-in scheduler that automatically copies newly added photos and videos to a destination of your choice. This feature targets 'newly-added content' rather than the entire library.[^1]

## Storage pressure drives new tools

Google's storage ecosystem is under strain. The company reports that users add more than 4.3 million GB of data across Gmail, Drive, and Photos each day. Every account still receives a free 15 GB quota shared among those services, and once that quota is exceeded, new uploads are blocked until additional space is purchased. A June 2021 policy change treats 'High Quality' photos the same as original-quality files, counting them against the 15 GB limit.[^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] Accounts inactive for two years risk deletion of their media.[^3][^6]

The storage changes are driven by an explosion of user-generated content. Google's storage infrastructure has to keep pace with the growing demand. To continue providing a seamless experience, Google must balance storage capacity with user needs.

The pressure on storage has led to a significant increase in the number of users exceeding their storage quota. As a result, Google is introducing new features to help users manage their storage more efficiently. The scheduled export feature is one such tool that allows users to automatically export their newly added photos and videos to an external storage service.[^1][^7]

## Collections view replaces Library tab

Google is reshaping the Photos front-end. The legacy Library tab is being retired in favor of a new Collections view that aggregates private albums, shared albums, and automatically generated folders.[^8] This shift changes how users locate and manage their content.

The Collections view aims to simplify content discovery. By grouping albums and folders in one place, users can more easily find the photos and videos they need. The change also paves the way for future features, such as Google's Gemini-powered Ask Photos.

The Collections view is designed to make it easier for users to find and manage their content. With the new view, users can quickly locate specific albums or folders and perform actions on them. This change is expected to improve the overall user experience and make it easier for users to get the most out of Google Photos.

## API deprecation reshapes developer workflows

The Google Photos Library API will drop certain scopes after a specified deadline. Google Photos is also introducing a new Photos Picker API. Developers must audit their integrations against the upcoming API sunset to avoid broken workflows.

The API changes reflect Google's efforts to refine its developer ecosystem. By streamlining APIs and deprecating unused scopes, Google aims to improve the overall developer experience. However, developers must adapt to the changes to ensure their applications remain functional.

The API deprecation is expected to have a significant impact on developers who rely on the Google Photos Library API. Developers must review their integrations and make necessary changes to ensure that their applications continue to function as expected.

## What this means

The scheduled export feature reduces friction for users who treat Google Photos as a de-facto backup. The UI shift to Collections changes how users locate content for export. Users can now more easily find and export specific albums or folders.

The scheduled export feature also benefits users who need to mirror their Google Photos library on external storage. By automating the export process, users save time and effort.[^1]

The introduction of the scheduled export feature and the Collections view reflects Google's efforts to improve the Google Photos experience. As the storage landscape continues to evolve, users and developers must stay informed to ensure a seamless experience.

Google's storage policy changes are designed to ensure that users have a seamless experience and that the company can continue to provide a high-quality service.
