## BepiColombo’s Final Approach

Eight years after launch, Europe’s BepiColombo spacecraft is closing in on Mercury. The mission’s arrival marks the longest cruise ever for a planetary probe.

The probe, a joint effort between European and Japanese agencies, entered Mercury’s sphere of influence this week. Engineers expect the first science data within days. The spacecraft carries a suite of instruments designed to probe the planet’s magnetic field, surface composition, and interior structure. The goal is to answer how Mercury formed and why it looks the way it does.

Mission controllers have already begun the delicate orbital insertion burns. Those maneuvers consume a fraction of the onboard fuel reserve, a resource that has been closely monitored throughout the eight‑year journey. The successful insertion will let the probe settle into a highly elliptical orbit, where it can map the planet’s surface with unprecedented resolution.

## US Space Rhetoric Turns Aggressive

A senior White House official recently warned that America expects foreign actors to stay on the U.S. space agenda. "Let me be clear: America is not only welcome—we want you there," the official said during a press briefing.

The blunt language sparked a flurry of commentary from diplomats and industry observers. Critics argue the stance could sour cooperation on the International Space Station, lunar gateway, and joint scientific missions. Proponents say the message underscores U.S. commitment to lead in a crowded orbital environment.

The comment came as the United States prepares its own deep‑space architecture, including Artemis lunar missions and a growing commercial launch sector. The rhetoric hints at a willingness to set the terms of engagement for any future partnership, a shift from the more collaborative tone of the past decade.

## European Launch Startups Seek New Capital

Two of Europe’s leading launch startups announced fresh fundraising rounds this week. The companies, still unnamed in public filings, are targeting the burgeoning small‑sat market and aim to secure slots on upcoming rideshare flights.

Both firms highlighted the need for additional capital to scale production of next‑generation engines and to certify their vehicles for payloads beyond low‑Earth orbit. The fundraising comes at a time when European governments are drafting new space policy frameworks that could open public‑sector contracts to private players.

Investors appear eager to back ventures that can compete with established players in the United States and China. The startups argue that a diversified launch ecosystem reduces reliance on a handful of providers and creates more resilient supply chains for scientific and commercial missions.

## The Bigger Picture: Competition and Collaboration

BepiColombo’s arrival, the White House’s assertive stance, and European startups’ fundraising all point to a shifting balance in space activities. Europe is demonstrating technical endurance with a mission that outlasts many of its peers, while the United States is reasserting political leverage.

At the same time, private European launch firms are positioning themselves to fill gaps left by national programs. Their success could enable more frequent missions to destinations like Mercury, the Moon, and beyond, without waiting for state‑run launch slots.

The convergence of long‑duration scientific missions, geopolitical posturing, and commercial financing suggests that space will become an arena where national pride, economic opportunity, and scientific curiosity intersect more tightly than before.

## What to Watch

Next month, BepiColombo will begin its primary science campaign, delivering the first high‑resolution maps of Mercury’s magnetic field. In Washington, the administration is expected to release a policy paper outlining criteria for foreign participation in U.S. space projects. European launch startups are slated to announce the names of their investors and the specific vehicle upgrades they plan to fund by year‑end. Tracking these three threads will reveal whether cooperation can survive the emerging competition.
