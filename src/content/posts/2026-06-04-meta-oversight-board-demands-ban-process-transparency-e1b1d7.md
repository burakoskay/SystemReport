## Oversight board slams ban process
Meta's independent Oversight Board warned that users are being cut off without a transparent path to appeal. The board's statement singled out the lack of due process as a breach of basic procedural fairness. 
Board members also pressed Meta to publish the criteria that trigger bans and to disclose how its AI models evaluate content. 
The Oversight Board's critique highlights the need for accountability in Meta's moderation practices. Without clear guidelines, users are left in the dark about what constitutes a violation and how to contest a ban. 
This lack of transparency can have significant consequences, particularly for creators who rely on Meta's platforms for their livelihood. A ban without a clear explanation or appeal process can be devastating, resulting in lost income and reputation damage. 
Furthermore, the opaque nature of Meta's ban process may lead to inconsistent application of rules, causing confusion among users and potentially undermining trust in the platform.

## Hidden face-recognition code in smart-glasses platform
Code reviewed by WIRED uncovered an unreleased face-recognition system embedded in Meta's smart glasses platform. It's designed to identify people via biometric data stored on users' phones. 
This discovery raises concerns about user consent and data privacy. Meta has faced criticism in the past for its handling of facial recognition technology, and the presence of this hidden code may exacerbate those concerns. 
The use of biometric data to identify individuals also raises questions about the potential for misuse or exploitation. As face-recognition technology becomes more prevalent, it is essential to ensure that companies like Meta are transparent about their data collection and use practices. 
The fact that this code was embedded in the smart glasses platform without users' knowledge or consent may be seen as a breach of trust, and could lead to increased scrutiny of Meta's data handling practices.
