Apple's new Get Ready tool lets customers lock in iPhone Duo orders before the official rush on Oct 16. By letting shoppers pre‑order a day early, Apple hopes to smooth the inevitable queue that every flagship launch generates.

The tool appears on Apple’s website this week and accepts reservations for the iPhone Duo ahead of the Friday, Oct 16, pre‑order window. Users simply enter their Apple ID, choose a color and carrier, and receive a confirmation that the device will be allocated when the public sale begins. The move mirrors similar early‑access mechanisms Apple tested for previous iPhone releases, but the timing this year feels more tactical given supply chain jitters.

## HomePod mini 2 and AirPods beta tweaks

Apple unveiled the HomePod mini 2 yesterday, exactly six years after the original model hit shelves. The refreshed speaker adds a new ultra‑wide tweeter and a modest bump in battery life, but the chassis remains unchanged. Pre‑orders opened alongside the announcement, signaling Apple’s intent to keep the smart‑speaker line alive without a full‑scale redesign.

The same day Apple pushed developer and public beta firmware to several AirPods models, including the AirPods Pro 3. The beta introduces a low‑latency mode for gaming and a refined spatial audio algorithm. While the changes feel incremental, they demonstrate Apple’s habit of using beta cycles to iterate on hardware that is already in customers’ ears.

## Vintage MacBook finally obsolete

Apple added the last 12‑inch MacBook model to its vintage and obsolete list, joining two other products already flagged. The 12‑inch line, praised for its thinness, never captured a mass market and has been out of production for years. By labeling it obsolete, Apple warns developers and repair shops that it will no longer receive parts or software updates.

The designation is more than a footnote; it signals the end of any lingering support for a device that Apple marketed as a premium ultraportable. Users still holding the laptop will need to migrate to newer hardware or risk security exposure.

## Legal battles: $5.7 B verdict and OpenAI docs

Apple is asking a federal judge to overturn a $5.7 billion patent verdict against it from Taction Technology. The lawsuit centered on the Taptic Engine used in iPhones and Apple Watches. Apple argues the damages were calculated on guesswork rather than a rigorous methodology.

Separately, a judge granted Apple early access to OpenAI internal documents and employee testimony in a trade‑secrets case. The expedited discovery could give Apple a strategic edge as it accuses former employees of leaking proprietary AI research to a competitor. Both cases illustrate how Apple is fighting on multiple legal fronts, from hardware patents to emerging AI disputes.

## Market shift in China

IDC reports that China’s overall smartphone shipments fell 15.4% in Q3 2026. Despite the market contraction, Apple’s share of shipments in the region grew, even as iPhone shipments declined 4.3% year‑over‑year. The paradox suggests Apple is capturing a larger slice of a shrinking pie, likely by focusing on premium models that retain demand.

The data also hints at a broader realignment: while budget brands bleed volume, Apple leans on brand loyalty and ecosystem lock‑in to maintain relevance. For investors, the mixed signal—shrinking volume but rising share—adds nuance to the narrative of Apple’s performance in Asia.

## What to watch next

The overhauled Health app expected in iOS 27.2 is absent from beta 4, meaning Apple will likely delay its rollout. Developers should monitor the next beta for its appearance, as the health suite has been a focal point for Apple’s services revenue.

Meanwhile, the outcome of the Taction verdict appeal and the OpenAI discovery will shape Apple’s patent strategy and AI positioning. Finally, the Get Ready tool’s impact on pre‑order logistics will be evident in the first‑day sales numbers on Oct 16. Those metrics will tell whether early access truly eases the rush or simply shifts the bottleneck.

**What to watch:** the Oct 16 iPhone Duo pre‑order statistics, the next iOS 27.2 beta for the Health app, the federal judge’s ruling on the $5.7 B verdict, and Apple’s share movement in China’s Q4 shipments.
