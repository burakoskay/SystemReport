OpenAI posted a security notice requiring macOS users to update ChatGPT and Codex due to a third-party developer tool issue. The company emphasized 'out of an abundance of caution' and found no evidence of user data being accessed.

## The looming ChatGPT redesign

The Financial Times reported that a revamped ChatGPT will roll out in the coming weeks. A senior OpenAI employee stated, 'Chat is dead,' signaling a strategic pivot. This overhaul suggests a shift toward more API-centric usage or tighter integration with developer tools. Such a move would align with OpenAI's growing focus on providing predictable, programmatic access to its models, rather than a conversational interface.

The revamp may also reflect OpenAI's efforts to expand its developer offerings. By making ChatGPT more API-friendly, the company can encourage developers to build applications on top of its models, potentially increasing adoption and usage. This approach can also help OpenAI to differentiate itself from competitors, which are also developing conversational agents and developer-focused models.

## macOS apps forced into a security sprint

OpenAI singled out Axios, a third-party developer tool linked to a broader industry incident. The company updated its security certifications and required users to install the latest builds of ChatGPT and Codex. This swift action demonstrates OpenAI's commitment to security and its proactive approach to mitigating potential risks.

The incident highlights the growing awareness of supply-chain risks in AI software distribution. As AI models become increasingly complex and interconnected, the potential attack surface expands, making it essential for companies like OpenAI to prioritize security and take proactive measures to protect their users.
