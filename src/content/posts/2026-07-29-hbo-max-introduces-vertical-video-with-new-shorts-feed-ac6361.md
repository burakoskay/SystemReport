# HBO Max Adopts Vertical Video
HBO Max is rethinking content discovery with a new 'Shorts' feed that uses vertical video. This format is popularized by platforms like TikTok and Instagram Reels.
## Content Discovery Challenges
Large libraries overwhelm viewers, making it hard to find content. HBO Max's 'Shorts' feed is designed to make content more accessible. Viewers are often presented with an abundance of options, leading to decision paralysis. The 'Shorts' feed aims to mitigate this issue by providing bite-sized content that can be easily consumed.
## Industry Context
HBO Max's move mirrors similar efforts by other streaming services. They are rethinking content discovery as large libraries make it hard for viewers to find something to watch and as audiences become accustomed to short-form content on platforms like TikTok and Instagram Reels. The shift towards vertical video is a response to changing viewer habits and the need for more engaging and accessible content. Other streaming services, such as Netflix and Disney+, have also been experimenting with new content discovery features. For example, Netflix has introduced a 'Fast Laughs' feature, which provides short-form comedy clips. Disney+ has also introduced a 'Shorts' section, featuring short-form content from its various franchises.
## Technical Mechanics
The implementation of vertical video on HBO Max's 'Shorts' feed is a significant technical undertaking. It requires adjustments to the platform's video encoding, playback, and user interface. The use of vertical video also presents opportunities for more creative and immersive storytelling, as it allows for a more intimate and engaging viewing experience. The technical team at HBO Max will need to ensure that the 'Shorts' feed is optimized for various devices and screen sizes, to provide a seamless viewing experience for users. This may involve developing new algorithms for video encoding and playback, as well as redesigning the user interface to accommodate the new vertical video format.
## History of Vertical Video
The use of vertical video is not new, but it has gained popularity in recent years with the rise of social media platforms like TikTok and Instagram Reels. These platforms have shown that vertical video can be an effective way to engage audiences and provide a unique viewing experience. Other companies, such as Snapchat and YouTube, have also experimented with vertical video. The success of these platforms has likely influenced HBO Max's decision to introduce a 'Shorts' feed with vertical video.
## Downstream Implications
The introduction of the 'Shorts' feed has implications for content creators and the broader streaming industry. It creates new opportunities for creators to produce short-form content that is optimized for vertical video. The success of the 'Shorts' feed will also be closely watched by other streaming services, as they consider their own strategies for content discovery and viewer engagement. Content creators will need to adapt to the new format and develop content that is engaging and effective in a vertical video format. This may require new skills and techniques, such as filming and editing for a vertical format.
## Next Steps
HBO Max will populate the 'Shorts' feed with content and measure its success. The platform will likely monitor viewer engagement and feedback to refine the 'Shorts' feed and improve the overall viewing experience. The company may also consider expanding the 'Shorts' feed to include other types of content, such as user-generated videos or live streams.
