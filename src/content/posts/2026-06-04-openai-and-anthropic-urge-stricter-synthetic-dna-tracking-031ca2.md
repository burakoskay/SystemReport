## AI Labs Push for Stricter Tracking of Synthetic DNA

OpenAI and Anthropic have joined other leading AI labs and scientists in urging lawmakers to improve the tracking of synthetic DNA sequences that could be used to develop biological weapons. The move comes as concerns grow over the potential misuse of AI and biotechnology.

The letter, signed by executives and scientists from various AI labs, highlights the need for better regulation and monitoring of synthetic DNA sequences. This comes as policymakers struggle to keep pace with the rapid advancements in AI and biotechnology.

## The Stakes of AI Regulation

The call for stricter tracking of synthetic DNA sequences comes as the US faces a potential shift in tech regulation under a new administration. With Vice President Kamala Harris possibly becoming the Democratic nominee, her stance on tech regulation is under scrutiny. Harris has previously spoken about the need for regulating AI, saying that she and President Biden 'reject the false choice that suggests we can either protect the public or advance innovation.'

Harris has also emphasized the importance of holding tech companies accountable, saying that 'in the absence of regulation and strong government oversight, some technology companies choose to prioritize profit over the wellbeing of their customers, the safety of our communities, and the stability of our democracies.'

## The Complex Landscape of AI Regulation

The push for AI regulation is happening against a complex backdrop. Representative Ro Khanna, a Democrat from California, has been vocal about the need for tech regulation. He notes that the current regulatory landscape is 'stupid and scary,' with many attempts at regulation being met with resistance from tech companies.

Khanna also highlights the challenges of regulating AI, saying that 'the politics of tech have changed a lot in these past few years — and things are only going to get both more complicated and more tense as Trump and Biden head into what will obviously be a contentious and bitter presidential election.'

## A History of AI Regulation Efforts

The US government has been grappling with AI regulation for several years. In 2020, the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) released a report on AI standards, which highlighted the need for more research on AI safety and security. The report also noted that AI systems can be vulnerable to attacks, which could have significant consequences.

In 2022, the Biden administration released an executive order on AI, which emphasized the need for more transparency and accountability in AI development. The order also called for more research on AI safety and security.

## The Technical Mechanics of Synthetic DNA Tracking

The tracking of synthetic DNA sequences is a complex technical challenge. Synthetic DNA sequences can be used to develop biological weapons, such as anthrax or Ebola. The sequences can be designed to be highly contagious and deadly, making them a significant threat to public health.

The current methods for tracking synthetic DNA sequences are limited. Most tracking systems rely on manual reporting and verification, which can be time-consuming and prone to errors. AI-powered systems could potentially be used to improve the tracking of synthetic DNA sequences, but they would require significant investment and development.
