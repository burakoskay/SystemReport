## Riot removes voice chat in League of Legends patch 26.20

Riot announced the removal of built‑in voice chat in patch 26.20 of *League of Legends*, according to Engadget. The change eliminates a long‑standing communication channel for the game’s 115‑million‑plus monthly players.

Riot did not provide a detailed rationale, but the timing coincides with a broader push to streamline client resources. Players who relied on the feature will need to adopt third‑party solutions or rely on external voice services. The move underscores how even the largest live‑service games can prune core utilities when engineering priorities shift.

## Polygon sold to Valnet, staff cut overnight

Vox Media transferred ownership of Polygon to Valnet, the content‑aggregator behind sites such as Game Rant and OpenCritic. The deal triggered immediate layoffs of senior staff, including co‑founder and editor‑in‑chief Chris Plante, senior writer Michael McWhertor, and longtime reporter Nicole Carpenter.

Polygon co‑founder Brian Crecente posted on Bluesky, "just completely sickened by this news." The sentiment was echoed by Curation Editor Pete Volk, who wrote, "Working at Polygon was a wonderful experience, and I’m proud of the work we did there." Senior Entertainment Reporter Petrana Radulovic lamented losing a job she loved just as summer blockbuster season began. Special Projects Editor Matt Leone added a brief note, "I really can’t complain too much—Polygon was a great place to work for the last decade‑plus—but if anyone’s hiring, please reach out!"

Valnet’s portfolio generates over 260 million page views across dozens of brands. Founder Hassan Youssef previously helped run Mansef Inc., the company behind Brazzers and a former minority investor in Pornhub. The acquisition marks Valnet’s entry into mainstream gaming journalism, a sector historically dominated by independent outlets.

## Consolidation pressures and the Valnet model

Valnet defends its business model by emphasizing high‑volume content production and a reputation for "high‑quality journalism." The company recently sued The Wrap in federal court, claiming the lawsuit harms its ability to staff a growing network of writers.

Critics note that Valnet’s writers often receive low pay for lengthy articles, a claim the company rejects. The contrast between Valnet’s aggressive growth strategy and Polygon’s legacy of deep‑dive reporting raises a structural tension: can a mass‑content operation sustain the investigative standards that made Polygon a respected voice?

The sale also reflects a broader trend of media consolidation in the gaming space. Larger parent companies acquire niche sites to capture audience share, then streamline operations to improve margins. When the cost of maintaining a dedicated editorial team exceeds projected ad revenue, layoffs become the default response.

## Enshittification and the future of gaming journalism

The rapid erosion of editorial independence at Polygon mirrors a phenomenon journalist Cory Doctorow calls "enshittification." The term describes how platforms prioritize engagement and revenue over content quality, leading to a spiral of click‑bait and reduced editorial resources.

Blue’s News, a 30‑year‑old site that has resisted corporate acquisition, offers a counterpoint. Founder Stephen "Blue" Heaslip describes his outlet as "no AI slop, no sponsored posts" and emphasizes a minimalist design that avoids the ad‑heavy layouts common on larger networks. Heaslip’s stance illustrates that a lean, ad‑light model can survive, but only at a much smaller scale.

Polygon’s fate suggests that even well‑established brands are vulnerable when parent companies prioritize scale over depth. The loss of veteran journalists removes institutional memory and reduces the industry’s capacity for nuanced analysis. As more gaming news sites become subsidiaries of conglomerates, the risk of homogenized coverage grows.

## Technical nuance: anti‑aliasing in PC games

Engadget notes that anti‑aliasing smooths jagged edges in PC games, but not all methods achieve the same result. Techniques such as MSAA, FXAA, and TAA differ in performance cost and visual fidelity. Understanding these trade‑offs matters for developers who must balance frame‑rate targets against image quality.

The discussion of anti‑aliasing highlights a broader point: technical decisions in games have downstream effects on player experience and community expectations. Just as Riot can remove a core feature like voice chat, developers can alter rendering pipelines, and both actions reverberate through the player base.

## What to watch next

Track Valnet’s integration of Polygon over the next six months, especially any shifts in editorial tone or content volume. Monitor Riot’s communication channels for follow‑up on the voice‑chat removal and any community backlash. Finally, watch how independent outlets like Blue’s News adapt to the pressure of enshittification while maintaining editorial integrity.
