SpaceX added Sequoia veteran Roelof Botha to its board, a move that surprised the venture firm’s partners. The appointment arrives as the aerospace maker tightens its governance amid soaring launch demand and as rivals scramble for talent.

A biker‑and‑bug‑lover coalition set up camp outside the proposed Google data center east of London, demanding the project be halted. The protest joins a wave of European opposition to the tech giant’s expansion plans.

## Boardroom shock at SpaceX

TechCrunch reports that Sequoia’s partners felt blindsided when Botha, a longtime Sequoia steward, accepted a seat on SpaceX’s board.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] Botha’s résumé includes leading the firm’s growth‑stage investments and guiding dozens of unicorns.[^7][^8] His shift to a board role at a company that frequently backs Sequoia‑backed rockets raises questions about conflict management.

The move also signals SpaceX’s intent to deepen its ties with Silicon Valley capital. By pulling a Sequoia insider into its governance, the launch company may be positioning itself for new financing rounds or strategic partnerships. The decision could reshape how venture firms influence aerospace startups, a sector that has traditionally operated with a thin board layer.

## Binance CFO departs after $6.3 billion breach

A week after hackers siphoned $6.3 billion worth of cryptocurrency from Binance users, the exchange announced its chief financial officer is leaving to “pursue a new venture.” The CFO’s exit underscores the operational fallout from the breach, which remains one of the largest crypto thefts on record.

The departure also leaves Binance scrambling to reassure regulators and investors. With the CFO’s financial oversight gone, the exchange must reassign risk‑management duties while it rebuilds its security architecture. The move may prompt other crypto firms to reassess their executive stability in the wake of high‑profile attacks.

## Epic Games pushes Linux compatibility

Communications Director Jake Jones confirmed to GamingOnLinux that the Epic Games Launcher will support SteamOS in its preview release. The announcement expands the launcher’s Linux footprint beyond Fedora, Cachy, and Omarchy, adding the Valve‑backed distro to a growing list.

Epic’s push into SteamOS matters because it offers a mainstream gateway for games like Fortnite to reach Linux users without workarounds. By providing native .deb packages instead of AppImage bundles, Epic reduces friction for developers targeting the Debian/Ubuntu ecosystem. The move could accelerate Linux adoption among gamers who have historically relied on Windows.

## Bikers, bug‑lovers, and a Google data center showdown

A coalition of cyclists and entomology hobbyists gathered outside the planned Google data center site east of London, demanding the project be stopped. Wired describes the group as “Bikers and Bug‑Lovers” who argue the facility threatens local ecosystems and community space.

The protest reflects a broader European pushback against large‑scale data infrastructure. Critics cite energy consumption, water usage, and habitat disruption. Google has yet to comment on the specific objections, but the demonstrators plan to maintain a physical presence until planning permission is revisited.

## What to watch next

SpaceX’s board reshuffle will be tested at the next quarterly meeting, where both governance and financing decisions will surface. Binance’s next CFO appointment and its response to regulator inquiries will indicate whether the exchange can stabilize after the hack. Epic Games’ full Linux rollout is slated for later this year; adoption metrics will reveal if the move translates into market share. Finally, the outcome of the Google data center planning review will set a precedent for future data‑center siting across Europe. Track corporate filings, regulator statements, and community actions to gauge how these storylines evolve.
