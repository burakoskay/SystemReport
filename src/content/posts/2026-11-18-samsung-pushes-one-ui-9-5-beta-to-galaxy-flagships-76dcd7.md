## Samsung opens One UI 9.5 beta to a handful of Galaxy phones
The beta for One UI 9.5 has begun appearing on a select group of Samsung Galaxy devices. The move signals the next step in Samsung's incremental rollout strategy, where new code lands on a limited set before a wider launch.

On April 9, 2026 Samsung expanded the One UI 8.5 beta to the Galaxy S23 series, Z Fold5, Z Flip5, S23 FE and A36 5G. The rollout started in India, South Korea, the United Kingdom and the United States. Those devices received a phased release, with foldables limited to Korea and the US and the A36 5G limited to India. Users signed up through the Samsung Members app.

## How the beta is being delivered
Samsung treats the beta as a phased release rather than a global push. The company lists market‑specific availability in the fine print and warns that timing may vary. This mirrors the 8.5 rollout, where the same app served as the enrollment portal and the same phased logic applied.

The rollout uses the standard OTA channel. Eligible phones receive a notification in the Settings menu, prompting users to download the build. Samsung requires participants to opt‑in, which lets the firm collect crash logs and usage metrics while keeping the experience optional for the broader user base.

## What One UI 9.5 might change
Samsung has not published a feature list for the 9.5 beta. Historically, beta builds introduce UI refinements, performance tweaks, and early integrations for upcoming hardware features. The company’s footnote on AirDrop‑style Quick Share support mentions that the feature is currently limited to the Galaxy S26 series, leaving the new devices without that capability for now.

Because the beta is still in early stages, most participants will notice subtle visual adjustments rather than headline‑grabbing new apps. The real test will be how Samsung balances battery impact with the smoother animations it typically promises in beta cycles.

## Competitive pressure from Apple and the open‑source world
Apple recently refreshed its iOS 26.5 beta 1 (v2) and iPadOS 26.5 beta 1 (v2) builds, replacing the March 30 seed. The timing underscores how the two giants still favor staged testing rather than a single public launch. Apple’s approach remains closed‑source, while Samsung’s beta runs on top of its proprietary One UI layer.

At the same time, the community‑driven AsteroidOS 2.0 reached its first stable release. The watch‑focused OS adds an always‑on display, expanded watch support, new launcher styles, customizable quick settings, and noticeable UI performance gains. Battery life improvements and a slew of stability fixes round out the update. AsteroidOS also gained broader integration through Gadgetbridge 0.73.0 and Amazfish, showing how open‑source projects can iterate quickly without the corporate gatekeeping that defines Samsung’s beta.

## Why the beta matters for power users
Power users and developers rely on beta builds to validate apps against upcoming platform changes. Samsung’s decision to widen the beta pool to older flagships and an A‑series model gives a broader test audience. That could surface edge‑case bugs that Samsung missed during internal testing.

The phased rollout also lets Samsung compare performance metrics across regions. Differences in network infrastructure, carrier‑level customizations, and regional UI preferences can all influence how the final release behaves. For developers, early access means they can adjust code for new APIs before the official launch, reducing post‑launch friction.

## What to watch next
The next checkpoint will be Samsung’s public announcement of the full One UI 9.5 release date. Observers should also track whether Quick Share gains AirDrop‑style compatibility on the newly enrolled devices. On the competitor side, Apple’s move to beta 2 will reveal whether the two ecosystems converge on similar feature sets. Finally, the AsteroidOS community plans a 2.1 point release that adds full mainline kernel support for additional watches, a development milestone worth monitoring for anyone watching the open‑source wearable space.
