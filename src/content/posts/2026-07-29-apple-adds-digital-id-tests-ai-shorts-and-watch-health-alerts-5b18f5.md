## Digital driver’s licenses go statewide, again

Apple is pushing its digital driver’s license feature into a 15th U.S. jurisdiction. The rollout adds one more state to the list that already includes 14 states and Puerto Rico. 9to5Mac spotted backend changes that signal the new addition is imminent, though the state name remains under wraps.

The expansion matters because it brings Apple Wallet one step closer to becoming a universal ID hub. Each state that joins requires a separate partnership with the local Department of Motor Vehicles, a process that can take months. The fact that Apple secured a 15th agreement this quarter suggests the company’s lobbying and technical integration teams are finally hitting a rhythm.

## HBO Max Shorts test shakes up iPhone video

Warner Bros. is slipping a new vertical‑video format called HBO Max Shorts into the hands of select iPhone users. The test runs on devices in the United States and leans on an AI tool to generate the clips. 9to5Mac reported that the AI assists with editing, pacing, and captioning, aiming to make short‑form content feel native to the iPhone’s portrait orientation.

Vertical video has already saturated platforms like TikTok and Instagram. HBO Max’s gamble is to see whether its premium brand can survive in that space without diluting its long‑form reputation. Early feedback will determine if the AI‑driven workflow is worth scaling or if it will be another niche experiment.

## Instapaper 10 reshapes reading on iPhone and iPad

Instapaper launched version 10 of its iPhone and iPad app, bundling a redesign of its web platform with a suite of new features. The update introduces a “Liquid Glass” reading mode, fresh Home Screen widgets, and a set of new fonts that aim to reduce eye strain during long reading sessions.

The redesign is more than cosmetic; it reorders how users interact with saved articles. By moving key actions to the widget layer, Instapaper hopes to cut the friction between discovery and consumption. The move reflects a broader trend where niche productivity apps double down on iOS integration to stay relevant against native Apple services.

## Apple Watch fall detection proves life‑saving in real world

A Cincinnati, Ohio resident credits his Apple Watch with saving his life after a sudden collapse outside his home. Mohammad Islam says the watch’s fall detection feature automatically called emergency services, dispatching help before he could press a button.

WKRC’s coverage highlighted that the feature relies on a combination of accelerometer data and machine‑learning models to distinguish a hard fall from everyday movement. The incident underscores the practical value of health sensors that many users treat as optional accessories.

## CEO transition frames Apple’s next moves

Tim Cook and incoming CEO John Ternus sat down with Deadline to discuss the leadership handoff, Apple TV strategy, and the broader roadmap. Cook emphasized continuity, noting that the executive team remains focused on delivering hardware upgrades and services growth.

Ternus, who rose through the hardware engineering ranks, hinted at a tighter integration between iOS devices and Apple’s media offerings. The timing of the digital ID expansion, the HBO Max Shorts test, and the Instapaper partnership suggests the new leadership will lean into cross‑platform experiences while keeping health and safety features front‑center.

## What to watch next

The next state to join the digital driver’s license program will reveal how quickly Apple can scale the feature nationally. Keep an eye on whether HBO Max Shorts graduates from a limited test to a permanent app slot, and watch for user sentiment on Instapaper’s new UI. Finally, monitor how Ternus’ tenure influences Apple’s health‑sensor roadmap and whether more life‑saving features roll out beyond fall detection.
