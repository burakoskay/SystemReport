Apple shipped macOS 26.5.1 to stop M5‑based Macs from powering off unexpectedly, and a Weibo leaker just confirmed a vapor‑chamber cooling system inside the upcoming iPhone Ultra. The twin announcements tighten the squeeze on enterprise IT teams and power users who have been waiting for reliable silicon and cooler phones.

The macOS update targets a bug that forced M5 silicon Macs to shut down under specific workloads, according to 9to5Mac. The same outlet also reported that the iPhone Ultra rumor includes a vapor chamber and an updated release window, while its daily deals roundup listed the M5 MacBook Air at $270 off and the M5 MacBook Pro at $300 off launch price. Those numbers set the stage for a busy holiday shopping season.

## macOS 26.5.1 patches M5 shutdown bug

Apple’s latest point release, macOS 26.5.1, arrives only weeks after developers flagged random power‑off events on M5‑chip Macs. The fix lives in a kernel patch that stabilizes the power‑management controller when the CPU hits sustained high‑load thresholds. Enterprise admins who have been rolling rollbacks can now push the update without fearing data loss.

The timing feels intentional. Apple released the patch ahead of the WWDC keynote, where it will likely showcase the next generation of macOS features. By nailing the bug now, the company avoids a potential PR nightmare that could have forced IT departments to postpone hardware refreshes.

## iPhone Ultra vapor chamber leak

Fixed Focus Digital posted a screenshot of a leaked iPhone Ultra internal diagram that shows a vapor‑chamber heat‑spreader sandwiched between the logic board and the glass. Vapor chambers are standard in high‑end Android flagships but have never appeared in an iPhone, making the claim noteworthy.

The same post hinted at a release timing shift, pushing the Ultra’s launch back by a month to align with the fall holiday window. If the cooling system lives up to the leak, the Ultra could finally handle sustained 5G streaming and gaming without throttling, a pain point for power users.

## Holiday pricing on M5 hardware and AirPods

9to5Mac’s lunch‑break roundup showed the M5 MacBook Air now selling for up to $270 off its retail price, while the M5 MacBook Pro hit an all‑time low of $1,499 on Amazon, a $300 discount from launch. Those cuts make the current‑gen M5 line the most affordable Apple silicon laptops on the market.

AirPods aren’t immune to the price swing. The AirPods Pro 3 are back at holiday pricing on Amazon, and AirPods 4 enjoy a flat 25 % discount. For developers who need a reliable Bluetooth audio companion for remote pair‑programming, the savings are welcome, but the deals also signal Apple’s push to clear inventory before the next hardware cycle.

## watchOS 27 rumors ahead of WWDC

The same source that broke the iPhone Ultra leak also teased three watchOS 27 features slated for next week’s WWDC reveal. Early reports point to a low‑power health sensor, a more granular workout API, and a redesigned complication system that lets developers push richer data to the watch face.

If Apple follows its pattern, watchOS 27 will ship alongside iOS 18 and macOS 27, tightening the integration across devices. Power users will likely see tighter latency between watch and Mac, a boon for developers who already rely on Continuity features.

## What to watch

Watch for Apple’s official WWDC announcements on watchOS 27 and the iPhone Ultra’s final specs. The next macOS security bulletin will confirm whether the M5 shutdown fix holds up under heavy enterprise workloads. Finally, track inventory levels for the discounted M5 laptops; a rapid sell‑through could force Apple to adjust its supply chain ahead of the 2024 refresh.
