## Gaming Site Polygon Hit by Layoffs Amid Sale to Valnet

Polygon, a 13-year-old gaming news site, has been sold to Valnet, a content-aggregating company, amid significant layoffs that have affected veteran journalists, including co-founder and editor-in-chief Chris Plante and Senior Writer Michael McWhertor.

The sudden job cuts, which were unexpected by those affected, come as Vox Media, the former owner of Polygon, has transferred ownership of the site to Valnet. The move has left many in the gaming journalism community reeling, with some expressing shock and disappointment at the news.

## What Happened

According to reports, the layoffs were announced suddenly, with some employees learning of their job loss via social media. Polygon Senior Reporter Nicole Carpenter, for instance, published a story about the Epic vs. Apple case just before sharing news of her layoff on Bluesky. "Along with just about everyone else at Polygon, I am now out of a job, ending over a decade at Vox Media for me," Curation Editor Pete Volk wrote on Bluesky.

## Industry Context

The gaming journalism landscape has been undergoing significant changes in recent years, with many sites struggling to stay afloat amid increasing competition and declining revenue. The sale of Polygon to Valnet is just the latest example of this trend, which has seen many respected gaming sites change hands or shut down altogether.

The move has also sparked concerns about the "enshittification" of online content, a term coined by journalist Cory Doctorow to describe the decline in quality of online media. As sites like Polygon struggle to adapt to changing market conditions, many are worried about the impact on the quality of journalism and the types of stories that get told.

## The History of Polygon

Polygon was founded in 2009 by Chris Plante and Michael McWhertor, and quickly established itself as a leading voice in the gaming journalism community. The site has been known for its in-depth coverage of the gaming industry, as well as its commitment to showcasing the work of independent developers and small studios.

## The Technical Mechanics
