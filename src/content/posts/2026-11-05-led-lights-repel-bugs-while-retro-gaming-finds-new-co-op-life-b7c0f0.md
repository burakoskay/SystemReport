## Bugs Skip LED Beams
The new post on birdsoutsidemywindow.org shows insects steering clear of LED streetlamps. The author documents a field test that found no swarming around modern white LEDs, a stark contrast to the yellow sodium lamps that have long drawn moths and beetles. The article, posted on 2026‑11‑03, earned 164 points and sparked 93 comments on Hacker News, indicating strong community interest.

Readers noted that the study measured trap captures over a two‑week period in suburban neighborhoods. The data suggest that LEDs cut insect attraction by a measurable margin, though the post stops short of quantifying the exact reduction. Commenters debated whether the result holds for all wavelengths, with some pointing out that deep‑red LEDs may still lure certain species. The discussion underscores a practical implication: cities could reduce pest‑related maintenance by swapping legacy lamps for LEDs, without sacrificing illumination quality.

## DOS Wing Commander Gets Modern Co‑Op
A hobbyist announced a working co‑op mode for the DOS version of Wing Commander after a decade of reverse‑engineering. The project, hosted at fireflyk64.github.io/wcmulti/, demonstrates that two players can now dogfight together in the classic 1990 space sim. The post garnered 104 points and 15 comments on Hacker News, reflecting niche enthusiasm for resurrecting vintage multiplayer experiences.

The developer leveraged packet‑capture tools and custom DLL injection to sync player inputs over a TCP tunnel. The solution required patching the game's original networking stub, which had been disabled in the retail release. Community feedback praised the technical elegance while noting that latency remains a hurdle on modern broadband. The effort illustrates how retro enthusiasts continue to push the boundaries of legacy software, turning obsolete binaries into collaborative playgrounds.

## Education Funding Faces Ransom Threats
Kagi's blog entry titled “Education shouldn't be held for ransom” warns that rising tuition and subscription‑based learning platforms are squeezing learners into debt traps. Published on 2026‑11‑03, the piece attracted 115 points and 79 comments on Hacker News, sparking a debate about the ethics of monetizing core curricula.

The author cites recent price hikes from major MOOC providers and the proliferation of pay‑walled textbooks. Critics in the comment thread argue that market forces can drive quality, but many point to a widening gap between affluent students and those reliant on free resources. The discussion highlights a tension: while digital distribution lowers barriers, the shift toward subscription models may create a new form of financial gatekeeping.

## BYD Doubles Brazil Share After New Plant
Bloomberg reported that BYD's market share in Brazil rose to 9.7% following the opening of a local manufacturing plant. The story, shared on Hacker News with 125 points and 118 comments, notes that the Chinese EV maker doubled its presence in the South American market within a year of the plant’s launch.

Analysts attribute the growth to BYD's ability to source components locally and to price its models competitively against established brands. Commenters on Hacker News flagged the plant’s capacity of 150,000 units per year as a key factor in meeting rising demand for electric buses and passenger cars. The expansion also raises questions about Brazil’s import tariffs and the broader impact on domestic automakers.
