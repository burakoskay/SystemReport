## AMD keeps AM4 alive, locks AM5 in through 2029

The 5800X3D and 7700X3D are the latest CPUs to join AMD's AM4 lineup, priced at $349 and $329 respectively. This move demonstrates AMD's commitment to its older AM4 socket, which refuses to die. By continuing to support AM4, AMD is providing a stable platform for users who have invested in this socket.

AMD also confirmed that its newer AM5 socket will receive new CPUs at least until 2029. This guarantees a long-term platform for builders who have already invested in the new socket. A long socket lifespan reduces churn for OEMs and waste for consumers. This approach may pressure rivals to rethink their own platform strategies.

The decision to extend AM5 support through 2029 is significant, as it provides a clear roadmap for developers and consumers. This stability can lead to more optimized software and hardware development, ultimately benefiting users.

## Intel unveils Crescent Island, a low-cost AI chip

Intel revealed Crescent Island, an air-cooled AI chip that uses LPDDR5 memory. The design cuts cost and power consumption compared to Nvidia's and AMD's options. This move is part of Intel's strategy to make AI more accessible and affordable.

Crescent Island's use of LPDDR5 memory is notable, as it provides a balance between performance and power consumption. This chip is expected to appeal to developers and companies looking for cost-effective AI solutions.

## Nvidia enters PC silicon arena, targets Apple Silicon

Nvidia announced its first PC-focused chip, targeting high-end laptops. The product is a direct alternative to Apple's in-house silicon. This move marks Nvidia's entry into the PC hardware market, where it will compete with established players like AMD and Intel.

Nvidia's focus on high-end laptops suggests that it is targeting the premium segment of the market. This strategy could pay off, as high-end laptops often command higher prices and offer better profit margins.

## Dell counters the $599 MacBook Neo

Dell launched a new laptop priced at $599, directly matching the cost of Apple's MacBook Neo. Dell's offering targets budget-conscious consumers seeking a lightweight Windows experience.

According to Dell, there's a strong suspicion that Apple's MacBook Neo launch was imminent, allowing Dell to prepare a response. This proactive approach by Dell demonstrates its commitment to competing in the PC market.
