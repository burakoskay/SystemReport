## Automattic board forces Matt Mullenweg into leave

Automattic’s board placed founder Matt Mullenweg on a forced leave of absence. The decision arrived in a Slack message that Mullenweg described as “against his will.”

The move marks a rare public rupture at a company built around open‑source software. Automattic has not disclosed the board’s rationale, and no external commentary has clarified whether performance, governance, or personal disputes drove the action. The silence fuels speculation among investors and developers who rely on WordPress for their businesses.

## Turning Point bets tens of millions on midterms

A year after the death of activist Charlie Kirk, Turning Point USA announced a spending plan that targets the 2024 midterm elections. Sources say the organization will allocate “tens of millions” to advertising and voter‑turnout operations.

The money will flow primarily to New Hampshire, Arizona, and Nevada. Those states are swing battlegrounds where small shifts can decide Senate control. Turning Point’s strategy mirrors past efforts that focused on localized media buys and grassroots mobilization.

The scale of the spend raises questions about the group’s fundraising pipeline. Critics argue that the influx of cash could amplify polarizing messaging, while supporters claim it will boost civic engagement among young conservatives.

## Amazon sued over pregnancy accommodations

Four women have filed a lawsuit accusing Amazon of denying basic workplace accommodations to pregnant employees. The complaint alleges that Amazon refused bathroom breaks and failed to provide appropriate chairs.

The plaintiffs also claim they were terminated after raising the issue. Amazon has not yet responded publicly to the filing. If the allegations hold, the case could expose a gap between the retailer’s public commitments to employee welfare and its on‑the‑ground practices.

The lawsuit arrives amid broader scrutiny of big‑tech labor policies. Regulators and labor advocates have been tracking how large employers handle pregnancy‑related needs, and this case may become a reference point for future enforcement actions.

## Meta’s AI agent steals Muse’s social handles

Meta’s new AI assistant, also named Muse, inadvertently captured the social‑media handles of the British rock band Muse. The mix‑up occurred when Meta executives tagged the band instead of the AI agent.

The exact circumstances remain unclear, but the error highlights the challenges of managing brand identities in an environment where AI products share human‑readable names. The band’s online presence was temporarily redirected, causing confusion among fans and advertisers.

Meta has not issued a formal apology, and the incident has sparked discussion about the need for stricter naming protocols for AI agents. The episode serves as a reminder that even large tech firms can stumble on seemingly simple branding decisions.

## What to watch next

Automattic’s board will likely convene again to outline a succession plan for Mullenweg. Observers should track any shareholder filings that reveal the board’s longer‑term vision.

Turning Point’s ad buys will become visible in the weeks leading up to the November elections. Monitoring the content of those ads will indicate whether the group leans toward issue advocacy or pure voter mobilization.

The Amazon lawsuit will move through the district court docket. Any settlement or court ruling will set a precedent for how tech giants must accommodate pregnant workers.

Meta is expected to rename or rebrand its AI agent to avoid further collisions. The company’s next public statement will signal whether it treats the mishap as a minor glitch or a systemic branding flaw.
