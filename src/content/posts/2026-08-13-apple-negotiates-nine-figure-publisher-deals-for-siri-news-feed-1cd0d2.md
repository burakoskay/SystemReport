Apple is in talks with major news publishers to pay for real‑time content that will power the next version of Siri. The move aims to fix the headline‑generation errors that forced Apple to pull news summaries from iOS 18.

The Wall Street Journal reports that Apple has floated a nine‑figure budget for the deals and wants multiyear contracts where publishers are paid each time Siri cites their articles. The arrangement differs from typical AI licensing, which usually involves a flat fee for broad content access. Apple plans to roll the new Siri AI out this fall on iOS 27, iPadOS 27, and macOS 27. The company previously struck content agreements for Apple News+ in 2019, but some publishers later terminated those partnerships over revenue disputes.

## Deal Structure and Financial Scale

Apple’s proposal ties compensation to actual usage rather than a fixed lump sum. Under the draft terms, a publisher would receive a payment every time Siri pulls a headline or excerpt from its site. The model mirrors the per‑click royalties used in digital advertising, but it is novel for large‑language‑model assistants.

A nine‑figure budget signals that Apple expects a substantial volume of queries. The exact figure was not disclosed, but the WSJ describes it as “nine‑figure,” implying at least $100 million. Apple has not revealed how many publishers are in the negotiation pool, but the language of “multiyear agreements” suggests a long‑term commitment.

The usage‑based approach also sidesteps the flat‑fee structures that have drawn criticism from newsrooms. Those deals often grant AI firms unrestricted scraping rights for a set price, regardless of how often the content appears in downstream products. Apple’s model could give publishers more visibility into the value generated by their stories.

## Technical Motivation and Past Missteps

Siri’s upcoming AI overhaul is a departure from the rule‑based assistant that launched in 2011. The new version relies on large language models to generate conversational answers, including news summaries. Apple’s push for fresh content follows a high‑profile failure in iOS 18, where the “notification summary” feature displayed fabricated headlines that misled users.

Apple removed the news‑summary widget for more than a year after the incident. Internal reports describe the episode as an embarrassment that eroded user trust in AI‑generated news. The company has since been tightening its content pipelines, which explains the urgency of securing reliable sources for the fall launch.

The technical challenge is twofold: ingesting up‑to‑the‑minute articles and ensuring the model does not hallucinate. Direct licensing from publishers could give Apple vetted, timestamped feeds that reduce the risk of spurious claims. It also aligns with Apple’s broader strategy of keeping AI training data under tighter control, as seen in earlier agreements that granted the firm AI‑training rights.

## Publisher Reactions and Market Context

Publishers have mixed feelings about the proposal. Some see a usage‑based payout as a fairer reflection of their content’s value, while others remain wary after the Apple News+ fallout. In 2022, a handful of outlets walked away from Apple’s News+ program, citing low revenue shares and opaque metrics.

The current negotiations arrive at a time when other AI players are locking down news licenses. Google, Microsoft, and Meta have all signed broad‑scope contracts that pay flat fees for unlimited access. Apple’s approach could force a rethink of how media companies monetize AI consumption, especially if the usage model proves more lucrative.

Industry analysts note that Apple’s brand cachet may attract premium publishers that have resisted AI deals so far. If Apple can demonstrate that its per‑use payments translate into measurable earnings, it could set a precedent for a more granular licensing ecosystem.

## Implications for AI Assistants

The Siri AI rollout will test whether a curated news pipeline improves user experience. Accurate headlines are a low‑stakes but visible metric; a failure here could echo the iOS 18 debacle. Conversely, a steady stream of verified news could restore confidence in voice assistants as reliable information sources.

Apple’s move also raises questions about data ownership. By paying per use, Apple may limit the amount of raw text it stores, potentially reducing the scope of model training on copyrighted material. That could influence ongoing legal debates about AI training data and fair use.

Competitors will watch Siri’s performance closely. If Apple’s model yields higher accuracy without sacrificing speed, other firms might adopt similar licensing structures. The shift could reshape the economics of AI content ingestion, moving the market away from flat‑fee bulk deals toward performance‑based contracts.

## What to Watch

The first test will be the Siri AI debut in iOS 27, scheduled for the fall. Track how often the assistant cites partner publishers and whether Apple releases usage metrics. Also monitor any public statements from participating newsrooms about revenue impact. The next quarterly earnings report may reveal whether the nine‑figure outlay translates into measurable user engagement or subscription growth. Those data points will indicate whether Apple’s usage‑based licensing gains traction across the AI assistant market.
