## Genetics rebuts "race realism"
Modern genetics does not support claims of innate racial differences in intelligence, according to a recent Ars Technica analysis. The piece emphasizes that the genetic variation observed across populations does not map onto the controversial notion of a fixed IQ hierarchy.

The article cites peer‑reviewed studies that show heritability estimates for cognitive traits are complex and heavily mediated by environment. It also points out that the genetic markers once thought to differentiate groups are largely shared worldwide, undermining any scientific basis for the "race realism" argument.

## Army postpones lead‑azide test amid public alarm
The U.S. Army has delayed detonating a stockpile of lead azide, labeling it "catastrophically hazardous." Modeling predicts a plume of neurotoxic lead could travel up to 100 miles, raising concerns for nearby communities.

Officials cited the model’s worst‑case scenario, which includes wind patterns that could carry the toxin across state lines. The delay reflects pressure from local residents who expressed fear and anger after learning about the potential exposure.

## FAA prepares to reveal ground‑stop cause
The Federal Aviation Administration will soon announce the root cause behind a recent nationwide ground stop that halted commercial flights for several hours. A former FAA insider warned that upgrading the agency’s decades‑old communication network carries inherent risk.

The insider suggested that a software mismatch in the legacy system triggered the outage, and that the agency is now weighing a full replacement versus incremental patches. The upcoming disclosure is expected to shape future investment in aviation infrastructure.

## Fitbit reset: restarting vs. factory wiping
Engadget explains that restarting a Fitbit merely reboots the operating system, while a factory reset erases all user data and restores default settings. The distinction matters for users who plan to sell or recycle the device.

A factory reset clears paired Bluetooth connections, removes saved health metrics, and forces the device to re‑pair as if it were brand new. The article advises backing up any important logs before initiating the wipe.

## Anthropic employee claims Claude solved the Riemann Hypothesis
Levent Alpöge, an Anthropic researcher, says he collaborated with the Claude language model to produce a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, the century‑old problem that underpins much of analytic number theory. Alpöge’s claim appears in an Engadget feature that details his workflow with the AI.

The post notes that the purported proof has not been peer‑reviewed, and that the mathematics community remains skeptical until the argument is vetted by experts. Nonetheless, the claim highlights the growing role of large language models in exploratory research.

## When to ditch a cracked screen protector
Engadget lists practical signs that a phone’s screen protector needs replacement. Visible cracks, bubbling, and a noticeable reduction in touch responsiveness are primary indicators.

The article also mentions subtler cues: a faint halo around bright objects, increased glare, and the appearance of dust particles under the protector. Ignoring these signs can lead to the underlying glass becoming more vulnerable to impact.

## iCloud Private Relay versus traditional VPNs
Apple’s iCloud+ service includes Private Relay, which routes user traffic through two separate relays to obscure IP addresses and encrypt DNS queries. The feature offers privacy benefits but does not replace a full‑featured VPN.

Private Relay cannot mask all traffic types, such as UDP‑based gaming packets, and it does not allow users to select exit locations. For comprehensive location spoofing or corporate network access, a conventional VPN remains necessary.

## Galaxy Z Fold 8 pushes foldable usability
The Galaxy Z Fold 8 arrives as the latest iteration in Samsung’s foldable lineup. Its hinge mechanism now tolerates more frequent opening cycles, and the inner display supports a 120 Hz refresh rate for smoother scrolling.

The device’s outer screen runs Android with a thin bezel, enabling quick interactions without unfolding. Reviewers note that the form factor feels less fragile than earlier models, making it a viable daily driver for users who need both phone and tablet real estate.
