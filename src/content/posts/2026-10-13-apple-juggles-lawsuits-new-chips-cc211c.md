Apple finds itself fighting a courtroom battle, unveiling new hardware, and pushing services—all within a single week.

Epic Games says Apple engineered a pause in their App Store case, while Apple pushes an M6‑powered MacBook Pro, fresh iCloud+ perks, a Vision Pro horror debut, and the start of iPhone Duo pre‑orders.

## Epic Games accuses Apple of manufacturing a pause

Epic Games filed a fresh court brief alleging that Apple deliberately manufactured a procedural pause in the ongoing antitrust fight over App Store commission rates. The brief frames the pause as a tactical move to buy time and pressure the court.

Apple’s response rejects the accusation outright, insisting the pause reflects standard procedural scheduling and not a calculated strategy. The clash adds another layer to a dispute that has already produced multiple injunctions and public statements from both sides.

## M6 MacBook Pro promises three upgrades

Apple announced a new 14‑inch MacBook Pro slated for launch later this month. The chassis retains the current industrial design, but the machine will ship with Apple’s next‑generation M6 silicon.

Industry analysts expect three hardware upgrades tied to the M6 chip, though Apple has not disclosed the specifics. The upgrades likely target performance, efficiency, and possibly GPU core counts, given the pattern of previous silicon iterations. Early reviewers will be watching to see whether the promised gains justify the premium price tag.

## iCloud+ adds a handful of new subscriber benefits

Apple’s iCloud+ service, long known for expanding storage, received a batch of new subscriber benefits in the past few weeks. The rollout includes enhancements that deepen privacy controls and broaden the suite of cloud‑based tools available to paying users.

While Apple has not published a detailed checklist, the timing suggests the company is using iCloud+ to reinforce its services revenue as hardware margins tighten. Subscribers will need to explore the updated dashboard to see which new tools apply to their workflows.

## Vision Pro’s first immersive horror film arrives

Apple unveiled “Remnants,” billed as the first immersive horror experience for its Vision Pro headset. The film drops this week, accompanied by a teaser video that showcases the headset’s spatial audio and 3D visual capabilities.

The move signals Apple’s intent to seed Vision Pro with original content that pushes the platform beyond productivity and into entertainment. Horror, with its reliance on atmosphere and surprise, may be a litmus test for how comfortable users feel in fully immersive environments.

## iPhone Duo pre‑order process opens early

Apple opened the iPhone Duo pre‑order flow on Friday, but the company let customers begin configuring their devices today via the Apple Store online and the Store app. Shoppers can lock in exact specifications before the official launch.

The early configuration step mirrors Apple’s recent approach to high‑demand product drops, aiming to smooth the checkout experience and reduce server overload. Observers will monitor whether the tactic curbs the long‑queue frustrations that plagued previous releases.

## What to watch next

Keep an eye on the court docket for any rulings that could reshape App Store economics, especially if Epic’s pause claim gains traction. The M6 MacBook Pro benchmarks will arrive within days, offering a concrete measure of Apple’s silicon roadmap. iCloud+ feature adoption rates will reveal whether the service can offset hardware slowdown. Vision Pro’s “Remnants” reception will hint at the viability of original immersive titles, while iPhone Duo sales figures will indicate market appetite for Apple’s latest dual‑screen experiment.
