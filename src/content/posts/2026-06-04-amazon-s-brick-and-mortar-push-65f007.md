## Amazon's Brick-and-Mortar Push

Amazon plans to open 300-400 bookstores across the US, marking a significant shift in the e-commerce giant's strategy. The company seeks to integrate physical locations with its online efforts. This move is notable, especially when compared to Barnes & Noble's current store count of 640.

The news came during General Growth Properties' fourth-quarter conference call, where CEO Sandeep Mathrani revealed Amazon's plans for a substantial brick-and-mortar presence. According to Mathrani, 95% of all soft goods are bought in brick-and-mortar stores. Consumers are still seeking in-person shopping experiences, as evident in the growth of fast-fashion brands like H&M and Uniqlo.

## The Rise of Omni-Channel Retail

The trend towards brick-and-mortar expansion among e-commerce companies reflects a growing recognition of the importance of physical locations in the retail experience. Amazon's brick-and-mortar strategy is informed by its e-commerce data, including sales and customer ratings. The company's new stores will stock books and other products, and serve as showcases for its gadgets, such as the Echo speaker and Fire TV streaming service.

## Autonomous Robots in the Warehouse

Amazon has also been investing in autonomous robots to improve its warehouse operations. The company's new Proteus robot is fully autonomous and can be controlled using plain language. This technology will improve inventory management and order fulfillment. The Proteus robot is part of Amazon's efforts to upgrade its logistics and supply chain capabilities.

## History of Amazon's Retail Expansion

Amazon's entry into physical retail is part of a broader trend among e-commerce companies, including Warby Parker, Bonobos, and Birchbox. These companies have recognized the importance of physical locations in building brand awareness and providing customers with a tangible shopping experience. Amazon's expansion into brick-and-mortar retail is a significant development in the company's evolution, marking a shift away from its early focus on online-only sales.

## Technical Mechanics

The integration of online and offline data is a key aspect of Amazon's brick-and-mortar strategy. The company's use of data from its e-commerce site to inform its physical store operations could have broader implications for the retail industry. This approach allows Amazon to create a seamless shopping experience across channels. For example, customers can browse products online and then pick them up in-store, or vice versa.
