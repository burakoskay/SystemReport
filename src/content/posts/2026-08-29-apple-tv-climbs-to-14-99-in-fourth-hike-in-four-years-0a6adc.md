Apple TV+ now costs $14.99 per month, a $2 jump that pushes the service into the $15‑plus tier for the first time. The increase arrives alongside a $20 rise in the annual plan and a $2 bump for Apple One bundles that include the streaming service.

The change went live on Friday, with Apple notifying existing subscribers before their next billing cycle, according to Deadline and Variety. Monthly Apple TV+ fees moved from $12.99 to $14.99, while the yearly subscription climbed from $99 to $119. Apple One’s entry‑level tier, which bundles Apple TV+, Apple Music, iCloud storage and other services, rose from $19.95 to $21.95 per month. This marks the fourth price adjustment Apple has made to its streaming offering in as many years.

## The price change in detail

Apple’s pricing announcement is terse: a flat increase that translates to roughly a 15 % rise for the monthly plan and a 20 % jump for the annual commitment. The company framed the adjustment as a response to rising content costs, a rationale it has echoed in previous hikes. The immediate effect is a higher headline price that aligns Apple TV+ with the mid‑range tier of competing services.

For users on the Apple One bundle, the $2 increase may feel more pronounced because it affects three other services as well. The bundle’s new price of $21.95 still undercuts the combined cost of subscribing to each service separately, but the margin has narrowed. Existing subscribers will see the change reflected on their next statement, giving them a brief window to switch to the annual plan if they prefer a lower per‑month rate.

## Why Apple is pushing higher fees

Apple’s subscription business has matured from a niche add‑on to a core revenue stream. Since launching Apple TV+ in late 2019, the company has invested heavily in original programming and licensing deals, driving up content acquisition costs. Each price hike appears timed to recoup those expenses while preserving subscriber growth.

The fourth hike in four years suggests a pattern: Apple is comfortable raising fees on a regular cadence. The company’s broader ecosystem, anchored by the Apple One bundle, gives it leverage to shift costs across multiple services without losing customers who value the convenience of a single bill. Critics have called the moves “aggressive,” but Apple’s balance sheet can absorb short‑term churn in exchange for higher average revenue per user.

## How the hike fits into the streaming market

Streaming rivals have also been nudging prices upward. While Apple’s increase is modest compared to some premium tiers, it narrows the gap between Apple TV+ and services that bundle higher‑budget productions. The shift signals that Apple no longer positions its service as a low‑cost entry point but as a competitive player with a growing library.

The price adjustment may also influence how Apple markets its original titles. With a higher price tag, Apple will need to demonstrate value through marquee shows and exclusive releases. The company’s recent slate includes several high‑profile series that could justify the added cost for fans.

## What to watch next

Apple will likely monitor churn rates over the next quarter to gauge subscriber tolerance. The next data point to track is whether the company rolls out additional original content that can offset price sensitivity. A further adjustment to Apple One pricing could appear if the bundle’s cost advantage erodes further. Keep an eye on Apple’s earnings calls for hints about future pricing strategy and on competitor moves that could reshape the mid‑tier streaming segment.
