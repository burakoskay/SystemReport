## Greenairy plants office air with smart towers
Greenairy unveiled a tower of leafy greenery that filters office air. The unit combines plant biology with sensor feedback to target volatile organic compounds that accumulate in closed workspaces. The design fits a standard desk footprint and plugs into a wall outlet for low‑power operation. Early testers report a perceptible reduction in the "stale" smell that often follows a day of HVAC cycling.
The system logs pollutant levels to a companion app, letting facilities managers see real‑time data. By exposing the raw sensor readout, Greenairy avoids the vague "better air" claim that many indoor‑air products make. The approach mirrors a growing niche of bio‑integrated office accessories that aim for measurable health benefits.

## Rivian R2 adds live pet cam to lockscreen
Rivian R2 owners can now display a live feed of a pet on the phone lockscreen. The feature streams video from a compatible camera and updates whenever the vehicle is locked. It lets users glance at a cat, dog, or even a reptile without unlocking the phone.
Installation requires a Rivian‑approved camera that pairs via the vehicle's infotainment system. Once linked, the lockscreen widget draws power from the phone, not the car, preserving battery life. The move follows a trend of integrating personal‑care functions into vehicle interfaces, blurring the line between transportation and home monitoring.

## Ring smart lock survives power loss with hand crank
Ring introduced a smart lock that includes a built‑in hand crank. The crank generates enough electricity to open the deadbolt when the lock's batteries are exhausted. Users can turn the crank for a few seconds and gain entry without a backup key.
The mechanical generator is sealed inside the lock body, preserving the device's weather‑proof rating. The design addresses a common complaint that smart locks become useless during outages. By providing a purely manual power source, Ring sidesteps the need for external battery packs or wired backups.

## Surface Laptop Ultra showcases NVIDIA RTX Spark
The Surface Laptop Ultra arrives as a premium notebook powered by NVIDIA's RTX Spark. The laptop sports a high‑resolution display and a chassis that reviewers describe as "beautiful". RTX Spark brings hardware‑accelerated ray tracing to a thin‑and‑light form factor.
Despite its aesthetic focus, the device delivers desktop‑class graphics performance, enabling creators to edit 4K video and render complex 3D scenes without a bulky workstation. The combination of a sleek form and advanced GPU marks a shift toward high‑performance laptops that do not sacrifice design.

## Google patches Android quirks across ecosystem
Google rolled out three separate fixes in October 2026. First, the Pixel 11 series remains on an older OS version; the October update did not upgrade the phones to Android 17 QPR1. The delay leaves Pixel owners awaiting the next patch cycle.
Second, a bug that prevented smart LEDs from changing color through Google Home has been corrected. The fix restores expected behavior for millions of Hue and comparable bulbs. Third, Android Auto now respects the "autoplay disabled" setting, stopping music from starting automatically when a car powers on. The trio of updates illustrates Google's ongoing effort to polish edge‑case interactions that affect daily use.
