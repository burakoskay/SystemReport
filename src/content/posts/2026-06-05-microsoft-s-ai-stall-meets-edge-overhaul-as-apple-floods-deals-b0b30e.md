## Microsoft’s AI Products Miss the Mark

Microsoft’s AI offerings are not selling, according to a recent WIRED interview. The outlet spoke with VP Scott Hanselman, who warned that the company appears to be in catch‑up mode.

Hanselman cited sluggish revenue from Azure AI services and a wave of GitHub incidents that have eroded confidence among developers. He did not provide exact figures, but the tone was clear: Microsoft’s AI ambitions have stalled while rivals push ahead.

The lack of traction matters because AI is now a primary growth engine for cloud providers. If Microsoft cannot convert interest into paying customers, it risks ceding market share to OpenAI, Google and Amazon, all of which have announced aggressive pricing and feature roadmaps. The gap is not just financial; it also affects talent recruitment and ecosystem vitality.

## Edge Ditches Master Passwords for Windows Hello

Microsoft Edge is removing the traditional master password from its password manager, as reported by 9to5Google. The browser now forces users to authenticate with Windows Hello before any saved credentials are revealed.

The change eliminates a single point of failure but also raises friction for users who rely on a simple PIN or password. Windows Hello requires biometric or hardware‑based verification, meaning Edge users on unsupported devices lose access to their vault entirely.

Edge’s move reflects a broader industry push toward password‑less authentication. Apple’s Safari already leans on iCloud Keychain tied to Face ID, and Google Chrome has begun surfacing passkeys. Microsoft’s decision signals a willingness to sacrifice convenience for security, but it may also alienate a segment of its user base that values cross‑platform portability.

## Apple’s Discount Blitz Hits AirPods, Watch, and MacBook Air

9to5Mac listed a series of steep discounts across Apple’s flagship hardware. AirPods Pro 3 and AirPods Max 2 are advertised at nearly 25 % off, while the Apple Watch Series 11 drops to $299, a $100 reduction. The entry‑level Apple Watch SE 3 is priced at $219, and a rare black Ultra Trail Loop band is also on sale.

MacBook Air models equipped with Apple’s M5 chip are advertised with up to $270 off, according to the same source. The promotions are framed as a “Lunch Break” special, suggesting a limited‑time window for consumers to snap up premium devices at consumer‑grade prices.

These deals matter because they lower the barrier for developers and power users to adopt Apple’s ecosystem. With hardware costs trimmed, more engineers can experiment with the latest silicon, potentially shifting the talent pipeline toward iOS and macOS development. The pricing also puts pressure on Windows‑centric vendors that rely on hardware bundles to drive software adoption.

## The Bigger Picture: Software Struggles vs. Hardware Incentives

Microsoft’s AI slump and Edge’s security overhaul sit uneasily alongside Apple’s aggressive hardware pricing. While Microsoft wrestles with product‑market fit, Apple leverages inventory and seasonal demand to boost unit sales.

The contrast highlights a strategic divergence. Microsoft is betting on cloud and AI services to fuel growth, yet the lack of clear revenue signals suggests execution gaps. Apple, by contrast, can stimulate demand through price cuts without compromising its premium brand, because its margins on hardware remain robust.

Developers watching these moves must weigh where to allocate resources. A developer eyeing AI workloads may find Azure’s offering less compelling than competing clouds, while the same developer could benefit from cheaper Apple hardware for testing iOS apps. The ecosystem tug‑of‑war creates a decision matrix that extends beyond pure price tags.

## What to Watch

Next quarter, Microsoft is expected to release an earnings update that will reveal whether Azure AI revenue rebounds. Analysts will also look for any clarification from the company on the GitHub troubles mentioned by Hanselman. On the Edge front, user adoption metrics for Windows Hello‑only access will surface in telemetry reports, indicating whether the security trade‑off pays off.

Apple’s discount window is slated to close by the end of the month, according to 9to5Mac. Tracking inventory levels and subsequent pricing moves will show if the company plans a repeat of the “Lunch Break” strategy during the holiday season. The interplay between Microsoft’s software challenges and Apple’s hardware incentives will shape the purchasing calculus for engineers and founders alike.
