## Trump Revises AI Executive Order

President Trump signed a revised executive order on AI oversight, requiring only voluntary prerelease government reviews of advanced models. This comes after industry objections to the original order, which would have established a framework for the government to vet the national security risks of the most advanced AI systems.

The revised order marks a significant shift from the original plan, which would have mandated government reviews of AI systems before their public release. Instead, the new order relies on voluntary collaboration with participating US-based tech companies, including Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google.

## Industry Objections and Concerns

The original order faced opposition from the tech industry, which expressed concerns that it could dull America's edge in AI technology. President Trump himself reportedly shared these concerns, saying he didn't want to do anything that would get in the way of the US lead in AI.

The push for government action on AI oversight follows growing concern within the banking industry and other institutions about the leaps in AI's abilities to find cybersecurity vulnerabilities in software. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and outgoing Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell convened an urgent meeting with Wall Street CEOs in April, warning them about the cybersecurity risks posed by Anthropic's AI model, Claude Mythos.

## AI Safety Regulations and Politics

Trump had pledged to undo the AI safety regulations set by his predecessor, Democratic President Joe Biden. Trump's administration has viewed the AI sector as an engine to help deliver on his pledges to expand the economy, and he has promoted its major players at events at the White House and around the world.

The AI sector has become a contentious issue, with Republicans divided over whether to embrace the industry or side with voters who express skepticism about the technology. The government's interest in working with Anthropic on cybersecurity is complicated by the government's ongoing legal fight with the company.

## Broader Industry Context

The AI industry has experienced rapid growth in recent years, with major players like Google, Amazon, and Microsoft investing heavily in AI research and development. The market size for AI is expected to continue growing, with estimates suggesting it will reach $190 billion by 2025. However, this growth has also raised concerns about the potential risks and challenges associated with AI, including cybersecurity vulnerabilities and job displacement.

The increasing demand for AI has led to a surge in the development of new AI models, including large language models (LLMs) like Anthropic's Claude Mythos. These models have the potential to revolutionize a wide range of industries, from healthcare to finance. However, they also raise significant concerns about cybersecurity and the potential for AI systems to be used for malicious purposes.

## History of AI Regulation

The US government has a history of regulating emerging technologies, including AI. In 2019, the Trump administration issued an executive order on AI, which established a national strategy for AI research and development. However, the revised executive order on AI oversight marks a significant shift in the government's approach to regulating AI.

In the past, the government has taken a more hands-off approach to regulating AI, allowing the industry to develop and deploy AI systems with relatively little oversight. However, as AI has become increasingly powerful and ubiquitous, concerns have grown about the potential risks and challenges associated with the technology.

## Technical Mechanics

The revised executive order relies on voluntary collaboration with participating US-based tech companies to review AI systems before their public release. This approach is designed to balance the need for government oversight with the need to avoid overregulating the AI industry.

Under the terms of the revised order, participating tech companies will be required to submit their AI systems for review by the government before they are released to the public. This review process will focus on identifying potential cybersecurity risks and ensuring that AI systems are designed and deployed in a way that minimizes these risks.

## Downstream Implications

The revised executive order has significant implications for the AI industry and the broader economy. The implementation of the voluntary review process will likely have a major impact on AI development in the US, and could influence the development of AI regulations globally.

The industry will need to take steps to address cybersecurity risks, and the government may need to take further action if the voluntary review process is not effective. The revised order also raises questions about the future of AI regulation, and whether the government will take a more active role in overseeing the development and deployment of AI systems.
