## Slate Auto’s screenless GPS approach

Slate Auto released a two‑door pickup that forgoes a digital display in favor of a spiral‑bound paper map. The vehicle’s brochure describes the map as a “bare‑bones” navigation aid, echoing the brand’s minimalist philosophy. No touchscreen, no infotainment system, just the printed atlas tucked beside the driver’s seat.

The decision eliminates the need for a dedicated GPS module, reducing component cost and power draw. It also sidesteps the software integration challenges that plague many modern trucks. Slate Auto’s marketing material frames the paper map as a nostalgic counterpoint to the ubiquitous screen.

Ars Technica noted the juxtaposition of a “screenless GPS” with a contemporary pickup, highlighting the oddity of a high‑tech vehicle relying on a 20th‑century navigation method. The article suggests the move is as much a statement about user experience as it is a cost‑saving measure.

## The resurgence of analog navigation in a digital age

Paper maps dominated driver navigation until the early 2000s, when smartphone GPS apps began to replace them. The shift was driven by the convenience of real‑time routing and automatic updates. Yet the digital transition introduced new pain points: screen glare, software bugs, and driver distraction.

A subset of drivers now prefers the tactile certainty of a physical map. The tactile feedback eliminates the need to glance at a screen, reducing eyes‑off‑road time. For long‑haul truckers, a paper atlas can survive harsh weather and rough handling better than a fragile LCD.

Industry analysts have observed a niche market for analog accessories, from laminated road atlases to magnetic map holders. The trend is not mainstream, but it reflects a broader appetite for simplicity amid feature‑bloat. Slate Auto’s offering sits at the intersection of this niche and the broader automotive market.

## Apple CarPlay’s role in driver alerts, focusing on speed‑camera warnings

Apple CarPlay integrates iPhone navigation apps with a vehicle’s display, allowing drivers to use Apple Maps, Google Maps, or Waze while the car controls audio and voice commands. Engadget explains that enabling the speed‑camera warning toggle in any of these apps triggers audible alerts when the vehicle approaches a camera.

The warning feature relies on crowdsourced databases that update in real time. When a driver passes a known camera location, the app emits a tone and flashes a visual cue. The alert works across all three supported navigation apps, provided the CarPlay settings are active.

Engadget’s guide stresses that the warning system is optional; drivers must turn it on in the app’s settings menu. The article also notes that the feature does not guarantee coverage in every jurisdiction, as some regions restrict speed‑camera data. Nevertheless, the integration demonstrates how CarPlay extends a phone’s capabilities into the vehicle’s ergonomics.

## Compatibility hurdles and aftermarket solutions for CarPlay

Determining whether a vehicle supports Apple CarPlay is straightforward, according to Engadget. The guide advises checking the infotainment system’s menu for a CarPlay logo or consulting the manufacturer’s specifications. If the vehicle lacks native support, owners can add CarPlay through aftermarket head units.

Aftermarket solutions range from plug‑and‑play adapters to full‑replace infotainment panels that run Apple’s interface. These units often require a wired connection to the car’s CAN bus and a USB link to the iPhone. Installation can be performed by a dealer or a skilled DIY enthusiast.

The proliferation of third‑party CarPlay kits reflects demand for the ecosystem’s seamless app integration. However, not all kits support every CarPlay feature; some may omit video playback or advanced voice commands. Buyers should verify feature parity before purchase to avoid a partial experience.

## What to watch

The automotive market will reveal whether Slate Auto’s analog gamble gains traction as other manufacturers experiment with low‑tech interiors. Track sales figures for the pickup and any subsequent model updates that might reintroduce digital aids.

On the software side, monitor Apple’s CarPlay roadmap for expanded driver‑assist alerts, especially as regulations around speed‑camera data evolve. Future iOS releases could tighten or relax data sharing, directly affecting the reliability of the warning system highlighted by Engadget.
