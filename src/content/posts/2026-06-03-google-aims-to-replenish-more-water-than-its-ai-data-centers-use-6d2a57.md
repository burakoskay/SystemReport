## Introduction to Water Usage in AI Data Centers
Google will aim to return more water than its AI data centers consume by 2030. The pledge includes investments in local water infrastructure and a promise to disclose usage metrics. Data center operators face scrutiny for their impact on water quality and availability. Google's commitments include a goal to replenish more water than it uses at its data centers by 2030.

## Water Use in AI Data Centers
Data center operators are under pressure to reduce their environmental footprint. Google is not alone in this effort, as companies like Microsoft are also exploring ways to minimize their water usage. The use of immersive cooling is one approach being experimented with, which can significantly reduce water draw.

## Addressing Water Concerns
OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has downplayed concerns about data centers' water use, calling them 'fake' and comparing the energy used by AI systems to that of humans. However, critics argue that water scarcity will become a bottleneck regardless of current inference efficiencies. The debate highlights the need for data center operators to be transparent about their water usage and to invest in sustainable solutions.

## Reducing Water Use
Data-center operators are experimenting with immersive cooling to reduce water use. This approach can slash water draw by orders of magnitude. Microsoft's sustainability lead Amy Luers has supported research into these alternatives, noting that even modest reductions in water use can alleviate pressure on municipal supplies. The use of immersive cooling is just one example of the innovative solutions being explored to reduce water usage in data centers.

## Technical Mechanics of Water Usage
The use of water in data centers is primarily for cooling purposes. Traditional cooling systems use water to cool the servers, which can be a significant draw on local water supplies. Immersive cooling, on the other hand, uses a liquid coolant to cool the servers, which can be much more efficient. The use of immersive cooling can reduce water usage by orders of magnitude, making it a promising solution for data center operators looking to reduce their environmental footprint.
