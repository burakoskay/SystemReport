## Introduction to the Ruling
Google must make it easier for users to install rival app stores on Android devices, Judge James Donato ruled. This decision follows a jury's verdict that Google had an illegal monopoly over Android apps.

The lawsuit, filed by Epic Games, began when Epic challenged Google's control over the Android app ecosystem. Google requires apps to be distributed through its Google Play Store, giving the company significant control over available apps.

## Background of the Lawsuit
Epic argued that Google's control was anti-competitive and stifled innovation. In 2021, a jury decided Google had an illegal monopoly over Android apps. Recently, Judge Donato ordered Google to make rival app store installs easier, one month after Epic and Google appeared to have settled.

## The Current Ruling
Judge Donato focused on easing side-loading, not mandating Play Store integration. Google will have to comply with the ruling, although the exact details have not been made public. Epic Games and other companies pushing for greater competition in the Android app ecosystem see this as a victory.

Google argued that its control over the Android app ecosystem ensures security and quality. Allowing rival app stores could compromise device security, according to Google. Epic Games and other companies countered that greater competition would lead to innovation and better prices for consumers.
