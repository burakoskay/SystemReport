Google's Android 17 push hit a snag: Samsung's latest Android 17 phones can't run the new Googlebook "Better Together" features. The mismatch forces developers and early adopters to reconsider rollout timelines.

Googlebooks now require Android 17, but Samsung's Android 17 handsets refuse to launch the "Better Together" functionality at launch. The discrepancy surfaced in the first week of the update, leaving Samsung users without access to the promised integration. Google has not issued a public timeline for a fix, and Samsung has not confirmed whether a patch is in the works.

## Google Wallet swaps list for stacked cards

Google Wallet for Android received a visual overhaul this year, and the change is now live for most users. The redesign replaces the traditional scroll list with a stacked card view that surfaces credit and debit cards in a more compact layout.

The rollout follows an earlier update that refreshed the bottom half of the homepage. Early testers report that the stacked view reduces the number of taps needed to select a card, but the change also shrinks the visible card numbers, which some power users find inconvenient. Google has not announced a timeline for additional tweaks, so the current version will likely sit in the wild for the next few weeks.

## Pixel Buds firmware adds Gemini control

Google's latest firmware push for the Pixel Buds Pro, Pro 2, and Pixel Buds 2a adds a sizable set of features, including deeper integration with Gemini. The update arrived a month after Gemini control first appeared on the earbuds, expanding voice‑activated shortcuts and on‑device processing.

The rollout is being delivered over the air, and users see a version bump in the Bluetooth settings. Early feedback highlights smoother handoff between phone and earbuds, though a subset of users reported occasional latency spikes during high‑resolution audio playback. Google has not disclosed the exact firmware version numbers, but the update is labeled as "sizable" in the source announcement.

## Motorola widens Android 17 reach to Razr Ultra 2026

Motorola announced that its Android 17 rollout is moving beyond flagship phones to include the Razr Ultra (2026) this week. The update brings the same core OS improvements seen on newer Pixel devices, such as refined background task handling and updated security patches.

The Razr Ultra update arrives alongside a broader push to older Motorola models, though the company did not list every device in the statement. Users who opted into the beta channel received the update notification within 24 hours of the public announcement. Motorola's timing suggests a coordinated effort to keep its mid‑range lineup competitive as Android 17 adoption climbs.

## AI Edge Foresight brings offline note‑taking to Mac

Google unveiled EmbeddingGemma 2, a new model designed for on‑device organization, search, and connection of information. To showcase the model, Google released an AI Edge Foresight app for macOS that turns handwritten scribbles into structured notes without sending data to the cloud.

The app runs entirely offline, leveraging the EmbeddingGemma 2 model to parse ink strokes and suggest headings, bullet points, and tags. The demonstration emphasizes privacy and speed, positioning the tool as a competitor to existing cloud‑based note‑taking services. Google has not disclosed pricing or a release schedule beyond the preview, leaving enterprises to watch for a broader rollout.

## What to watch next

The next weeks will reveal whether Samsung patches its Android 17 handsets to support Googlebook "Better Together" features, and whether Google will iterate on the Wallet stacked view based on user feedback. Motorola's continued expansion of Android 17 suggests the OS will soon be the default on most mid‑range Android phones, raising the stakes for app developers to adopt the new APIs. Finally, the performance of EmbeddingGemma 2 on consumer hardware will dictate how quickly offline AI tools move from preview to production. Track firmware version releases for Pixel Buds and the rollout status of the AI Edge Foresight app for clues about Google's broader strategy for on‑device intelligence.
