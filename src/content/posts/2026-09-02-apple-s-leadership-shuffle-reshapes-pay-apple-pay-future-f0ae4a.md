## CEO pay revamp and Cook’s new chairmanship
Apple filed a proxy that lists a $58 million compensation package for incoming CEO John Ternus. The figure combines salary and target stock awards and dwarfs the $22.8 million packages senior executives received in 2016. The filing also outlines Tim Cook’s new agreement as executive chairman, though it stops short of publishing the exact numbers.

The jump in Ternus’s pay signals Apple’s confidence in the hardware chief’s ability to steer the company after Cook’s September 1 departure. At 51, Ternus inherits a business that has leaned heavily on services revenue, a trend that will test his hardware pedigree.

## Jennifer Bailey exits after two decades of Apple Pay growth
Longtime Apple Pay and Wallet vice president Jennifer Bailey announced her retirement effective October. Bailey helped launch Apple Pay in 2014 and expanded the Wallet app to store virtual keys, IDs, and even car access.

Eddy Cue, Apple’s services chief, praised Bailey’s impact, noting that Apple Pay now processes more than $7.5 billion in revenue annually, according to Bloomberg estimates. The service has become a key reason users keep their iPhones, turning a simple tap into a revenue engine.

## A wave of senior exits reshapes Apple’s executive roster
Bailey’s departure joins a broader exodus of Apple veterans in their 60s. General counsel Kate Adams is set to retire this year, and environment chief Lisa Jackson left early in 2026. Former CFO Luca Maestri and longtime marketer Phil Schiller remain in reduced roles, hinting at an approaching end to their tenures.

Other pillars—services chief Eddy Cue, retail head Deirdre O’Brien, and marketing lead Greg Joswiak—have each spent roughly four decades at the company. Their longevity underscores a demographic challenge: a generation of executives who built Apple’s fortunes is now approaching retirement, and many have already amassed substantial wealth.

## What the turnover means for Apple Pay and services revenue
Apple Pay’s $7.5 billion annual haul is more than a line‑item; it anchors the broader services portfolio that now rivals hardware in profit contribution. With Bailey leaving, Apple must ensure continuity in the product roadmap that includes Apple Card, the recently announced device‑upgrade subscription, and the expanding digital‑key ecosystem.

The transition could spur internal reshuffling. Ternus, fresh from a hardware background, recently hired a retired hardware leader for his senior team, suggesting he may prioritize engineering talent in the services arena as well. How Apple balances that with the need for seasoned payments expertise will shape the next iteration of Wallet and its integration with emerging fintech partners.

## What to watch next
Track the succession plan Apple will unveil before Bailey’s October exit. The identity of her replacement will indicate whether Apple leans toward a payments specialist or a broader services strategist. Also monitor the SEC filings for Tim Cook’s executive chairman compensation, as the size of that deal will hint at how Apple values its former CEO’s continued influence. Finally, keep an eye on Apple Pay’s quarterly revenue reports; any slowdown could trigger strategic pivots in the services division under the new leadership.
