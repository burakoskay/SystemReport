## Apple's Desperate Bid to Stay Relevant in a RAM-Scarce World

Apple has launched a 'Upgrade' device leasing program in partnership with Klarna, a Swedish fintech company best known for its 'buy now, pay later' model. But is this new initiative a clever move to keep customers loyal, or a desperate attempt to mitigate the effects of 'RAMageddon', the industry-wide shortage of memory chips that has sent hardware prices soaring?

The answer lies in the program's specifics. For $49.99 per month, customers can lease an iPhone, with an option to upgrade to a new device after 12 months. The catch? You'll need to return the old phone in decent condition to avoid additional fees. Apple is also throwing in some sweet perks, like a free pair of AirPods with the lease of a new iPhone 13 Pro.

But here's the thing: this program is not just about keeping customers happy. It's also a clever way for Apple to offload the risk of declining hardware sales. With RAM prices expected to remain high for the foreseeable future, Apple needs to find ways to incentivize customers to upgrade – and fast. The Upgrade program is just one part of a larger strategy to reduce its exposure to the volatile memory chip market.

## The RAMageddon Effect

The shortage of memory chips is a global problem, but Apple is particularly vulnerable due to its reliance on a small group of suppliers. The company has been struggling to keep up with demand, leading to delays and price hikes for its products. The Upgrade program is a direct response to this crisis, but it's not the only solution Apple is exploring.

According to a report by Bloomberg, Apple is also in talks with several suppliers to secure a steady supply of RAM. The company is said to be considering a range of options, from increasing its own manufacturing capacity to investing in new technologies that can reduce its reliance on traditional memory chips.

## The Competitive Landscape

But Apple is not the only company feeling the pinch of RAMageddon. Its competitors, like Samsung and Google, are also struggling to keep up with demand. In fact, Samsung has been hit particularly hard, with the company's chip sales expected to decline by as much as 20% in the coming quarter.

## What's Next

So what does this mean for Apple and its customers? In the short term, the Upgrade program is a welcome relief for those looking to upgrade their devices without breaking the bank. But in the long term, it's a sign of a deeper problem – one that Apple needs to address if it wants to remain a leader in the tech industry.

As for what's next, look out for Apple's continued efforts to mitigate the effects of RAMageddon. With the company's stock price under pressure, it needs to find ways to reduce its exposure to the volatile memory chip market. Will the Upgrade program be enough to save the day? Only time will tell.

**What to watch:** Apple's quarterly earnings release in October, which will provide a clearer picture of the company's performance in the face of RAMageddon.
