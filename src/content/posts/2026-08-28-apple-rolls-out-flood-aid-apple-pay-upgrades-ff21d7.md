Apple pledged a cash donation after the deadly floods that swept Nepal and Tibet this week. The company did not disclose the amount, but the gesture lands amid a wave of corporate aid.

The floods left dozens dead and thousands without shelter, prompting a global relief effort. Apple’s contribution joins donations from tech peers and nonprofit groups. By stepping in, Apple reinforces its public‑relations playbook that pairs product launches with charitable headlines.

Apple Pay received four new capabilities and a rollout that expands its footprint to new merchants. The updates aim to tighten the service’s grip on everyday transactions. One of the additions lets users store more loyalty cards directly in the wallet, cutting the need for separate apps.

Another feature speeds up checkout by pre‑authorizing payments for recurring services. A third tweak improves offline transaction handling, a pain point for travelers. The fourth upgrade adds a shortcut for transit fares in select cities. The major expansion now brings Apple Pay to additional point‑of‑sale terminals, widening its acceptance network.

These changes arrive as Apple battles a lawsuit that could reshape its relationship with AI startups. OpenAI, along with Chang Liu, Tang Tan, and io Products, filed a petition asking a court to dismiss Apple’s trade‑secret misappropriation complaint with prejudice. A dismissal with prejudice would bar Apple from refiling the claim.

Apple alleges that the defendants stole proprietary code related to its generative‑AI tools. OpenAI counters that the allegations rest on vague accusations and that the code in question is publicly available. The petition argues that the lawsuit wastes resources and distracts from innovation.

If the court grants the dismissal, OpenAI would avoid a costly legal battle and could focus on its own product roadmap. Apple would lose a lever to pressure competitors over intellectual‑property boundaries. The outcome could set a precedent for how big platforms enforce trade‑secret claims against fast‑moving AI firms.

Apple announced tax and price updates for apps, in‑app purchases, and subscriptions in four countries. The changes adjust the base price tiers to reflect local tax regimes and currency fluctuations. Developers will see the new pricing reflected in the App Store Connect dashboard within days.

The affected regions include Germany, France, Japan, and Canada, according to Apple’s developer blog. The move aligns Apple’s pricing structure with recent regulatory pressure to increase price transparency. Critics argue that the adjustments could push subscription costs higher for consumers in those markets.

For developers, the timing coincides with the upcoming iOS 27 release, which adds an auto‑change feature to the Passwords app. The new capability can detect weak passwords and replace them without user interaction. While the feature promises convenience, it also raises questions about privacy safeguards and fallback mechanisms.

Apple’s recent hardware announcements, such as the rumored new Apple TV 4K and the M6 Mac mini, have been overshadowed by these service‑focused moves. The company appears to be leveraging incremental software upgrades to maintain ecosystem momentum while navigating legal and public‑relations challenges.

What to watch: The court’s decision on OpenAI’s petition will signal how aggressively Apple will pursue trade‑secret claims. Apple Pay’s new features will be measured by adoption rates in the newly supported merchants. Finally, the impact of the App Store price changes on subscription revenue will become clear in the next quarterly earnings report.
