## Office 2019/2021 for Mac now view-only

Microsoft changed Office 2019 and Office 2021 for Mac so that files saved with those perpetual-license versions can only be opened in view-only mode after the latest update. This change was first reported on a consumer-rights wiki that tracks Microsoft's licensing tweaks.

The software still launches, but when trying to type or format a slide, the app refuses and displays a notice that the document is view-only. No workaround is offered within the product itself; the only path forward is to subscribe to Microsoft 365 or move the file to a supported online editor. For professionals who have been editing PowerPoint decks on a Mac for years, the shift feels like a sudden lock-out.

The view-only limitation affects files created in Office 2019 and 2021 for Mac, but not files created in other versions of Office or Microsoft 365. This has led to confusion among users who are unsure about the compatibility of their files.

## Community reaction

The change has sparked significant backlash, with 583 points and 189 comments on a Hacker News thread discussing the issue. This reaction suggests that Microsoft underestimated the impact on its power users. Many users have expressed frustration and disappointment with the change, citing concerns about the loss of functionality and the limitations imposed on their workflow.

The backlash has also raised questions about Microsoft's licensing strategy and the company's efforts to push users towards subscription-based models. Some users have argued that the change is an attempt to force them into Microsoft 365, while others have expressed concerns about the impact on their businesses.

## Technical mechanics

When a user attempts to edit a file created in Office 2019 or 2021 for Mac, the software checks the file's metadata to determine if it is a valid, editable document. If the file was created in a perpetual-license version of Office, the software will only allow view-only access. This change is likely due to Microsoft's efforts to enforce its licensing agreements and encourage users to upgrade to a subscription-based model.

The technical mechanics behind the change are complex and involve several components, including the Office software, Microsoft's licensing servers, and the file metadata. However, the end result is clear: users who have files created in Office 2019 or 2021 for Mac are now limited to view-only access.
