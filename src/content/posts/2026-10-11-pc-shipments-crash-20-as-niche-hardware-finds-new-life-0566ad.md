## The PC market’s sharpest tumble in years

Global PC shipments plunged 20.1 percent last quarter, the steepest decline since Q1 2023. The contraction squeezes notebook margins, forces manufacturers to rethink pricing, and nudges a segment of engineers and creators toward alternative hardware experiments.

The data comes from a recent industry report that warned the current decline may be just the beginning of a new downward cycle. Analysts link the slide to rising component costs and lingering pandemic‑era inventory imbalances. As OEMs grapple with weaker demand, they are poised to raise notebook prices, a move that could further suppress sales.

## Mechanical keyboards become a quiet rebellion

Keychron, the Taiwanese startup that burst onto the scene with a 2017 Kickstarter, is now a mainstay for developers who crave tactile feedback without paying flagship‑tier premiums. Its flagship K2 model sits alongside a niche lineup that includes an all‑wood‑body variant, catering to users who value both ergonomics and aesthetics.

The company’s growth illustrates how a shrinking PC market can buoy peripheral niches. When notebook shipments stall, power users often upgrade peripherals rather than replace whole systems. Keychron’s blend of affordable pricing and distinctive design has turned its keyboards into a low‑cost upgrade path for those stuck with aging laptops.

Beyond price, the keyboards offer a tactile experience that many find missing from thin‑and‑light notebooks. The clack of a well‑tuned switch can make a modest laptop feel like a workstation, extending the useful life of hardware that would otherwise be discarded.

## Retro upcycling thrives amid hardware scarcity

In Spain’s Museo de Historia de la Computación, a striking Cray‑1 replica assembled from thirty “obsolete” Mac Minis draws crowds. The project merges vintage supercomputer aesthetics with modern, readily available components, delivering a functional homage without the original’s prohibitive cost.

The exhibit underscores a broader trend: hobbyists repurposing surplus or end‑of‑life devices to create nostalgic experiences. By salvaging Mac Minis that would otherwise collect dust, the museum showcases stylish upcycling with a retro flair, proving that scarcity can spark creativity.

For engineers facing a market where new notebooks are pricier and less plentiful, such projects offer a hands‑on alternative. Building a replica or customizing a keyboard becomes a way to stay engaged with hardware without relying on a steady stream of new devices.

## Notebook outlook: higher prices, weaker demand

TrendForce projects that global notebook shipments could decline by a high‑single‑digit percentage in 2027, depending on how manufacturers respond to rising component costs. The forecast signals that the current 20.1 percent slump may be a prelude to a longer‑term contraction.

If OEMs choose to pass cost pressures onto consumers, notebook price points could climb beyond the comfort zone of many professional users. Higher prices risk accelerating the shift toward cloud‑based workstations and peripheral‑centric upgrades, reinforcing the relevance of devices like Keychron keyboards.

Conversely, a sustained decline might push manufacturers to explore modular designs, allowing users to refresh key components without buying an entirely new machine. Such a move would align with the upcycling ethos seen in the Cray‑1 replica and could soften the impact of weaker demand.

## What to watch next

The next quarter’s shipment figures will reveal whether the 20.1 percent drop was an anomaly or the start of a deeper cycle. Track how major OEMs price their 2025‑2026 notebook lines and whether they introduce modular upgrade options. Keep an eye on hobbyist forums for spikes in mechanical‑keyboard sales and retro‑build projects, as these micro‑trends often foreshadow broader shifts in consumer hardware strategy.
