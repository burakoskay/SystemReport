## Android Auto gets a UI facelift

The Android Auto display can now be rearranged to match a driver’s preferred workflow. A new guide walks users through swapping tile order, toggling light and dark themes, and pinning frequently used shortcuts. The changes stay within the existing infotainment framework, so no aftermarket hardware is required.

The guide, published by Engadget, emphasizes that the steps are reversible and do not affect core navigation functions. It also notes that the customization options are consistent across most recent head‑unit firmware versions. For fleet managers, the ability to standardize a layout across dozens of vehicles could reduce training time.

## Android app deals sweeten the Play Store

9to5Google listed a dozen Android apps and games on discount, ranging from indie titles to productivity tools. Out There: Ω Edition, Tokaido, and Eight‑Minute Empire all dropped below their regular price points. The roundup also highlighted hardware bundles: Nothing’s CMF Watch 3 Pro sold for $59, the Galaxy Z Fold 8/Ultra received up to $570 off, and Nothing Buds Neo were priced at $20.

These promotions arrive as Google pushes more users toward its subscription services. By lowering the barrier to entry for premium apps, the Play Store can capture higher engagement metrics. The deals are time‑limited, with most expiring within the next week.

## Redmagic OS 12 pushes Android 17 toward global release

Redmagic announced that its OS 12 build, based on Android 17, will soon roll out to a subset of its devices. The exact rollout schedule remains unclear, but the company confirmed that the upgrade will land on its latest flagship models. According to 9to5Google, the update brings performance tweaks and a refreshed UI that mirrors Google’s own Android 17 design language.

The move signals that Android 17 is edging closer to a worldwide launch. Redmagic’s early adoption provides a testing ground for developers targeting the new API level. It also puts pressure on other OEMs to accelerate their own Android 17 roadmaps, lest they fall behind in feature parity.

## Pixel Tag expands Find Hub capabilities

Google’s upcoming Pixel Tag tracker will ship with a refreshed Find Hub app that adds “Left Behind” and “Ring your phone” alerts. The new options, revealed in an app update preview, let users set geofenced reminders that trigger when the tag is out of Bluetooth range. A “Ring your phone” command will emit a loud tone from the paired device, helping locate misplaced phones in noisy environments.

The feature set arrives just weeks before the Pixel Tag’s public launch, suggesting Google is fine‑tuning the user experience based on internal testing. By integrating these alerts into the existing Find Hub ecosystem, Google aims to make the Pixel Tag a more compelling alternative to competing Bluetooth trackers.

## OnePlus adopts ColorOS as Android 17 spreads

OnePlus confirmed that the OnePlus 13 will abandon OxygenOS in favor of ColorOS as part of the broader Android 17 rollout. The switch aligns the brand with its parent company’s software strategy and promises a more unified update cadence across devices. 9to5Google reported that additional OnePlus models will receive the ColorOS transition in the coming months.

Critics have long debated the merits of OxygenOS versus ColorOS. The migration may streamline development resources, but it also risks alienating users who preferred OxygenOS’s lighter skin. Early adopters of the OnePlus 13 will be the first to gauge whether the new UI feels like a genuine improvement or an unnecessary overlay.

## What to watch

The next few weeks will reveal whether Android 17 gains a truly global foothold. Track the Redmagic OS 12 rollout, the OnePlus 13 ColorOS migration, and the Pixel Tag launch dates. Play Store deal expiration timelines will also indicate how aggressively Google is using price cuts to drive ecosystem adoption. The convergence of these moves could reshape how developers prioritize Android 17 features and how users experience Android across cars, phones, and wearables.
