A trio of WIRED‑published discount codes for October 2026 cuts prices on Lego sets, NordVPN subscriptions, and Booking.com bookings. The offers force shoppers to confront a new baseline: cheap is expected, not occasional.

WIRED’s list shows a 20% off Lego coupon for the month, a NordVPN code that trims 2‑year plans by up to 77% and adds three free months, and a Booking.com voucher that also delivers 20% off travel bookings. All three promotions appear on the same day, suggesting a coordinated push from coupon aggregators to capture holiday‑season traffic.

## The offers on the table

Lego’s October code promises a flat 20% discount on any set purchased through the official store. The deal also unlocks free shipping for orders over $35, a standard sweetener for online brick buyers. The code is listed without an expiration time beyond the month, implying a short‑term sales spike rather than a permanent price cut.

NordVPN’s coupon is more aggressive. It advertises up to a 77% reduction on a two‑year plan and throws in three extra months at no charge. The discount dwarfs typical seasonal promotions, which usually hover around 30‑40%. The code targets privacy‑conscious users who are willing to lock in a multi‑year commitment for a deep discount.

Booking.com’s voucher mirrors Lego’s 20% off language but applies to travel bookings. The site also highlights “handpicked travel deals,” a phrase that hints at curated inventory rather than a blanket discount. The promo code is tied to the same October window, aligning with peak planning for winter vacations.

## Why shoppers chase coupons now

The proliferation of coupon sites has turned discount hunting into a daily habit for many online buyers. Users compare multiple codes before checkout, treating the process like a micro‑competition for the lowest price. This behavior erodes the perceived value of full‑price listings and forces brands to embed discounts into their pricing models.

Data from e‑commerce analysts shows that coupon‑driven traffic now accounts for a double‑digit share of total site visits for major retailers. When a shopper lands on a product page already armed with a code, the expectation shifts: the price without the coupon feels inflated. Brands that ignore the trend risk losing cart completions to competitors who advertise deeper cuts.

## Brands scramble to stay profitable

Lego’s flat 20% reduction is modest compared to NordVPN’s near‑80% slash, yet both moves signal a willingness to sacrifice short‑term margin for volume. Lego’s strategy leans on its high‑margin, collectible product line, betting that a surge in unit sales will offset the discount. NordVPN, on the other hand, banks on the multi‑year lock‑in to smooth revenue over a longer horizon.

Booking.com’s travel discount follows a similar logic: lower the barrier for booking now, capture revenue that might otherwise be delayed or diverted to rival platforms. The risk is that travelers grow accustomed to 20% off and begin to discount the brand’s perceived value. If the coupon culture continues, companies may need to redesign loyalty programs or introduce tiered pricing that rewards repeat purchases without constant markdowns.

## Regulatory eyes and market backlash

U.S. regulators have begun probing the transparency of online coupon advertising. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) warned that deceptive discount claims could mislead consumers, especially when “original price” figures are inflated. While WIRED’s listings appear straightforward, the broader ecosystem includes sites that exaggerate savings to drive clicks.

European consumer agencies have also issued guidance on “price anchoring,” urging merchants to disclose whether a discount reflects a genuine price drop or a temporary promotion. As coupon usage spikes, brands may face increased scrutiny over how they calculate “regular” prices. Non‑compliance could result in fines or mandatory corrective advertising, adding another cost layer to the discount equation.

## What to watch

The next quarter will reveal whether these October codes translate into sustained traffic or a one‑off surge. Track NordVPN’s renewal rates for the two‑year plan cohort, Lego’s quarterly unit sales, and Booking.com’s booking volume for the holiday season. A dip in any of those metrics could signal that deep discounts are eroding brand equity faster than they boost revenue.
