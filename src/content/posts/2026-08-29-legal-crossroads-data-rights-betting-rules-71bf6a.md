## ICE detains Milo Yiannopoulos in Louisiana
Milo Yiannopoulos landed in ICE custody on Wednesday. The UK‑born far‑right commentator was arrested in Lafayette, Louisiana, and placed in federal detention pending removal.

WIRED reports that immigration officials seized Yiannopoulos after a routine check. The detention follows a warrant issued by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement. The case highlights how immigration enforcement can intersect with high‑profile media figures.

The incident underscores the limited recourse for non‑citizens facing removal. Legal experts note that the process can extend for months. The detention also raises questions about the balance between free speech advocacy and immigration law.

## California data‑access law proves a compliance nightmare
California residents can now demand that companies hand over personal data they have collected. The state law, enacted under the California Consumer Privacy Act, grants a statutory right to access.

WIRED documents that exercising this right remains a burdensome process. Companies often respond with deletion notices rather than the requested data. The mismatch forces users into a costly back‑and‑forth with corporate legal teams.

The enforcement gap reflects a broader tension between consumer privacy ambitions and corporate data‑management practices. Regulators have begun to issue fines for non‑compliance, but the procedural hurdles persist. Analysts warn that the law’s intent may be undermined without streamlined response mechanisms.

## Kalshi cannot sidestep Nevada gambling statutes by rebranding bets as swaps
A federal judge ruled that Kalshi's sports‑betting contracts are not swaps. The decision rejects the platform’s argument that its products fall outside Nevada gambling regulations.

Ars Technica notes that the court emphasized the functional similarity between Kalshi's contracts and traditional bets. By labeling them “swaps,” Kalshi hoped to avoid the state’s gambling licensing requirements. The ruling forces the company to seek a gambling license or halt the offering.

The judgment clarifies the legal boundary between financial derivatives and gambling. It signals that regulators will look beyond nomenclature to the underlying economic activity. Industry observers anticipate a wave of similar challenges as fintech firms blur product categories.

## GOP seeks Supreme Court relief over TV ad pricing dispute
Republican campaign committees filed a petition for emergency review after losing a lower‑court case on television ad rates. The groups argue that the ruling hampers their ability to purchase ad slots ahead of the upcoming election cycle.

Ars Technica reports that the GOP aims for a swift Supreme Court decision before ad buying intensifies next week. The petition frames the issue as a matter of political speech and market fairness. The lower court had ruled that the pricing model violated antitrust principles.

If the Supreme Court grants relief, the parties could resume ad purchases under the contested pricing scheme. A denial would compel the GOP to renegotiate rates or shift to alternative media channels. The outcome may set a precedent for how election‑related advertising is regulated.
