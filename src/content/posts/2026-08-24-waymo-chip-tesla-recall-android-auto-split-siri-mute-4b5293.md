## Waymo’s custom silicon fuels robotaxi push
Waymo rolled out a purpose‑built processor to run its self‑driving stack. The chip sits at the heart of the fleet that the company hopes will dominate robotaxi services.
The announcement arrived in TechCrunch Mobility, which frames the silicon as the linchpin for Waymo’s next generation of autonomous rides. The processor promises lower latency and higher throughput than the off‑the‑shelf GPUs Waymo previously relied on. Engineers will see the difference the moment a vehicle negotiates a busy intersection without a human hand on the wheel.
From a competitive angle, the move mirrors what other players have done: building hardware that matches the demands of perception, planning, and control. Waymo’s decision signals that the company is no longer content to outsource compute. It wants a tighter integration between software and silicon, a classic play for anyone who wants to squeeze every millisecond out of a safety‑critical system.

## Chinese regulators demand Tesla fix nearly 3 million cars
China’s safety watchdog issued a sweeping remediation order to Tesla, covering almost three million vehicles. The regulator flagged a door‑opening defect that could leave occupants trapped after a crash.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
The directive forces Tesla to issue a software update and, where needed, a mechanical repair to ensure doors release as designed. The scale of the recall dwarfs most regional actions Tesla has faced, and it arrives at a time when the company is courting Chinese buyers with new model variants.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
For Tesla, the cost is not just the logistics of fixing millions of cars. The episode also dents the brand’s reputation for safety compliance in its biggest overseas market. Investors will watch how quickly Tesla can roll out the fix and whether the regulator’s pressure prompts broader quality audits across the lineup.

## Android Auto and Android Automotive are not interchangeable
Android Auto and Android Automotive sound alike, but they live on opposite sides of the in‑car software stack. Android Auto streams a phone’s UI to the dashboard, while Android Automotive runs a full operating system directly on the vehicle’s hardware.[^6][^7][^8]
The distinction matters for developers and drivers alike. With Android Auto, the phone remains the source of navigation, music, and voice commands. Android Automotive, by contrast, can host native apps that run without a phone, offering tighter integration with vehicle sensors and controls.[^6]
Manufacturers that adopt Android Automotive gain a more consistent user experience across models, but they also inherit the responsibility of maintaining the OS lifecycle. Android Auto’s reliance on the driver’s phone sidesteps that burden, yet it limits the depth of integration possible with the car’s climate system or instrument cluster.[^6]
Understanding the split helps engineers decide where to invest effort. It also clarifies why some new cars ship with Android Automotive while older models only support Android Auto via a cable or wireless link.

## Siri’s interruptions on CarPlay can be silenced
CarPlay users have complained that Siri pops up mid‑drive, turning a helpful assistant into a distraction. Engadget outlines a simple toggle that mutes Siri’s voice prompts while preserving the ability to summon the assistant when needed.
The fix lives in the iPhone’s Settings under the CarPlay menu. Turning off “Allow Siri When Locked” stops the assistant from speaking automatically when a navigation cue arrives or a message is read aloud. Drivers can still press the voice‑command button to invoke Siri on their own terms.
The change is a modest quality‑of‑life improvement, but it matters in a market where safety regulations increasingly scrutinize in‑vehicle voice interactions. Reducing unsolicited chatter can lower cognitive load, making the driving experience smoother without sacrificing the convenience of voice‑controlled apps.

## What to watch next
Waymo’s chip rollout will be measured by how quickly the company can retrofit its existing fleet and whether the hardware translates into measurable safety gains. Tesla’s Chinese remediation will test the company’s ability to coordinate massive field repairs under regulatory pressure. The adoption curve of Android Automotive versus Android Auto will reveal which integration model manufacturers deem more future‑proof. Finally, Apple’s CarPlay mute option may prompt other platform owners to add similar granular controls, shaping the next wave of in‑car voice UI design.
