## Gemini gets a camera makeover on Android

Google has overhauled the Gemini in‑app camera on Android. The new layout lets users snap a photo and attach it to a prompt in a single tap. The change removes the need to leave the chat, switch apps, or wrestle with a clunky upload flow.

Engadget notes that the redesign feels "slick" because the capture button sits directly beneath the text field, and the preview updates instantly. For developers, the shift means fewer API calls and a tighter feedback loop when training Gemini on visual data. Users who rely on visual prompts—designers, field technicians, or hobbyists—can now iterate faster without juggling separate camera apps.

The move signals Google's intent to make multimodal AI a default part of everyday workflows. By embedding the camera directly into Gemini, Google sidesteps the friction that has kept many Android users on third‑party image generators.

## Fitbit Air finally shows live workouts

A long‑standing gripe among Fitbit Air owners was the inability to see a workout in progress. The device would display only the start and end times, leaving users blind to real‑time metrics.

The latest Google Health update adds an "in‑progress" workout card to the Fitbit Air UI. According to 9to5Google, the card surfaces live heart‑rate, elapsed time, and calorie burn while the user is moving. The fix arrives in the 2026 firmware cycle, the same year the reviewer called the Fitbit Air "one of my favorite products of 2026."

For people who use the Air as a primary workout companion, the change removes a major usability hole. It also demonstrates how Google is willing to patch AI‑adjacent health software when user feedback pinpoints a concrete problem.

## Gemini AI powers Google Workspace for small businesses

Google Workspace now bundles a full suite of Gemini‑driven features aimed at small and micro businesses. The AI tools automate drafting emails, summarizing meeting notes, and generating spreadsheet formulas on the fly.

9to5Mac reports that the rollout includes a promotional discount: 20 % off Business Starter, Standard, and Plus plans for the first six months. The offer is limited to readers of the site, but the underlying message is clear—Google wants AI to become the default productivity assistant for businesses that cannot afford large‑scale automation teams.

Early adopters say the AI suggestions cut the time spent on repetitive tasks by half. The integration also means that a single Gemini model now powers both creative image prompts on Android and business‑level text generation in the cloud.

## DxDiag remains the go‑to Windows DirectX troubleshoot

While Google pushes AI into every corner of its ecosystem, Windows users still lean on a decades‑old utility: DxDiag. Engadget describes DxDiag as a "handy and free resource for troubleshooting DirectX."

The tool generates a plain‑text report that lists driver versions, hardware IDs, and DirectX feature levels. When a game fails to launch or shows graphical glitches, users can copy the DxDiag output into forums or support tickets. The simplicity of the utility contrasts sharply with the layered AI experiences Google is rolling out.

Despite its age, DxDiag has not been replaced by a more polished UI. Its continued relevance underscores a broader truth—core system diagnostics still rely on straightforward, text‑based tools rather than AI‑driven assistants.

## Securing home networks amid AI‑rich devices

The proliferation of AI‑enabled gadgets—from smart speakers to AI‑enhanced wearables—has expanded the attack surface of home Wi‑Fi. Engadget's guide on blocking unknown devices walks users through identifying rogue MAC addresses in their router admin panel and adding them to a block list.

The article stresses that users should verify each device before blocking, as many IoT products share similar identifiers. By securing the network, homeowners prevent unauthorized access that could compromise the data streams feeding AI services like Gemini.

As AI features become more embedded in everyday hardware, the line between a simple connectivity issue and a privacy breach blurs. A robust block‑list process becomes a prerequisite for safely enjoying the benefits of AI‑powered tools.

## What to watch

Google plans to extend Gemini's multimodal capabilities beyond Android and Workspace later this year, with rumors of a desktop‑focused version circulating in developer forums. Keep an eye on the next Google Health release for deeper integration of live metrics across other wearables. On the Windows side, watch for any updates to DxDiag that might add AI‑driven diagnostics—though none have been announced. Finally, monitor router firmware updates that incorporate automated rogue‑device detection, a feature that could become essential as AI‑enabled hardware proliferates.
