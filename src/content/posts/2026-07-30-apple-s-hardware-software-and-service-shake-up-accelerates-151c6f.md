Apple is swapping out core components, reshuffling services and teasing a new OS version, all while a major game reappears on its phones in Brazil.

The iPhone 18 Pro may ditch Qualcomm’s modem for an Apple‑designed C2 chip, Apple launched a new Upgrade service this week and ended the iPhone Upgrade Program[^6][^7][^8], iOS 27 is in beta for a fall launch, and Fortnite returned to the iPhone via the Epic Games Store in Brazil, according to 9to5Mac.

## Apple’s modem gamble with the iPhone 18 Pro

A report on 9to5Mac says Apple is planning a major transition away from Qualcomm modems in the upcoming iPhone 18 Pro. The report calls the new component the C2 modem and labels it an Apple‑designed solution[^1][^2][^3]. Replacing Qualcomm could give Apple tighter control over radio performance and supply chain costs. It also means the iPhone 18 Pro will rely on silicon that Apple has built in‑house.

The move follows Apple’s pattern of internalizing components that were once outsourced. The iPhone 18 Pro will be the first flagship to ship with the C2, according to the same source. If the C2 meets expectations, Apple may roll it out to lower‑tier iPhones next year. Critics note that Qualcomm’s 5G portfolio still leads in global coverage, so Apple will need to match that breadth.

## Home product overhaul: the long‑awaited push

Bloomberg, cited by 9to5Mac, confirmed that Apple is about to launch a major Home product overhaul. The report says the push includes devices the author has wanted for years. Rumors point to a new smart speaker, an upgraded hub and tighter integration with Matter standards.

Apple’s Home ecosystem has lagged behind competitors that launched dedicated hubs earlier. The upcoming lineup could finally give developers a stable platform for automations. The report does not list pricing, but expects the devices to hit stores soon. If Apple delivers a compelling hub, it could force other smart‑home vendors to rethink their strategies.

## Upgrade services: new model, old program dies

Apple introduced its Upgrade service this week, according to 9to5Mac[^8]. The service lets customers pay a monthly fee to receive the latest iPhone on a regular schedule. At the same time, Apple announced the discontinuation of the iPhone Upgrade Program[^6][^7][^8].

Both offerings let users avoid a large upfront cost, but the new service bundles hardware and AppleCare together. The iPhone Upgrade Program, which required a separate AppleCare purchase, will cease as existing contracts expire. The shift simplifies the upgrade path but also ties users more tightly to Apple’s financing ecosystem.

Industry analysts see the change as a way to boost hardware revenue while reducing churn. The new model may attract users who were hesitant about the older program’s complexity. However, the monthly cost is higher than a straight purchase for many buyers, so price‑sensitive customers might stay on traditional contracts.

## iOS 27: the beta whispers

iOS 27 is currently in beta and slated for a fall release, 9to5Mac reports. The beta has not yet shown the five rumored features, but sources claim they will arrive before the public launch. The rumored features include a revamped notification system, deeper AI integration, a new privacy dashboard, expanded multitasking gestures and an offline map cache.

Apple has not confirmed any of the features, and the beta remains limited to developers. If the new notification system reduces noise, power users may welcome the change. The AI integration could enable on‑device summarization of messages, a request many have voiced. The privacy dashboard would extend the current tracking transparency tools.

## Ecosystem friction: Fortnite’s Brazil comeback

Fortnite is back on the iPhone in Brazil, launching today via the Epic Games Store, 9to5Mac says. The launch follows a nine‑step process that Epic Games calls excessive. Epic is publicly criticizing the process as a barrier for players.

Brazil’s market represents a sizable portion of mobile gamers, so the return could boost Fortnite’s revenue there. The nine‑step flow includes account creation, device verification, and a separate payment link. Critics argue the steps undermine the seamless experience iOS users expect.

## What to watch

Watch Apple’s next hardware event for a formal C2 modem reveal and the first Home device prototypes. Track the adoption rate of the new Upgrade service as the iPhone Upgrade Program phases out. Monitor iOS 27 beta builds for the five rumored features and note any developer feedback. Finally, keep an eye on Epic Games’ response to the Brazilian rollout and whether Apple adjusts its app‑distribution policies in response.
