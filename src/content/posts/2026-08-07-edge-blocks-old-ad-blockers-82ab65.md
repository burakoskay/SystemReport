# Microsoft Edge Ends MV2 Support
Microsoft Edge is ending support for the Manifest V2 extensions platform, cutting off uBlock Origin and 57 other extensions. This move aligns with Google Chrome's earlier decision to deprecate MV2, a change that has sparked controversy among developers and users alike.
## Impact on Users
58 extensions on the Edge Add-On Store still use MV2, but only three lack MV3 equivalents. Users can switch to alternative ad blockers or browsers like Opera, which still supports MV2 extensions. However, this may not be a seamless transition for all users, particularly those heavily invested in the ecosystem of MV2 extensions.
## Background and Context
Google Chrome made a similar change earlier this year, citing improved security features and performance as the primary reasons for the shift to MV3. The cutoff date for MV2 support in Microsoft Edge is set for January 2024, giving developers and users a limited timeframe to adapt to the new standard. The decision to end MV2 support reflects a broader industry trend towards enhancing browser security and privacy.
## Alternative Options
Opera still supports MV2 extensions, providing a temporary reprieve for users who are not yet ready to migrate to MV3-compatible ad blockers. Developer concerns and community backlash are growing over MV2 deprecation, with some arguing that the transition period is too short and that MV3's capabilities are not yet on par with those of MV2. As the deadline for MV2 support approaches, the browser landscape is poised for significant changes, with users ultimately having to decide between adapting to new ad blocking solutions or seeking out browsers that continue to support older extensions.
## Industry Context
The move away from MV2 is part of a larger shift in the browser industry towards more secure and efficient extension platforms. MV3 offers several improvements over its predecessor, including better privacy controls and more robust security features. However, the transition has not been without its challenges, particularly for developers who have built extensions based on the older standard. As the industry continues to evolve, browsers will need to balance the need for security and performance with the demands of developers and users who require functional and flexible extension ecosystems.
## Technical Mechanics
Under the hood, MV3 introduces several key changes that enhance security and performance. One of the main differences between MV2 and MV3 is the way extensions interact with web pages. MV3 extensions are designed to be more sandboxed, reducing the risk of malicious extensions compromising user data. While these changes are beneficial for security, they also require developers to rethink how their extensions are designed and implemented, which can be a significant undertaking.
## History of MV2 and MV3
The development of MV2 and MV3 has been a long and complex process, with both platforms having their own strengths and weaknesses. MV2 was initially introduced as a way to provide developers with more flexibility and control over their extensions, but it has since been criticized for its security vulnerabilities. MV3, on the other hand, has been designed with security and performance in mind, but it has also been met with resistance from some developers who are concerned about the limitations it imposes.
## Downstream Implications
The deprecation of MV2 support in Microsoft Edge will have downstream implications for both developers and users. For developers, the shift to MV3 will require significant updates to their extensions, potentially leading to delays and additional costs. For users, the change may result in a period of adjustment as they adapt to new ad blocking solutions or browsers. However, the long-term benefits of improved security and performance are likely to outweigh the short-term inconveniences, leading to a more robust and secure browser ecosystem.
## Broader Industry Trends
The move away from MV2 is part of a larger trend in the tech industry towards greater emphasis on security and privacy. As users become increasingly aware of the importance of protecting their personal data, browsers and other software providers are responding by implementing more robust security measures. This shift is likely to continue in the coming years, with browsers and other software providers continuing to evolve and adapt to the changing needs of their users.
