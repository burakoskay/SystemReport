Ambrosia Energy will pour 10 GWh of lithium‑ion cells into a new Texas plant, a move that could reshape power for off‑grid data centers. The factory promises round‑the‑clock clean energy by pairing batteries with solar arrays, a combination that could reduce reliance on diesel generators.

The startup announced the Texas site in a brief press release, citing a need for large‑scale storage to keep data centers online when the sun sets. A 10 GWh capacity translates to enough energy to run a modest data hub for months without recharging. The project also marks the first dedicated battery line aimed at off‑grid computing workloads, a niche that has grown as edge AI workloads migrate away from traditional cloud zones.

## Formlabs' Form Studio 1 Lands on Desks

Formlabs dropped the Form Studio 1 at a price point that forces hobbyists to reconsider desktop manufacturing. The $699 printer fits on a typical office desk and claims a reduced odor footprint compared with larger resin machines.

The machine uses a low‑volume vat and a 405 nm laser to cure photopolymer layers. Early reviewers note that the smell, while still present, is only a faint whiff rather than the acrid plume of older models. The modest footprint and price suggest Formlabs is targeting small studios that need rapid prototyping without allocating a dedicated lab.

## Breville Eye Q Toasting Tries Optical Precision

Breville's Eye Q toaster adds a sensor array to a kitchen staple, measuring the darkness of each slice before the heating elements finish the cycle. The optical sensors feed a microcontroller that adjusts timing in real time.

In practice, the tech feels gimmicky. The toast lands slightly uneven, and the browning curve still misses the mark on thicker slices. Reviewers appreciate the novelty but agree the core toasting performance lags behind Breville's more conventional models.

## JackRabbit MG Cargo Bike Redefines Urban Hauling

JackRabbit's MG cargo bike arrives as a compact, plucky alternative for city dwellers who need to move gear without a truck. The frame folds into a narrow profile, yet the motor still delivers enough torque to climb steep streets with a full load.

Riding the MG feels playful; the geometry keeps the bike nimble, and the electric assist smooths out the effort of hauling groceries or tools. The bike's small wheels and short wheelbase make it easy to park in tight spaces, a clear advantage over bulkier cargo e‑bikes.

## Nothing (1) Pro Headphones Hit Studio Quality

Nothing's latest headphones, the (1) Pro, aim for studio‑grade sound while keeping a minimalist aesthetic. The ear cups house balanced‑armature drivers and a detachable cable, and the EQ settings can be tweaked via a companion app.

Sound quality lives up to the hype: a clean mids range, tight bass, and a spacious soundstage that rivals higher‑priced reference models. Comfort scores high thanks to memory‑foam cushions, and the adjustable EQ lets users fine‑tune the response for mixing or casual listening.

## What to Watch

Ambrosia's Texas plant will break ground later this year, and its first battery shipments are slated for early 2028. Track the factory's ramp‑up schedule and any partnership announcements with edge‑data providers. On the consumer side, watch how Formlabs' pricing affects desktop resin adoption, whether Breville refines its sensor algorithm, if JackRabbit scales the MG into a broader lineup, and how Nothing's EQ app evolves with firmware updates. Each of these moves will reveal whether novelty translates into lasting market traction.
