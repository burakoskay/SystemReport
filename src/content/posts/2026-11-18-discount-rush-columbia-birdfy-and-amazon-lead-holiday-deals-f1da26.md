Deal hunters are already sprinting for the season's deepest cuts.

WIRED reports that Columbia is pushing a 15% discount across jackets, outdoor gear, and apparel throughout November 2026, supplementing the offer with student discounts and free shipping. The retailer's promotion targets adventure‑seeking consumers who already budget for high‑performance gear. By anchoring the discount to a specific month, Columbia forces shoppers to align purchase timing with the promotion window, a tactic that squeezes out casual browsers.

The Columbia code appears on multiple coupon aggregators, confirming its active status. The discount applies site‑wide, not just to clearance items, which suggests the brand is willing to thin margins to clear inventory before winter peaks. For customers, the deal translates to immediate savings on items that typically carry premium price tags.

Birdfy, a maker of smart pet feeders and companion cameras, has opened a 15% site‑wide discount that coexists with deeper cuts of up to 40% on select feeders, camera kits, and accessories. WIRED’s verification of the codes underscores their legitimacy, a rarity in the crowded pet‑tech coupon space. The tiered discount structure incentivizes bulk purchases of higher‑margin accessories while still offering a baseline reduction for first‑time buyers.

The 40% markdown on flagship smart feeders is especially striking given the product’s usual price bracket of $150‑$250. Birdfy’s move signals a willingness to sacrifice short‑term profit to expand its installed base ahead of the holiday gifting season. For tech‑savvy pet owners, the deal removes a common barrier to entry: the perceived cost of a connected feeding system.

Amazon has begun seeding early Black Friday deals ahead of its official November 19 kickoff. The e‑commerce giant’s strategy, outlined in WIRED’s early‑deal roundup, encourages shoppers to stock up now rather than wait for the traditional door‑buster rush. By front‑loading discounts, Amazon aims to capture budget‑constrained consumers before they drift to competing platforms.

The early‑deal slate includes a mix of electronics, home goods, and subscription offers, each marked with a limited‑time price cut. Amazon’s approach leverages its massive logistics network to sustain inventory levels while testing price elasticity across categories. Shoppers who act early lock in savings, while Amazon gathers data on which products generate the most traffic before the main event.

These three discount campaigns converge to amplify a broader shift in holiday retail: the erosion of a clear “deal day.” Consumers now expect a rolling wave of promotions rather than a single peak. Brands like Columbia and Birdfy are using limited‑time codes to create urgency, while Amazon spreads its price cuts across weeks to smooth demand.

Historically, Black Friday began as a single‑day price slash that retailers used to clear excess stock. Over the past decade, the model has fragmented into pre‑Black Friday teasers, Cyber Monday extensions, and now, continuous discount windows. The Columbia and Birdfy codes illustrate how niche brands adopt the same temporal tactics once reserved for large retailers.

What to watch: Columbia will reveal whether its November discount lifts Q4 revenue in its earnings call later this year. Birdfy’s next product launch may test if the discount strategy sustains long‑term growth or merely spikes short‑term sales. Amazon’s post‑Black Friday performance will indicate if early‑deal cannibalization harms or helps its overall holiday margin.
