## A New Revenue Stream for Partiful

Partiful is getting into the ticketing business, allowing users to buy and sell tickets directly within the app. This move marks a significant shift for the social event planner, which has previously focused on connecting people and events.

The company is taking a page from the playbook of event planning apps like GRUP, an open-source RSVP app built with Flutter and Supabase. GRUP allows users to create recurring events, invite friends, and track RSVPs, all within a simple and user-friendly interface. GRUP's focus on simplicity and group events has gained traction among users looking for effective event planning solutions.

## How It Works

Partiful's ticketing feature will enable users to purchase tickets to events without leaving the app. The company has not disclosed the specifics of its ticketing platform, including fees or partnerships with event organizers. 

This development could put Partiful in competition with established ticketing platforms like Eventbrite. Eventbrite has long been a dominant player in the ticketing space, but Partiful's integration with its existing user base could give it an edge. Eventbrite's fees range from 2% + $0.30 per ticket for its basic plan to 15% + $0.30 per ticket for its premium plan. If Partiful can offer competitive pricing, it may attract event organizers and users away from Eventbrite.

## History of Event Ticketing

The event ticketing industry has a long history, dating back to the 1970s. In the early days, ticketing was a manual process, with event organizers selling tickets in person or by mail. The introduction of computerized ticketing systems in the 1980s revolutionized the industry, making it easier for event organizers to manage ticket sales. The rise of online ticketing platforms in the 1990s and 2000s further transformed the industry, making it possible for event organizers to sell tickets online and track sales in real-time.
