## Orkut's Comeback Push Targets AI Content

The founder of Orkut announced a plan to resurrect the early‑2000s social network. The revival will focus on limiting algorithmic feeds and AI‑generated posts. The founder argues that opaque recommendation engines erode authentic conversation. The new Orkut will surface posts chronologically and flag content that appears synthetic. The move pits the resurrected platform against the dominant AI‑driven models that power most modern feeds.

The announcement arrived alongside a broader critique of algorithmic curation. The founder said that users are tired of “engagement loops” that prioritize clicks over community. By stripping away AI‑curated timelines, the revived Orkut hopes to attract users who value transparency. The proposal does not include a pricing model or a timeline for launch. It does, however, signal a rare public pushback from a social‑media pioneer against the AI status quo.

## Publishing Houses Lean on LLMs, Staff Push Back

Employees at three major publishing houses told WIRED that large language models now draft publicity blurbs, cover‑art concepts, back‑cover copy, and internal emails. Executives have asked junior staff to experiment with the technology and champion its benefits. The workers describe a workflow where an AI generates a first draft, and a human editor refines it.

The staff response ranges from cautious adoption to outright revolt. Some editors report that AI‑generated copy often misses brand tone and requires extensive rewrites. Others claim that the pressure to produce AI‑enhanced content feels like a cost‑cutting measure that threatens creative jobs. The reports note that the push for AI use is not uniform across the houses; some divisions have embraced the tools, while others remain skeptical.

The tension highlights a broader industry dilemma: balancing efficiency gains with the risk of homogenized output. The publishing sector has long relied on human judgment to shape narrative voice. Introducing LLMs threatens that tradition, and the current revolt suggests that the trade‑off is not yet accepted.

## TV Drama Mirrors Real AI Fears

The 26th season premiere of the procedural legal drama "Law & Order" depicted AI CEOs as power‑mad and unaccountable. The episode framed a fictional tech mogul who used an autonomous decision‑making system to manipulate markets and evade regulation. The show’s writers positioned the AI leader as a villain whose influence eclipsed traditional corporate governance.

Critics note that the episode reflects growing public anxiety about unchecked AI authority. While the series is scripted, its portrayal aligns with real‑world concerns raised by policymakers and workers alike. The narrative does not claim to predict any specific case, but it amplifies the perception that AI could concentrate power in the hands of a few technocrats.

The dramatization arrives as lawmakers debate transparency requirements for AI systems. By casting AI CEOs as antagonists, the show adds cultural weight to the technical debate. It also underscores how entertainment media can shape public understanding of emerging technology risks.

## Netflix Packages Crypto Scandal as AI Narrative

Netflix released a trailer for "The Altruists," a miniseries chronicling the rise and fall of the cryptocurrency exchange FTX. The preview positions the story within a broader critique of unchecked innovation. While the series focuses on financial fraud, the marketing emphasizes the role of algorithmic trading and automated decision‑making in the collapse.

The trailer shows founders leveraging sophisticated software to attract investors, then scrambling to cover losses with opaque transactions. The visual language hints at a world where code replaces human oversight, echoing concerns raised in the publishing and social‑media sectors. Although the series does not claim to be about AI per se, its framing suggests that algorithmic opacity contributed to the disaster.

The Netflix push reflects a trend where streaming platforms mine recent tech scandals for dramatic material. By tying the FTX saga to broader questions about algorithmic control, the series may influence audience perception of AI’s role in financial systems.

## What to Watch

The next few months will test whether Orkut’s anti‑algorithm stance can attract a sustainable user base. Publishers will likely see more union or employee‑led actions demanding clearer AI policies. Lawmakers may cite the "Law & Order" episode when drafting AI accountability bills. Netflix’s miniseries could spur renewed scrutiny of algorithmic trading practices. Tracking user growth on the revived Orkut, staff turnover at the publishing houses, legislative proposals on AI transparency, and viewership metrics for "The Altruists" will reveal how cultural pushback translates into concrete change.
