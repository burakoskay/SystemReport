## A second wave of hardware on the calendar
Apple will unveil a second batch of products in the final week of October, adding to the October 13 event already on the calendar. The timing suggests Apple wants to stretch media attention across the month.

MacRumors reports that the company plans a separate unveiling after the October 13 keynote. The later showcase will likely feature devices that did not fit the first slot. Apple has not disclosed which products will appear, but the pattern mirrors past years when surprise hardware slipped into a follow‑up event.

## OpenAI trade‑secrets fight heats up
Apple has turned the tables on OpenAI in a high‑stakes trade‑secrets lawsuit. The tech giant accused the AI startup of filing arguments that exceed what court rules allow.

OpenAI had earlier claimed Apple added new evidence improperly. Apple’s rebuttal now frames OpenAI’s response as a procedural overreach. The back‑and‑forth underscores how fiercely both companies guard their intellectual property.

## iPhone Duo gets developer love while Safari watches Chrome
Apple opened its App Store to iPhone Duo‑ready apps built with Xcode 27.1. The move invites developers to tailor games and utilities for the upcoming dual‑screen handset.

At the same time, Apple is reportedly preparing to track when Google promotes Chrome or the Google app inside Safari. The data will show how users react to those promotions. If Apple begins measuring cross‑browser clicks, the analytics could influence future ad‑tech negotiations.

## macOS 27.2 hints at a touchscreen MacBook Pro
macOS 27.2 includes new gesture support that only a touchscreen could exploit. The update adds APIs for swipe and pinch actions that map to a glass panel.

Analysts interpret the changes as groundwork for a touchscreen MacBook Pro that could ship this month. Apple has not confirmed a touch‑enabled laptop, but the software clues are hard to ignore. A touchscreen Mac would be a radical shift for a brand that has long championed the keyboard‑first notebook.

## Service tweaks and wearables get subtle upgrades
Apple Music appears to be rolling out a new logo, first spotted in iOS 27 beta 3. The redesign is subtle, but it signals a visual refresh for the streaming service.

Apple TV confirmed that a series originally billed as limited will return for a second season “by popular demand.” The decision shows the platform’s willingness to extend narratives that resonate with subscribers.

The Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 arrive with faster processors and new health sensors. The upgrades promise better performance, but day‑to‑day use feels familiar unless users enable the hidden features. Automatic Shazam recognition and expanded health metrics are the most noticeable additions.

## What to watch next
Track the final‑week October event for concrete product announcements and note which devices slip into the lineup. Monitor the court filings for any new procedural moves from Apple or OpenAI, as they could set precedent for AI‑related IP battles. Keep an eye on developer feedback for iPhone Duo and on any Safari telemetry that Apple might release. Finally, watch macOS 27.2 updates for additional touch‑gesture APIs that could confirm a touchscreen MacBook Pro before the next hardware reveal.
