## Introduction to Email Leaks
Companies are constantly sending sensitive information to 'no reply' emails, and two security researchers have been tracking the issue. By buying cheap domains like noreply.net and deleteduser.com, they've set up email listening services that receive hundreds of emails daily from various corporations.

The researchers' discovery sheds light on a significant problem in the business world. Many companies use 'no reply' emails for automated messages, but they often forget that these emails can still receive messages. As a result, confidential information like passwords, credit card numbers, and personal data is being sent to these unmonitored email addresses.

## The Extent of the Problem
The security researchers found that hundreds of companies are sending them corporate secrets. These companies include well-known brands and small businesses, all of which are unintentionally exposing their sensitive information. The emails received by the researchers contain a wide range of data, from employee personal details to confidential business plans.

The issue is not limited to a specific industry or company size. Both large and small businesses are affected, and the problem seems to be widespread. The researchers' email listening services have been receiving emails for months, and the volume of sensitive information being sent to 'no reply' emails is staggering.

## Technical Mechanics of Email Leaks
The technical mechanics behind the issue are relatively simple. When a company sets up an automated email system, it often uses a 'no reply' email address to prevent recipients from responding to the automated messages. However, these email addresses can still receive emails, and if a company is not monitoring them, sensitive information can be sent to these addresses without being noticed.

The security researchers used a simple technique to track the issue. By buying domains that are commonly used for 'no reply' emails, they were able to set up email listening services that receive emails sent to these addresses. The researchers then analyzed the emails to determine the extent of the problem and identify the companies affected.

## Industry Context and History
The issue of email leaks is not new, but the extent of the problem has not been fully understood until now. In the past, companies have been aware of the risks associated with using 'no reply' emails, but they have not taken adequate measures to prevent sensitive information from being sent to these addresses.

The problem is exacerbated by the fact that many companies are not monitoring their 'no reply' emails. This lack of monitoring allows sensitive information to be sent to these addresses without being noticed, and it can take months or even years for a company to realize that its information has been compromised.

## What to Watch
As the issue of email leaks becomes more widely known, companies will need to take steps to prevent sensitive information from being sent to 'no reply' emails. This may involve implementing better email monitoring systems or using alternative methods for automated messaging. The security researchers' discovery highlights the need for companies to be more vigilant about their email practices and to take measures to protect their sensitive information.

In the coming months, it will be important to watch how companies respond to the issue of email leaks. Will they take steps to improve their email monitoring systems, or will they continue to send sensitive information to 'no reply' emails? The answer to this question will determine the extent to which companies are able to protect their corporate secrets and prevent sensitive information from being compromised.
