## Fee and rollout
Apple is tacking a $5 monthly charge to iPhone Handoff for T‑Mobile subscribers in the United States. The fee appears with the iOS 27 update that ships later this month.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]

Engadget reports that the feature will be unavailable to any other carrier at launch.[^1][^2][^7][^8] Existing T‑Mobile customers will see the charge on their next bill unless they opt out.[^2][^3][^5] Apple has not explained why the service is not offered free or across all networks.

## How Handoff works
Handoff lets an iPhone hand over an active task—such as a call, a web page, or a document—to a nearby Mac or iPad. The handoff is seamless when both devices share the same Apple ID and are on the same Wi‑Fi network.[^2][^5]

A script posted on Hacker News years ago showed how a Mac could place a call through an iPhone running OS X Yosemite and iOS 8. That hack demonstrated the underlying continuity APIs that Apple now packages as a paid feature. The core technology has not changed; the new fee simply monetizes a capability that has been free for a decade.

## T‑Mobile exclusivity
Apple’s decision to limit the launch to T‑Mobile raises questions about carrier bargaining power. T‑Mobile has a history of bundling data‑heavy services for a premium, and this move fits that pattern.[^3]

The exclusivity also means that iPhone users on AT&T, Verizon, or prepaid plans miss out entirely until Apple expands the offering. Those users will either wait for a broader rollout or stick with the free, manual handoff methods that have existed since iOS 8.[^2][^3]

## Ecosystem rivalry
The fee lands in the middle of the ongoing tug‑of‑war between Apple’s and Samsung’s flagship lines. Engadget’s side‑by‑side comparison of the iPhone 18 Pro Max and the Galaxy S26 Ultra reduces the debate to ecosystem loyalty.

Samsung does not charge for similar handoff‑type features on its Android devices, relying on Google’s free Continuity services. Apple’s price point could push power users toward the Android side if the perceived value does not justify the extra cost.

## Apple’s continuity history
Apple introduced the first version of Handoff in iOS 8, pairing it with OS X Yosemite. The feature was marketed as a convenience, not a revenue stream.

In 2013 Apple released the iPhone 5s with a 64‑bit A7 processor and iOS 7. The hardware jump was praised for its forward‑looking architecture, even though the UI overhaul received mixed reactions. That launch showed Apple’s willingness to push technical milestones without attaching a direct fee.

The current $5 charge marks a departure from that philosophy. It signals that Apple now treats continuity as a premium service rather than a free add‑on. The move could foreshadow more monetization of features that were once bundled.[^3]

## What to watch
The next billing cycle will reveal how many T‑Mobile users actually adopt paid Handoff. Apple’s quarterly earnings call should hint at whether the fee meets revenue expectations. Keep an eye on whether Apple opens the feature to other carriers or raises the price beyond $5. Those signals will indicate how aggressively Apple plans to monetize its ecosystem in the future.[^3]
