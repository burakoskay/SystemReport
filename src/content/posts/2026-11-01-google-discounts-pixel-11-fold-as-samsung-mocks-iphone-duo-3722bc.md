Google cut Pixel 11 Fold prices just as Samsung launched a brash ad campaign targeting Apple’s iPhone Duo. The move sharpens a three‑way contest in a market that still struggles with durability and consumer confidence.

The US Google Store began offering Black Friday‑level discounts on the Pixel 11 series and Pixel Watch 5 at the end of October, according to 9to5Google. Samsung’s new ads ridicule the iPhone Duo’s “crackgate” problem while urging shoppers toward the Galaxy Z Fold 8. Meanwhile, Motorola posted a video that confirms a Tandem OLED display for its upcoming Signature 27 flagship, slated for a public reveal next week.

## Google pushes a sturdier foldable with early discounts

TechCrunch’s review described the Pixel 11 Pro Fold as a “sturdy rectangle” amid a crowded foldable segment. Google’s design emphasis on rigidity addresses the fragility complaints that have haunted earlier models. The early discount strategy mirrors retail tactics that aim to clear inventory before the holiday rush.

The discount applies to both the standard Pixel 11 and the Pro Fold variant. 9to5Google noted that the price cuts match typical Black Friday reductions, even though the sale starts in late October. By lowering the entry point, Google hopes to attract users who have been wary of foldable breakage.

## Motorola’s Signature 27 stakes a claim in the premium tier

A new 9to5Google video revealed that the Signature 27 will ship with a Tandem OLED display, a layout that stacks two OLED panels for a seamless visual experience. The video also hinted at other “key specs,” though it stopped short of publishing exact dimensions or processor details.

Motorola confirmed that a full specification reveal will occur next week. The company’s timing places the launch between Google’s discount window and Samsung’s ad blitz, positioning the Signature 27 as a potential alternative for buyers who want a flagship without a foldable hinge.

The inclusion of a Tandem OLED panel suggests Motorola is borrowing from Samsung’s foldable technology while keeping the form factor conventional. This hybrid approach could appeal to consumers who like large screens but distrust moving parts.

## Samsung’s aggressive ad campaign highlights iPhone Duo woes

Samsung’s latest television spots feature a mock‑up of the iPhone Duo’s cracked screen, labeling the incident “crackgate.” The ads directly compare the iPhone Duo’s fragile start to the Galaxy Z Fold 8’s reinforced hinge and Gorilla Glass Victus 2.

The campaign’s tone leaves little room for nuance; it frames the iPhone Duo as a misstep and the Z Fold 8 as the safer choice. By foregrounding durability, Samsung aims to capture the segment of buyers who have been put off by early foldable failures.

Industry observers note that Samsung’s messaging aligns with its broader strategy of defending market share against Apple’s tentative entry into foldables. The ad run coincides with Google’s price cuts, creating a three‑cornered pressure cooker for the foldable niche.

## Market dynamics: competition, durability, and consumer expectations

Foldable smartphones entered the mainstream three years ago, yet durability remains the primary barrier to mass adoption. Early models from multiple vendors suffered hinge failures and screen cracks, prompting manufacturers to reinforce chassis designs.

Google’s emphasis on a “sturdy rectangle” and Samsung’s promotion of a reinforced hinge both address the same pain point. Motorola’s decision to stay with a conventional slab while adopting a Tandem OLED display reflects a different risk calculus: it offers a large, immersive screen without the mechanical complexity that has plagued foldables.

Pricing also plays a pivotal role. The Pixel 11 series historically launched at premium price points, limiting its appeal to early adopters. By offering discounts ahead of the holiday season, Google hopes to broaden its user base and generate volume that can offset lower margins.

Meanwhile, Apple’s iPhone Duo, still in its infancy, has already attracted negative attention for screen integrity issues. Samsung’s direct attack may force Apple to accelerate engineering fixes or reconsider its rollout timeline.

The competitive landscape now features three distinct strategies: Google doubles down on durability and price, Samsung leans on brand confidence and aggressive marketing, and Motorola seeks differentiation through display technology while avoiding foldable mechanics.

## What to watch: upcoming launches and pricing trends

The next week will reveal Motorola’s full Signature 27 specifications, including processor, RAM, and camera details. Those numbers will determine whether the device can compete on performance as well as display innovation.

Google’s discount window closes at the end of October, so sales data will indicate whether price reductions translate into higher adoption rates for foldables. Samsung’s Galaxy Z Fold 8 launch is slated for early November; early reviews will test whether the advertised durability holds up under real‑world use.

Finally, Apple’s response to the “crackgate” narrative will shape the iPhone Duo’s market perception. A firmware fix, a hardware revision, or a PR campaign could mitigate the damage caused by Samsung’s ads.

Tracking these developments will reveal which durability narrative wins: Google’s reinforced fold, Samsung’s reinforced hinge, or Motorola’s display‑first approach. The outcome will set the tone for the next generation of premium smartphones.
