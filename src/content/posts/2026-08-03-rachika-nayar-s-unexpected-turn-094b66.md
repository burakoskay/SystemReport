## Introduction to Heaven Come Crashing
Rachika Nayar's Heaven Come Crashing drops a massive drum and bass beat two minutes and thirty seconds into the title track. This album is almost entirely drum-free, aside from brief moments like the rapid-fire mechanical hi-hats on the second track, 'Tetramorph'.
The title track's drum and bass beat is a stark contrast to her debut record Our Hands Against The Dusk, which has no percussion at all. This change is notable for fans of her debut, who may be surprised by the new direction.
## Musical Background
Heaven Come Crashing is an instrumental epic of desperate longing. The album's composition is reminiscent of her debut, where the focus was on creating a peaceful atmosphere. The addition of percussion in Heaven Come Crashing adds a new layer of depth to her music.
The drum and bass beat in the title track is a highlight of the album. The second track, 'Tetramorph', features rapid-fire mechanical hi-hats, showcasing Nayar's experimentation with new sounds.
## Context and Comparison
Heaven Come Crashing is a more experimental album compared to her debut. Nayar incorporates new sounds and instruments, creating a unique sound. The title track's drum and bass beat is a notable aspect, but not the only one. Other tracks provide a nice contrast to the more energetic moments.
