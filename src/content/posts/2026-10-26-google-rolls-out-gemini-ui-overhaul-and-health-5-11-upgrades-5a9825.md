Google shipped two Android updates that touch daily health tracking and AI chat. Gemini’s side panel now looks like a native Android drawer, while Google Health 5.11 expands Fitbit Air’s sensor suite.

The Gemini rollout follows the Spark‑to‑Task UI shift introduced last month. The new side panel slides from the right, swaps icons for larger tap targets, and groups settings under a single scrollable list. 9to5Google confirmed the changes are live for most Android 13 devices.

## Gemini’s side panel redesign

The refreshed panel replaces the cramped column that debuted with Spark‑to‑Task. Users now tap a thin edge to reveal the drawer. The drawer occupies a third of the screen, leaving room for the underlying app.

Settings have been consolidated into three sections: appearance, shortcuts, and advanced. Each section opens with a clear header and a single toggle per line. The layout mirrors Android’s native settings, reducing the learning curve.

The redesign feels like a quality‑of‑life upgrade rather than a gimmick. Early adopters report fewer accidental taps. The panel also respects system dark mode, which was missing before.

## Google Health 5.11 adds Fitbit Air Health Guardian

Google Health 5.11 began rolling out this week for Fitbit trackers and the Pixel Watch. The update introduces a Health Guardian feature for the Fitbit Air.

Health Guardian monitors heart‑rate spikes and alerts users to prolonged irregularities. The alert appears as a banner on the watch and as a notification in the app. 9to5Google notes that the feature leverages the Air’s new optical sensor firmware.

Sleep tracking received a noticeable boost. The algorithm now distinguishes light, deep, and REM phases with finer granularity. Users can see a nightly sleep score that reflects both duration and quality.

## Expanded activity detection across Fitbit Air and Pixel Watch

Version 5.11 also expands automatic activity detection to more than 100 motions. The list now includes rowing, stair climbing, and gardening.

Detection runs on‑device, which preserves battery life. The Pixel Watch reports a 5‑hour average runtime after the update, according to the rollout notes.

Fitbit Air users see the same activity list without needing a separate app. The phone app now aggregates detected workouts into a single timeline view.

## Why Google is tightening its health stack

Google’s health push follows the 2021 Fitbit acquisition. The company has been merging Fitbit data into Google Health ever since.

Apple dominates the wearable health market with its tightly integrated ecosystem. Google’s recent updates aim to close the feature gap without forcing users onto a new hardware platform.

The side‑panel redesign signals that Google views Gemini as a daily‑use assistant, not a novelty. By aligning Gemini’s UI with Android conventions, Google reduces friction for power users who already juggle multiple apps.

Privacy‑by‑design remains a question mark. Google Health now stores more granular activity data on the device, but the company still syncs to the cloud for backup. Analysts warn that increased data collection could attract regulator scrutiny.

## What to watch next

The next rollout wave will target Android 12 devices still on the older Gemini UI. Watch for a follow‑up patch that adds voice shortcuts to the side panel. On the health side, Google has hinted at a future “Health Insights” dashboard that will surface trends across Fitbit and Pixel Watch data. Tracking the adoption rate of Health Guardian alerts will reveal whether users trust Google’s automated health warnings. Stay tuned for the next version number, which should arrive before the holiday season.
