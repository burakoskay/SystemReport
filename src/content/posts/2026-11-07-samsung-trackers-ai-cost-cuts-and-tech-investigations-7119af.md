Samsung released the third‑generation SmartTag for Galaxy users on its online store. The device ships in two colors and starts at $29, according to 9to5Google.[^1][^4][^5][^6][^7][^8] The price places the tracker alongside earlier models while adding a larger battery and a louder speaker.

The launch arrives as sodium‑ion batteries are closing the cost gap with lithium‑iron‑phosphate cells. PV Magazine reports that supply agreements now cover 70 GWh of sodium‑ion capacity, a milestone that could reshape energy‑storage pricing.

## New hardware for everyday tracking

Samsung’s SmartTag 3 expands the ecosystem of Bluetooth‑based item locators that integrate with Galaxy phones. The tracker supports the Find My Mobile network[^1][^2][^4] and can trigger a ringtone from up to 120 meters away. The four‑pack bundle offers the same functionality at a reduced per‑unit cost, a typical strategy for accessory pricing.[^1][^2][^4][^3][^5][^6][^7]

The device’s battery is rated for three years of continuous use, according to the product page. Samsung markets the longer life as a response to consumer complaints about frequent battery replacements in earlier generations.

## Battery chemistry reaches parity

PV Magazine’s coverage of sodium‑ion technology notes that manufacturers have secured supply agreements for 70 GWh of cells. Those contracts bring the per‑kilowatt‑hour price within striking distance of LFP chemistry, which has dominated low‑cost markets.

Industry analysts cite the parity as a potential catalyst for broader adoption in electric‑vehicle fleets and stationary storage. The article does not claim that sodium‑ion will replace LFP, only that cost is no longer the primary barrier.

## Open‑source tools and VM security

Mitchell Hashimoto published libghostty 1.0 on his personal site, announcing a new Go library for building terminal user interfaces. The blog post provides a minimal example and links to the GitHub repository. No usage statistics are included, but the release follows a pattern of incremental improvements to Hashimoto’s open‑source portfolio.

Separately, Pwn.ai released a second installment of its Firecracker VM escape research, titled *PwnEscape Part 2*. The post details a privilege‑escalation chain that leverages a mis‑configured virtual device driver. The researchers do not claim a fully reliable exploit, but they demonstrate a path that could inform future hardening efforts.

## AI research acceleration and cost efficiency

Planned‑Obsolescence published an essay arguing that AI is dramatically accelerating AI research. The author points to a feedback loop where model improvements enable faster experimentation, which in turn yields more capable models.

A Hightouch blog entry describes a Jev that can ask the Opus language model for assistance, delivering the same accuracy at four times lower cost. The cost reduction stems from off‑loading compute‑intensive steps to a smaller, specialized model before invoking Opus. The post includes benchmark numbers but does not disclose the exact workload.

## Regulatory pressure and market valuations

The Washington Post reported that House Democrats launched congressional investigations into SpaceXAI, Meta and Apple. The committees cited concerns about data privacy, market dominance and alleged coordination with the current administration. The investigations are in early stages and no subpoenas have been issued yet.

Ars Technica covered a series of court decisions that left Kalshi’s attempts to block state gambling laws unsuccessful. Judges declined to grant preliminary injunctions, adding to Kalshi’s mounting legal setbacks.

Epyhia noted that buyers in Anthropic’s upcoming IPO are seeing valuations as low as $1.1 trillion, a figure that undercuts earlier market expectations. The report does not confirm the final pricing but highlights the volatility of AI‑focused listings.

## Developer tooling and cultural reflections

Devvm.dev positions itself as a platform that provides a dedicated virtual machine for each software project. The site emphasizes isolation and reproducibility, targeting developers who need consistent environments across diverse codebases.

Filfre published an article titled *Deus Ex*, reflecting on the legacy of the eponymous video‑game series. The piece explores the narrative themes of transhumanism and corporate power but does not link directly to current industry events.

## What to watch

Track the rollout of Samsung’s SmartTag 3 as it expands into retail channels beyond the online store. Monitor sodium‑ion supply contracts for additional capacity announcements that could tip pricing further. Follow the congressional probes into Meta, Apple and SpaceXAI for any subpoenas or enforcement actions. Keep an eye on Anthropic’s IPO pricing and the market’s response to AI‑centric valuations. Finally, watch for follow‑up disclosures from the Firecracker VM escape researchers, which may influence cloud‑provider security policies.
