## Ubisoft pulls the plug on its NFT title

Champions Tactics: Grimoria Chronicles vanished from stores this week, ending Ubisoft’s brief foray into blockchain‑backed games. The shutdown came just two years after the broader NFT hype faded, according to Engadget.

The game launched as a free‑to‑play tactical RPG that let players earn tokenized items. Ubisoft announced the closure without a public postmortem, leaving the few remaining players with inaccessible assets. The move mirrors the fate of other NFT experiments that never gained traction beyond speculative collectors.

Industry observers note that Ubisoft’s NFT push followed a 2021 wave of high‑profile projects, from Axie Infinity to Bored Ape Yacht Club. Those ventures attracted headline‑grabbing funding but struggled to retain active users once the speculative surge cooled. Ubisoft’s retreat signals a broader retreat among major publishers, many of which have quietly shelved blockchain features after initial backlash.

## Sony doubles down on disc‑free PS5 future

Sony confirmed it will continue phasing out physical discs for PS5 games, despite a vocal fan backlash that surfaced during its latest earnings call. Engadget reported the company’s executives defended the decision as a long‑term cost and logistics win.

The plan means new releases will ship exclusively as digital downloads, while existing disc‑based titles remain playable on current hardware. Sony’s statement emphasized that the shift aligns with “consumer preferences for instant access” and reduces manufacturing overhead.

Critics argue the move marginalizes users with limited broadband or those who rely on resale markets. The backlash echoes earlier console transitions, such as Nintendo’s 2017 switch to digital‑only titles on the Switch Lite, which sparked similar debates about ownership and accessibility.

## Microsoft brings Xbox to Hisense TVs

Microsoft began rolling out the Xbox app to Hisense televisions running the VIDAA operating system, allowing users to stream Xbox games without a console. Engadget highlighted the rollout as part of a broader push to embed cloud gaming into smart‑TV ecosystems.

The integration leverages Xbox Cloud Gaming (formerly Project xCloud) and requires only a compatible controller and a Microsoft account. Users can launch titles directly from the TV’s home screen, turning a living‑room set‑up into a plug‑and‑play gaming hub.

Analysts see the move as a direct challenge to Sony’s disc‑centric strategy, positioning Microsoft as the champion of device‑agnostic gaming. By partnering with Hisense, a major global TV manufacturer, Microsoft expands its reach into markets where console adoption is lower but broadband penetration is rising.

## Pathogenic proves roguelite twin‑stick shooters still have bite

Ars Technica praised the new roguelite twin‑stick shooter Pathogenic, declaring it “lives up to the hype.” The title blends fast‑paced action with procedurally generated levels, delivering a fresh take on a genre that has seen steady, if niche, growth.

Pathogenic’s developers focused on tight controls and a high skill ceiling, rewarding players who master enemy patterns and weapon synergies. The game’s art direction leans into gritty, cyber‑punk aesthetics, setting it apart from more cartoonish contemporaries.

While the market for twin‑stick shooters remains modest compared to AAA releases, Pathogenic’s success demonstrates that well‑executed indie titles can capture attention without relying on big‑budget marketing. The game’s reception may encourage other studios to revisit the formula with modern twists.

## What this means for the future of game distribution

The three moves illustrate a clear industry drift toward digital‑first, cloud‑enabled experiences. Ubisoft’s retreat from NFTs suggests publishers are recalibrating after a speculative bubble, focusing instead on proven revenue models.

Sony’s disc‑phase‑out, despite fan pushback, signals that console makers see digital as the default delivery method, especially as broadband speeds improve globally. Microsoft’s TV integration underscores the belief that hardware boundaries are eroding; the console becomes a service rather than a box.

What to watch: Ubisoft’s next title will reveal whether the company fully abandons blockchain or re‑enters with a more restrained approach. Sony’s upcoming fiscal report should show whether the disc‑free strategy impacts hardware sales. Microsoft’s partnership pipeline with other TV brands will indicate how quickly cloud gaming can replace traditional consoles in living rooms.

Stay tuned for the next earnings calls, platform updates, and indie releases that will test whether digital dominance holds or if a hybrid model resurfaces.
