## Gemini on Mac moves from chat to desktop automation
Google rolled out Gemini Spark to the macOS app, letting the assistant act on files and programs that sit on a user’s computer. The beta is limited to Google AI Ultra subscribers aged 18 and over in the United States and can be downloaded at **gemini.google/mac**. Users can ask Spark to sort PDFs in the Downloads folder, generate a budget spreadsheet from recent invoices, or schedule a recurring update for that sheet. The assistant only touches files that the user explicitly grants it permission to use.

The feature pushes Gemini beyond a pure chat window. It bridges the gap between cloud‑based AI and on‑device productivity tools, a move that feels more like a personal automation script than a conversational chatbot. Early adopters report that the workflow feels natural: a single prompt replaces a series of manual clicks.

## New Gemini Spark capabilities expand across apps and browsers
Gemini Spark now talks to a broader set of services. Integrated Google Tasks and Keep let Spark turn scattered notes into actionable items. Partnerships with Canva, Dropbox, Instacart, OpenTable and Zillow Rentals add design, file sharing, grocery ordering, restaurant reservations, and rental scheduling to the assistant’s toolbox. These connections roll out on the web and mobile first, with macOS support slated for the coming weeks.

A separate update embeds Spark directly into Chrome. With user permission, Spark can pull saved passwords and logged‑in accounts to complete web errands such as booking a flight or scheduling an apartment viewing. The rollout starts in the United States and will expand to more than 160 additional countries for Google AI Pro subscribers. The integration is designed to guard against prompt‑injection attacks and hands control back to the user for any payment step.

## Mobile Gemini upgrades: Live, 2.0 Flash, and deeper integration
On Android, Gemini Live now accepts images, files and YouTube videos in the conversation. The feature debuted on Samsung Galaxy S24, S25 and Pixel 9 devices, with a broader Android rollout promised in the weeks ahead. Project Astra will add screen sharing and live video streaming to the mobile app, starting with the Galaxy S25.

The underlying model also received a boost. Gemini 2.0 Flash delivers faster answers and stronger benchmark performance. Advanced users keep a 1 million‑token context window, enough for roughly 1 500 pages of uploaded files, plus priority access to Deep Research and Gems. Image generation switched to the latest Imagen 3, which renders richer textures and follows prompts with higher fidelity. Both the 2.0 Flash model and Imagen 3 are rolling out to the Gemini web and mobile apps, including enterprise accounts.

## Privacy and data handling concerns
Google’s updated privacy notice spells out how data from Gemini interactions is treated. Human reviewers—including contractors—may see some of the content to improve the service, so users are warned not to share confidential material. When Gemini Spark accesses a connected app, that app’s own privacy policy governs the data it stores.

Unintended activations are also covered. If a background noise sounds like “Hey Google,” the assistant will treat the input as a normal activation and may use the data to refine its models. Users can manage their data through the Google Account privacy hub, export their information, or request removal under applicable laws. The notice emphasizes that changing Gemini settings does not alter broader Google data‑collection settings such as Search History or Web & App Activity.

## What to watch
The next few months will reveal whether desktop automation gains traction beyond power users. Watch for the expansion of macOS Spark beyond the US beta, the rollout of Chrome integration to additional regions, and any regulatory scrutiny of the human‑reviewer pipeline. The interplay between convenience and data privacy will shape how quickly enterprises adopt Gemini as a core productivity layer.
