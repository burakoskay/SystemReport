## New Camera Tricks on the iPhone 18 Pro

Apple's latest iPhone 18 Pro is set to launch in a few weeks, with a trio of camera upgrades already making the rounds in tech circles. The 9to5Mac report that broke the story lists the upgrades as distinct features, though it does not name them individually. Apple is sticking with its incremental hardware gains strategy, rather than a wholesale redesign.

The iPhone's camera lineage has followed a clear pattern: larger sensors, faster lenses, and computational photography tricks introduced with each generation. The iPhone 14 Pro added a 48-megapixel sensor, and the 15 Pro refined low-light performance with a larger aperture. If the rumored trio of upgrades follows that pattern, the 18 Pro will likely push pixel-size and stabilization further, keeping the device competitive against Android flagships that already sport 200-plus-megapixel sensors. Apple's approach remains to pair modest sensor growth with software that squeezes out extra detail, a formula that has paid off in market share.

## iPhone Ultra Emerges as the Foldable Codename

Mark Gurman, Apple's long-standing leaker, told 9to5Mac that the internal moniker for Apple's upcoming foldable phone is iPhone Ultra. The outlet reported that the name is already in common use across Apple's engineering teams. No official product has been announced, but the codename signals a clear intent: Apple wants a premium label that sits above the current Pro line.

Apple's foldable ambitions are not new. Patents filed over the past five years show a persistent interest in hinge mechanisms and flexible displays. Competitors such as Samsung and Huawei have already shipped foldables that command premium pricing, but they have struggled to achieve mainstream adoption. By branding its effort as iPhone Ultra, Apple may be positioning the device as a niche, high-margin product rather than a mass-market handset. The name also aligns with the Watch Ultra, a model that emphasizes ruggedness and extended battery life, hinting that durability could be a selling point for the foldable.

## Solid-State Buttons on the iPhone 15 Pro

Reports point to a buttonless iPhone 15 Pro that replaces the traditional volume rocker and power switch with solid-state 'taptic' buttons. Renderings published by 9to5Mac show a sleek side panel devoid of moving parts. The change would mark the first time Apple eliminates all mechanical buttons on an iPhone.

A Hacker News post titled 'Apple, please don't take all of the buttons away' calls the move 'very bad and no good.' The author argues that haptic simulations, while impressive, cannot match the tactile feedback of a physical button. 'Physical buttons do not suffer this problem because they just work every time you press them,' the post asserts. The same writer points out that gloves and damp hands, common in real-world use, still benefit from a mechanical click that a haptic surface struggles to emulate.

Apple has experimented with haptic feedback before. The Force Touch trackpad on the MacBook and the Apple Watch Ultra's physical button demonstrate that the company can blend haptics with mechanical actuation. However, the watch's button remains a metal click, not a pure solid-state surface. The iPhone 15 Pro proposal would be a step toward a fully sealed chassis, potentially improving water resistance and simplifying internal layout, but at the cost of a user-experience element that many longtime iPhone users consider essential.

## Market and Regulatory Implications

If Apple ships a buttonless iPhone 15 Pro, it will join a broader industry trend toward sealed devices. The removal of moving parts can simplify the waterproofing process, potentially allowing Apple to meet stricter ingress protection (IP) ratings without additional gaskets. Regulators in the EU are also tightening requirements for repairability and modularity; a fully sealed phone could complicate compliance, though Apple has already faced scrutiny over its right-to-repair policies.

From a competitive standpoint, the move could differentiate Apple from Android manufacturers that still rely on physical volume and power keys. Samsung, for instance, continues to use mechanical buttons across its Galaxy S line, citing user preference. If Apple's haptic buttons prove reliable, the company may set a new standard that forces rivals to adopt similar technology or double down on traditional ergonomics.

## Conclusion

The next few weeks will reveal whether the iPhone 18 Pro's camera upgrades materialize in a launch event, and whether Apple will confirm the iPhone Ultra codename in a formal announcement. Equally important is the hardware revision of the iPhone 15 Pro; if Apple ships a model with solid-state buttons, early user reviews will test the claim that haptic feedback can replace a mechanical click. Track the first-hand impressions of the buttonless device, the EU's response to a sealed chassis, and any supply-chain signals—such as orders for new hinge components—that hint at a foldable's imminent arrival. Those data points will indicate whether Apple's gamble on cameras, foldables, and buttonless design will reshape its flagship strategy.
