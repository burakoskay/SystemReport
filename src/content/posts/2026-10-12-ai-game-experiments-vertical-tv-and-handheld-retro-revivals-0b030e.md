## AI Playground Experiments

Google’s AI Playground produced a batch of AI‑generated games that Wired labeled "terrible" in a piece titled *I Made Terrible Games With Google’s AI Playground*.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] The article describes a "long day’s haul in the video game slop mines" as the author churned out dozens of titles using the public model. Wired notes the experiment was less a commercial launch and more a proof‑of‑concept for how quickly a language model can output playable code.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]

The piece does not claim the games are market‑ready; instead it treats the output as a stress test for the platform’s creative limits. By publishing the source files, the author gave other developers a raw dataset to dissect, exposing both the promise and the pitfalls of AI‑driven game design. The experiment underscores a growing willingness among indie creators to weaponize large language models for rapid prototyping, even when the result is deliberately low‑quality.

## Vertical Video Moves to the Living Room

The Verge reported that startup Vurt wants viewers to treat vertical videos as a distinct genre, not just a mobile gimmick.[^6][^7][^8] Unlike most vertical‑focused services that optimize for phone screens, Vurt builds its player for widescreen televisions, arguing that the format deserves a "wide‑screen" experience.[^6][^7][^8]

Vurt’s approach flips the prevailing narrative that vertical content belongs only on TikTok‑style platforms. By targeting the living‑room TV, Vurt forces established streaming giants—Disney Plus, HBO Max, and others—to reconsider how they allocate screen real estate. The Verge notes that these companies see short‑form vertical video as an "existential threat" and are scrambling to embed it in their apps, but Vurt is the first to design a service around the format from the ground up.[^6][^7][^8]

## Streaming Prices and the Vertical Arms Race

WIRED listed a suite of Disney Plus discount codes offering 52% off for the month of October 2026. The promotion appears alongside verified student offers and partner bundles, indicating that Disney Plus is actively using deep price cuts to retain subscribers amid fierce competition.

The timing of the discount aligns with the vertical video push described by The Verge. As streaming services pour resources into new formats, they also lean on pricing tactics to keep churn low. WIRED’s discount roundup suggests that price elasticity remains a key lever, especially when platforms gamble on unproven vertical‑first experiences that may not yet attract a broad audience.

## Handheld Form‑Factor Innovation

Engadget detailed Anbernic’s upcoming RG DDS dual‑screen handheld, noting that the main display adopts a 5:3 aspect ratio while the lower screen uses a 4:3 ratio. The combination mirrors the Nintendo 3DS’s native dimensions, making the device a natural fit for legacy 3DS titles.

Anbernic’s design choice reflects a broader retro‑gaming resurgence, where manufacturers prioritize compatibility over novel screen shapes. By matching the aspect ratios of classic handhelds, the RG DDS promises a pixel‑perfect experience without the scaling artifacts that plague many emulation devices. Engadget’s coverage emphasizes that the hardware’s form factor could set a new baseline for future dual‑screen releases.

## Repairability in Emerging Consoles

Engadget’s teardown of the Steam Frame revealed a single, stubborn obstacle: the cooling fan sits deep within the chassis and is hard to reach. While the rest of the console scores well on modularity, the fan’s placement complicates field repairs and could increase service costs.

The difficulty mirrors a larger industry trend where sleek designs often sacrifice ease of maintenance. As consumers demand longer product lifespans, manufacturers that ignore repair pathways risk regulatory scrutiny and brand backlash. Engadget’s findings highlight that even a company known for PC‑centric openness can stumble on basic serviceability.

## What to Watch

Vurt plans a public launch of its TV‑first vertical player later this year.[^8] Tracking subscriber uptake and ad revenue will reveal whether the format can survive beyond the mobile niche. Simultaneously, Google’s AI Playground continues to evolve; any official update that lowers the barrier to high‑quality game generation could shift the indie development landscape dramatically.

On the hardware front, Anbernic’s RG DDS is slated for release in early 2027, and early reviewer impressions will test whether the dual‑screen aspect ratios truly deliver a superior retro experience. Keep an eye on Steam’s next hardware iteration for any redesign of the cooling system, as repair‑friendly engineering may become a competitive differentiator. Finally, monitor streaming‑service discount cycles—WIRED’s October 2026 Disney Plus code may be a bellwether for how aggressively platforms will price‑cut to fund experimental formats.
