Apple signaled a new revenue lever by courting third‑party AI applications during its latest earnings call. The company described the move as an "opportunity" for developers to sell services through its ecosystem, hinting that a cut of those sales could offset slowing hardware margins.

The announcement came on Monday night when Apple executives fielded analyst questions. They did not disclose a timeline or revenue target, but the phrasing suggested a shift from pure device sales toward platform‑level monetisation. Analysts noted that Apple’s App Store already extracts a 15‑30% fee from AI‑related subscriptions, but the call implied a broader, perhaps tiered, structure for third‑party models.

## iPhone Duo supply strain persists

Apple confirmed that production of the iPhone Duo is ramping, yet the company cannot forecast when output will meet demand. The firm warned that shipping delays will likely spill into the next calendar year.

The statement arrived alongside a broader discussion of component shortages that have plagued the iPhone line since the previous cycle. Apple did not provide unit counts, but the admission of a multi‑quarter lag underscores a bottleneck that could pressure premium pricing.

Industry observers point to the Duo’s dual‑camera module and custom silicon as choke points. Both require high‑precision assembly and access to advanced lithography, resources that remain stretched after the pandemic‑induced surge in demand for high‑end smartphones.

## Memory‑chip cost surge inflates inventory

Apple disclosed that its inventory rose 154% to $14.5 billion, driven in large part by unprecedented memory‑chip costs. The company warned that those costs are expected to continue climbing.

The surge reflects a market where DRAM and NAND suppliers have tightened supply while demand from data‑center operators and AI workloads remains robust. Apple’s balance sheet now carries a larger stock of devices that may need price adjustments to preserve margins.

Analysts caution that the inventory buildup could force Apple to discount upcoming models or delay refresh cycles. The company’s historical practice of absorbing component price hikes through incremental price hikes may be less viable when inventory levels already strain cash flow.

## German lawmakers receive spyware warnings

Apple alerted a group of German lawmakers and a documentary filmmaker that their iPhones may have been compromised by spyware. The notification arrived via Apple’s security dashboard, which flags devices exhibiting anomalous behaviour.

The warning did not name the spyware, but German officials have previously linked similar alerts to state‑level surveillance tools. Apple’s involvement suggests the firm is actively scanning for known malicious signatures across its device fleet.

The incident raises questions about the broader security posture of iOS devices in high‑risk environments. If the spyware originated from a foreign actor, the episode could prompt tighter scrutiny of Apple’s threat‑intelligence sharing with European regulators.

## What to watch

Track Apple’s rollout of third‑party AI monetisation models in the App Store; the first revenue reports are likely to appear in the next quarterly filing. Monitor iPhone Duo shipment data from supply‑chain analysts for signs of a supply‑chain correction. Watch quarterly inventory disclosures for any shift in the $14.5 billion figure, which will signal whether memory‑chip pricing pressure is easing. Finally, follow any formal statements from German authorities on the spyware case, as they may trigger policy changes that affect iOS security updates worldwide.
