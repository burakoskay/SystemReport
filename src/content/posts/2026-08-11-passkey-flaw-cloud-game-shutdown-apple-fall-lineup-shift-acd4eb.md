A Pass‑ta‑key attack has exposed inconsistencies in Windows passkey handling, while Cold Iron Studios pulled a $60 cloud‑based game without refunds, and Apple announced a fall thriller and new puzzle titles for its subscription services. The three moves highlight how security, ownership and content strategy intersect in today’s platform economy.

## Pass‑ta‑key Attack Undermines Windows Passkey Flow

Security researchers released a proof‑of‑concept called Pass‑ta‑key that demonstrates how passkey apps on Windows process authentication data differently from their macOS and Linux counterparts. The attack extracts credential material by exploiting a timing side channel in the Windows Credential Provider API. The researchers note that the same passkey flow on other operating systems does not expose the same vector, suggesting an OS‑specific implementation flaw.

The disclosure forces developers to audit their Windows integration paths. Companies that ship passkey‑enabled mobile or desktop apps must now verify that their Windows code paths do not leak timing information. The broader security community warns that the attack could be weaponized in targeted phishing campaigns, especially against enterprise users who rely on passkeys as a password replacement. Microsoft has not yet issued a patch, and the research paper recommends immediate mitigation through constant‑time handling and stricter sandboxing.

## Cold Iron Studios Pulls Cloud Version of $60 Game, No Refunds

Cold Iron Studios announced the shutdown of the cloud streaming tier for its flagship title, a game priced at $60 on major storefronts. The company disabled access for all cloud subscribers on Friday and offered no monetary compensation. Existing owners can still run the game locally, but the cloud component – marketed as a seamless way to play on low‑end hardware – is permanently offline.

The decision resurfaced a long‑standing debate about digital ownership. Players who purchased the cloud access expected a perpetual service, yet the terms of service allowed unilateral termination. Industry observers compare the move to previous cloud‑only failures, noting that the lack of refunds erodes consumer trust in subscription‑style game delivery. Legal analysts point out that, without clear statutory protection, studios can retreat from cloud promises with minimal liability, leaving buyers with a fragmented experience.

## Apple TV Unveils "Last Seen" as Fall Thriller Anchor

Apple TV released the first trailer for "Last Seen," a crime thriller slated to launch at the start of its fall lineup next month. The two‑minute spot shows a gritty urban chase and hints at a non‑linear narrative structure. Apple positions the series as the flagship original to draw subscribers back after a lull in high‑profile releases.

The timing aligns with the broader streaming arms race, where major players drop flagship titles in September to capture post‑summer viewership. Competitors such as Netflix and HBO Max have already announced their own crime dramas for the same window. Analysts suggest that Apple TV’s reliance on original scripted content marks a shift from its earlier focus on documentary and sports programming, signaling a deeper investment in narrative series.

## Apple Arcade Adds Two Beloved Puzzlers in September

Apple Arcade announced that two classic puzzle games will join the ad‑free catalog in September, following the August launch of Madden NFL 27. The titles, long‑standing favorites among indie fans, will be available to all Arcade subscribers at no extra cost. Apple highlighted the move as a way to diversify the platform’s genre mix ahead of the holiday season.

The addition arrives as puzzle games experience a resurgence on mobile devices, driven by casual players seeking low‑commitment experiences. By bundling well‑known titles, Apple Arcade aims to retain existing subscribers while attracting new ones who value a curated library over free‑to‑play alternatives. The strategy also counters recent criticism that the service’s lineup leans heavily toward action‑oriented titles, offering a more balanced portfolio.

## What to Watch

Watch how Microsoft responds to the Pass‑ta‑key disclosure; a patch or mitigation plan will set a benchmark for OS‑level passkey security. Track Cold Iron Studios’ next move—whether the studio will offer a refund window or shift to a purely local‑only model could influence future cloud‑gaming contracts. Monitor Apple TV’s subscriber metrics after "Last Seen" premieres; early viewership will indicate whether the thriller can anchor the fall slate. Finally, keep an eye on Apple Arcade’s September launch numbers; strong uptake of the new puzzles may prompt the platform to double down on classic indie acquisitions for the next quarter.
