## Power banks get a reality check

Travelers can now keep phones, laptops, and handheld gaming consoles alive with a new batch of WIRED‑tested power banks. WIRED’s "Best Power Banks (2026): My Picks After Testing Over 100" lists travel‑friendly models that survived a marathon of charge‑cycle tests.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]

The guide notes that the top picks balance size, output, and price. Reviewers praised units that fit in a coat pocket yet delivered enough wattage for a 15‑hour laptop session. The article does not claim any model is a miracle; it simply flags the ones that survived real‑world use.

The broader market is saturated. Hundreds of manufacturers push specs that sound impressive on paper but falter when you need a steady charge on a train. WIRED’s hands‑on approach cuts through the hype by confirming that a few models actually deliver the advertised capacity.

For budget‑conscious buyers, the guide suggests looking for a 20,000 mAh capacity at under $50. Anything higher in price should earn a clear performance edge, which most models fail to show.

## Vacuum cleaners get a discount sprint

WIRED’s "Our Top Tested Picks From Tineco’s Black Friday Sale (2026)" showcases vacuum cleaners, carpet cleaners, and steam mops slashed by up to 60 percent. The article lists the exact discount percentages for each product, letting shoppers compare savings side‑by‑side.

The featured Tineco units include a cordless stick vacuum that clears pet hair in a single pass. Testers measured suction consistency across hardwood and carpet, confirming the claim that the machine holds power for a full 45‑minute run.

Other highlighted models pair a steam mop with a detachable handheld vacuum. The combination promises a two‑in‑one clean for hard‑floor apartments, a useful claim for renters who cannot store multiple appliances.

The 60 percent markdown is striking, but the article warns that deep‑discount items often arrive in plain packaging without accessories. Buyers should verify that the included charger and filter meet their needs before checking out.

## Wearables and wireless plans join the price war

Apple’s AirPods lineup faces steep competition as holiday shoppers compare three models. 9to5Mac’s "AirPods 5 vs AirPods Pro 3" breaks down pricing: AirPods Pro 3 sell for $179 (regular $249), AirPods Max 2 for $419 (regular $549), and AirPods 5 start at $129.

The price drop on the Pro 3 makes the feature‑rich, active‑noise‑cancelling earbuds more accessible. Reviewers noted that the Pro 3 still delivers the same adaptive EQ and spatial audio that justified the original premium price.

Meanwhile, Total Wireless is offering 50 percent off select plans for the same period. The promo applies to both unlimited data and family bundles, effectively halving the monthly cost for new subscribers.

Mountain Hardwear adds a 15 percent coupon for November purchases of high‑performance mountaineering apparel. The discount applies to jackets and insulated pants that normally carry a premium price tag.

These overlapping promotions create a crowded field where consumers must juggle multiple savings codes. The net effect is a lower entry price for premium tech, but the trade‑off is a more complex checkout experience.

## Why the holiday discount frenzy matters

Discounts of this magnitude signal that manufacturers are clearing inventory ahead of next‑generation releases. When a brand like Apple trims the Pro 3 price, it often hints at an upcoming iteration that will render the current model less desirable.

Similarly, Tineco’s 60 percent Black Friday cuts suggest a pipeline of newer suction technologies slated for 2027. Early adopters who wait for the next wave may miss out on the current savings, while bargain hunters secure a functional device at a fraction of the original cost.

The pattern extends to wireless carriers. A 50 percent plan discount can attract customers who might otherwise stay with legacy providers. However, the reduced price may come with limited data speeds or a shorter contract term, details that the promo page does not always highlight.

For outdoor enthusiasts, the 15 percent Mountain Hardwear coupon offers a rare chance to buy high‑altitude gear without the usual premium. The discount does not change the material quality, but it does lower the financial barrier for a season that often requires multiple layers.

## What to watch next

Track the launch dates of Apple’s next AirPods generation; a price dip now could precede a new release in early 2027. Monitor Tineco’s product announcements for a possible 2027 suction‑technology update that may further devalue current models. Keep an eye on Total Wireless’s contract terms for the 50 percent plan discount, as any change in data caps could affect long‑term cost. Finally, watch for WIRED’s follow‑up testing round later this year, which will reveal whether the 2026 power‑bank picks still hold up after a year of use.
