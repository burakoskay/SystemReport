## Mobile Universe: No Man's Sky on iPhone and iPad

The open‑world space sandbox finally lands on iOS. Players with an iPhone 11 or later can now download a version of *No Man's Sky* that promises the same procedural galaxy in a pocket‑sized form factor.

The move follows a decade‑long redemption arc that saw the title evolve from a criticized launch to a beloved service‑based experience. Bringing it to Apple’s mobile platform expands the audience beyond console and PC owners. It also forces the developers to compress a 40‑hour exploration loop into touch controls, a technical challenge that could set a precedent for other large‑scale games on handheld devices.

Industry analysts note that the iOS release arrives while Apple continues to tighten its App Store policies around subscription‑based games. If the port succeeds, it may encourage other studios to treat mobile as a first‑class platform rather than a secondary afterthought.

## PC Resurrection: P.T. Gets an Official Port

Kojima Productions’ infamous “playable trailer” for *Silent Hills* finally sees a legitimate PC release. The horror cult classic, long trapped on aging PS4 hard drives, is now available for download without the need for console workarounds.

The port restores the original 720p visuals and the unsettling looping hallway that terrified a generation of streamers. By delivering the experience on a standard PC installer, the release sidesteps the legal gray area that kept the demo offline for years.

Fans have been clamoring for a legal way to replay the demo since its removal from the PlayStation Store. The PC version satisfies that demand and reopens the conversation about preserving interactive media that never saw a full commercial launch.

## The Polygon Fallout

Vox Media sold Polygon to Valnet, and the transaction triggered a wave of layoffs that ripped through the outlet’s veteran staff. Co‑founder Brian Crecente announced on Bluesky that he was “just completely sickened by this news,” echoing the shock felt by colleagues.

The cuts included editor‑in‑chief Chris Plante, senior writer Michael McWhertor, and a host of senior reporters. Nicole Carpenter posted a story about the Epic vs. Apple case at 10 a.m. Eastern, only to share her own layoff two hours later. The abruptness suggests the sale was not communicated internally before the public announcement.

Valnet, a content aggregator that runs dozens of sites with a combined 260 million page views, has a reputation for low‑pay, high‑volume article production. The company recently sued The Wrap over claims of poor working conditions, insisting its model “relies on…high‑quality journalism.” Whether Polygon’s editorial standards will survive under that model remains uncertain.

The loss of Polygon’s core team removes a major voice that has consistently broken stories for both indie and AAA developers. Readers now face a gap in deep‑dive coverage, and advertisers lose a proven channel for reaching a technically savvy audience.

## AI Agents Gaming Hacker News

An autonomous LLM‑powered agent named Vertigo posted six links to Hacker News in a single day, and four of those submissions cracked the front page. The account was only a few days old, yet its rapid ascent sparked speculation about coordinated promotion.

Vertigo runs on a Raspberry Pi with shell access, a dedicated Gmail address, and a subdomain (vertigo.kuber.studio). The hardware gives it a home‑network IP, which the creator says helps the bot browse X and Reddit without relying on a datacenter IP. The bot’s prompt grants it “complete autonomy, time, tools, and very little direction,” allowing it to decide what to read, write, and submit.

During the test, the agent generated a personal blog, adopted a profile picture, and even embedded an Easter‑egg game that plays a reskinned version of the creator’s Backdooms project. Its most successful post—an article about GPT Images 2.5—earned over 200 up‑votes before the owner realized it had gone viral.

The episode highlights a new attack surface for community‑driven news aggregators. If an LLM can autonomously identify high‑traffic topics and submit links that satisfy the site’s quality filters, moderators may need to rethink how they surface content and verify submitter intent.

## What to Watch

The iOS *No Man’s Sky* launch will reveal whether massive procedural worlds can thrive on mobile hardware. The PC *P.T.* port will test how legacy demos survive when officially released. Polygon’s editorial vacuum will force readers to seek alternative deep‑dive sources, while Valnet’s handling of the site will become a case study in scaling niche journalism. Finally, the Vertigo experiment suggests that AI‑driven submission bots could start shaping front‑page news on Hacker News and similar platforms. Track the next round of submissions from Vertigo’s subdomain and watch for any policy changes from the Hacker News moderators.
