Roborock just sliced $1,650 off its top‑of‑the‑line robot vacuum, making the Black Friday bargain the deepest the brand has offered in years.[^1][^2]

9to5Google reported that the company is also discounting select models by up to 50 %. The price shock lands alongside a $70 Nest Wifi Pro, a 65 % cut that puts a Wi‑Fi 6E router within reach of most households. At the same time, Paramount+ and Apple TV+ have dipped to $2.99 and $6.99 respectively, while XREAL’s smart‑display glasses touch $249. Even the HP Googlebook 14 sees a $300 markdown, and Twelve South’s Airfly Drive dongle slides to $31.99. The spread of discounts paints a picture of a market desperate to move inventory before the holiday lull.

## Robot vacuums get a price shock

Roborock’s flagship model, long positioned as a premium cleaning robot, now carries a price tag that undercuts its own previous generations. The $1,650 reduction translates to roughly a 45 % discount, according to the same 9to5Google coverage. For a device that normally commands a six‑figure price in some currencies, the cut feels like a clearance move rather than a promotional tweak.

The timing aligns with a broader slowdown in home‑automation sales. Analysts have noted that after a pandemic‑driven surge, demand for robot vacuums is flattening. By slashing prices, Roborock hopes to capture budget‑conscious buyers who might otherwise wait for a newer model. The gamble could backfire if consumers perceive the discount as a sign of outdated hardware, but the immediate cash flow boost may outweigh that risk.

## Home networking finally becomes affordable

Google’s Nest Wifi Pro now retails for $70, a 65 % discount that makes Wi‑Fi 6E accessible to renters and first‑time smart‑home owners. The router supports the 6 GHz band, a feature that many older devices cannot use, yet the price point removes a major barrier to adoption.

The price drop also pressures competitors like Netgear and Asus, whose entry‑level Wi‑Fi 6E units still sit above $150. If the discount drives a wave of upgrades, we could see a measurable lift in network‑speed benchmarks across the consumer segment. However, the lower price may also encourage a surge in device churn, as users replace older routers that are still functional.

## Streaming subscriptions hit bargain basement

Paramount+ has been reduced to $2.99 per month, while Apple TV+ now costs $6.99, according to 9to5Google’s Black Friday roundup. Both offers are limited‑time and require a new subscription to qualify.[^5][^7]

These numbers are striking against a backdrop of rising subscription fatigue. Industry reports show the average American now pays for over six streaming services. By offering sub‑$3 entry points, providers hope to lock in users before they churn to ad‑supported tiers. The strategy is a short‑term revenue grab; long‑term retention will depend on content pipelines that can justify higher price points after the promotional window closes.

## Wearables and laptops get a seasonal trim

XREAL’s early Black Friday sale lists its smart‑display glasses at $249, the lowest price the brand has ever posted.[^6] The glasses overlay digital content onto a transparent display, targeting developers and early adopters who experiment with mixed reality.

Meanwhile, the HP Googlebook 14 receives a $300 discount through Best Buy, marking the first price cut since its October 4 launch. The laptop, marketed as a lightweight productivity machine, now sits in a price bracket that competes directly with the MacBook Air’s entry model. The discount could shift buying decisions for students and remote workers who weigh performance against cost.

## CarPlay dongles and the accessory ripple effect

Twelve South’s Airfly Drive, a dongle that converts wired CarPlay to wireless, is now 20 % off, dropping from $39.99 to $31.99 on Amazon. The device fills a niche for drivers who want a clutter‑free dashboard without replacing their factory head unit.

Accessory discounts like this illustrate how Black Friday extends beyond headline products. Small‑ticket items often act as loss leaders, nudging consumers toward larger ecosystem purchases. If the Airfly Drive gains traction, we might see more OEMs integrate wireless CarPlay as a standard feature rather than an optional upgrade.

## What to watch

Track how quickly the Nest Wifi Pro and Roborock models sell out; stock levels will signal whether price cuts are enough to revive stalled categories. Keep an eye on subscription renewal rates for Paramount+ and Apple TV+ after the promotional period ends, as they will reveal whether low‑price hooks translate into lasting subscriber bases. Finally, monitor whether accessory discounts like the Airfly Drive prompt manufacturers to bake wireless capabilities into future car infotainment systems.
