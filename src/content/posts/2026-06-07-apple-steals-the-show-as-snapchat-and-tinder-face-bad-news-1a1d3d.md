## The Distraction

Apple's annual developer event, WWDC, has once again drawn attention away from other tech companies' negative news. On the same day as Apple's big announcement, Snapchat and Tinder found themselves dealing with unwanted press.

Snapchat settled an embarrassing legal battle with an ousted cofounder, Reggie Brown, who had claimed he was pushed out of the company and stripped of his rightful share of ownership. The terms of the settlement were not disclosed, but Snapchat acknowledged Brown's contribution to the creation of the app.

## The News That Slipped Under the Radar

Meanwhile, Tinder apparently resolved a lawsuit with its former vice president of marketing, Whitney Wolfe, who had claimed that the company's chief marketing officer, Justin Mateen, made several inappropriate comments about her. Mateen has since left the company.

These announcements came on the same day that Apple was expected to unveil its latest products and features. The company's event was a major draw, with many tech enthusiasts and journalists tuning in to see what Apple had in store.

## A Look Back at Apple's History of Stealing the Show

This is not the first time Apple has overshadowed other tech companies' news. In the past, the company has used its events to distract from negative press about other companies. For example, in 2013, Apple's iPhone 5 announcement took attention away from BlackBerry's latest struggles.

## The Tech Industry's Response

The tech industry is no stranger to companies using strategic timing to their advantage. When a major player like Apple announces new products or features, it can be difficult for other companies to get their news noticed. This can lead to a phenomenon known as "newsjacking," where companies try to capitalize on the attention surrounding a major event.

## The Broader Industry Context

The mobile app industry, where Snapchat and Tinder operate, has seen significant growth in recent years. According to a report by Statista, the global mobile app market size was valued at around $189 billion in 2020 and is expected to reach $407 billion by 2026. However, the industry is also highly competitive, with many companies vying for attention and market share.
