## Skydance merger locks in CNN leadership

Mark Thompson will stay on as head of CNN when Paramount finalizes its $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery this week. The deal folds the two media giants into a single entity named Skydance, with Paramount CEO David Ellison and former Mattel chief Ynon Kreiz sharing the top spot.

Thompson’s internal memo promises that the combined firm will "fully support the principle and practice of the kind of independent news that CNN has always stood for," a pledge that aims to quiet lingering doubts about editorial autonomy. The announcement also listed a broader slate of senior executives, but the headline remains the preservation of CNN’s newsroom leadership amid the biggest media consolidation in a decade.

## Youngest exoplanet challenges formation models

Astronomers have confirmed that the planet Elias 2‑24b is less than one million years old, making it the youngest exoplanet ever observed. The world‑size body orbits a star in the Orion Nebula and its extreme youth forces scientists to rethink how quickly planets can coalesce from protoplanetary disks.

The discovery, reported by WIRED, suggests that core accretion may happen on timescales far shorter than the several‑million‑year windows most models assume. If the planet’s mass and orbital data hold up under further scrutiny, theory papers will need to accommodate rapid gas capture and migration, reshaping the narrative of planetary birth.

## Verifact Markets pushes prediction trading into disputed facts

A new platform called Verifact Markets is extending the prediction‑market craze into the realm of contested information. Users can place bets on whether a particular claim is true, false, or still ambiguous, turning historical debates into tradable contracts.

The service rides a wave of interest in crowd‑sourced truth‑finding, but it also raises questions about incentivizing misinformation. As the platform scales, regulators may need to decide whether disputed‑fact contracts count as financial instruments subject to oversight.

## Staples' print service speeds holiday gifting

Staples’ Print and Marketing Services now promises same‑day pickup for photo prints, novelty gifts, and business documents. In‑store specialists help customers fine‑tune layouts, ensuring that family photos or marketing flyers look exactly as intended.

The offering targets holiday shoppers who need a quick turnaround without sacrificing quality. By leveraging its nationwide footprint, Staples positions itself as a one‑stop shop for both personal and professional printing needs during the busiest shopping season.

## Russian plague researcher death triggers quarantine

Russian health officials imposed a mass quarantine after a plague researcher died under mysterious circumstances. Unconfirmed reports hint at a laboratory accident, though details remain scarce.

The swift containment measures reflect lingering fears of accidental pathogen release. International observers are watching the response for clues about Russia’s biosafety protocols and any potential spillover risk.

## Egyptian burial study revives retainer sacrifice theory

New forensic analysis of First Dynasty mass burials reveals that 39 percent of the skulls show fatal blunt‑force trauma. Researchers interpret the pattern as evidence of a “retainer sacrifice” practice, where servants were killed to accompany elites in death.

The findings, published by Ars Technica, challenge earlier views that the mass graves resulted solely from disease or conflict. If the sacrifice hypothesis gains acceptance, it will reshape our understanding of early Egyptian social hierarchy and funerary customs.
